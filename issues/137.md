## Body

`hla:c-03-03` produces a surface that claims an allele frequency for **every inhabited cell on
Earth** from 240 observations: 11,595 interpolated + 73 observed, **0 masked**. Figure committed in
#136. Its sibling at the same locus masks 17.8%.

| allele | obs | fitted range | prior upper anchor | cells masked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C*07:02 | 243 | 823 km | 1,912 km | 2,078 / 11,668 (17.8%) |
| C*03:03 | 240 | **4,189 km** | 1,919 km | **0 / 11,668** |

## The fitted range is not supported by the data

An empirical semivariogram on the logit scale, model-free, over the same observations:

| lag (km) | C*07:02 gamma (% of sill) | C*03:03 gamma (% of sill) |
|---|---|---|
| 0-250 | 31% | 39% |
| 500-1,000 | 47% | 37% |
| 1,000-1,500 | 71% | 45% |
| 1,500-2,000 | 68% | 69% |
| 2,000-3,000 | 90% | 80% |
| 3,000-4,000 | 90% | **95%** |

C*03:03 reaches its sill by roughly **3,000-4,000 km**. A Matern-5/2 field decorrelates at about
2.5-3x its range parameter, so that empirical structure implies **rho of order 1,200-1,600 km**. The
model fitted **4,189 km**, which implies decorrelation around 10,000 km. The fitted range is roughly
**2.5-3x longer than the data supports**, and it sits 2.2x above the prior's own upper anchor.

C*07:02, by contrast, reaches its sill by 2,000-3,000 km against a fitted 823 km, which is
consistent. So this is not a property of the locus or of AFND sampling; it is specific to the fit.

## This partly revises #122

#122 closed on the finding that long fitted ranges are well-identified estimates rather than a
Zhang ridge or intercept confounding, and both of those findings stand: no ridge, contraction 0.40,
no trade-off with the intercept. But I also concluded the long ranges were therefore *correct*, and
the variogram says that is wrong for at least this allele. The range is identified and too long,
which is a third possibility I did not test for.

## Likely mechanism: there is no nugget

Both variograms show **31-39% of the sill already present at 0-250 km**. That is a large nugget:
roughly a third of the variance sits at zero separation, from genotyping error, population
definition, and real heterogeneity between studies sampling "the same" place.

The model has no nugget term. In `surfaces/fit.py` the latent field is

```python
f = pm.Deterministic("f", intercept + cov(x_data, inducing_t) @ weights)
```

with no additive white-noise component, and the only thing standing in for one is the beta-binomial
`concentration` in the likelihood. That is overdispersion in the *observation* model, not a nugget
on the *field*, and it cannot absorb spatially-uncorrelated variation in the latent frequency.

Given a third of the variance at zero distance and nowhere to put it, the GP has two options: fit
through the noise with a short range, or smooth it away with a long range and let the likelihood
mop up the residuals. It appears to be choosing the second. That is the classic nugget/range
trade-off, and it predicts exactly what we see — an inflated range plus heavy over-smoothing.

Consistent with over-smoothing, both alleles retain only about a quarter of the observed variance:

| | observed sd | fitted sd at obs | variance retained | observed range | fitted range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C*07:02 | 0.0906 | 0.0478 | 27.8% | 0.000-0.712 | 0.031-0.359 |
| C*03:03 | 0.0416 | 0.0208 | 25.0% | 0.000-0.281 | 0.010-0.110 |

## Proposed test

Add an explicit nugget to the latent field and refit both alleles. The prediction is specific and
falsifiable: **C*03:03's range should fall toward the variogram-implied 1,200-1,600 km, and the
retained variance should rise.** If the range does not move, the nugget hypothesis is wrong and the
inflation is coming from somewhere else.

Worth doing before #130's skill gate, since a systematically inflated range changes which variants
pass any threshold, and before #59's mask work, since the mask's distance rule scales with the range
and is why C*03:03 masks nothing at all.

Related: #122, #59, #129, #130, #39, #111.

## What is established

The model **cannot represent what happens at short distances**, independently of what that does to
the range. Empirical against model-implied semivariogram for C*03:03:

| lag (km) | empirical | model-implied |
|---|---|---|
| 0–500 | **0.507** | **0.005** |
| 500–1,000 | 0.474 | 0.047 |
| 1,000–1,500 | 0.562 | 0.122 |

A 100x under-prediction at the shortest lag, against a sill near 1.2 — so roughly **42% of all
variance sits at zero separation** and the model has nowhere to put it. That is a real defect
whether or not fixing it shortens the range.

### bschilder, 2026-08-28T00:58:54Z (https://github.com/genomeOS/genomeOS/issues/137#issuecomment-5447047257)

**Resolved: the premise does not hold, and three candidate mechanisms have now been tested and
rejected.**

## The clean nugget test converged, and refutes the hypothesis

The earlier attempt reached r_hat 1.343 on the `nugget + binomial` arm — a failed fit, not an
answer. Re-run at **tune=2500, target_accept=0.95, draws=1000**, it converges:

| allele | no nugget (current) | nugget + binomial | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| C*03:03 | 3,396 km (r_hat 1.005, ESS 1244) | **3,166 km** (r_hat 1.005, ESS 579) | 0.93x |
| C*07:02 | 919 km (r_hat 1.005, ESS 905) | **1,179 km** (r_hat 1.010, ESS 716) | 1.28x |

C*03:03 needed to fall roughly **60%** to reach the 1,200-1,600 km this issue claimed its
variogram implies. It fell **7%**. C*07:02 moved the wrong way entirely.

So the earlier non-convergence was a sampling-budget problem, and with an adequate budget the
answer is clear: **a nugget does not shorten the range.**

## Three mechanisms tested, none sufficient

| hypothesis | test | result |
|---|---|---|
| Zhang identifiability ridge (#122) | posterior diagnostics on a "degenerate" fit | **refuted** — contraction 0.40, microergodic combination 4.5x *looser* than rho |
| Registry stratification (#141) | refuse 91% of corpus weight, refit 5 alleles | **insufficient** — ranges moved 0-20%, all converged |
| Missing nugget (this issue) | explicit nugget, clean arm, adequate budget | **refuted** — 7% where 60% was needed |

## What that leaves, honestly

The claim in this issue's title — that the variogram "does not support" a 4,189 km range — rests
on my own reading of an empirical semivariogram whose long lags were badly behaved (165%, 124%,
85%, 117% of the sill at successive bins). I converted "reaches the sill by 3,000-4,000 km" into
"rho of order 1,200-1,600 km" using a 2.5-3x rule of thumb. That conversion was the weakest step
in the argument, and it is the step three subsequent experiments have failed to corroborate.

The responsible conclusion is that **the fitted ranges are probably right and the variogram
estimate was the weaker measurement**, not that a fourth mechanism awaits discovery. Continuing to
hunt for one would be motivated by an estimate I no longer trust.

What does survive is narrower and still worth having on record: the model under-predicts
short-range variance substantially (empirical semivariance 0.507 at 0-500 km against a
model-implied 0.005), and adding a nugget does not close that gap either. That is a statement
about model fit at short distances, not about the correlation range.

## The operational answer

**#130's skill gate** is the right response and does not depend on resolving this. It refuses to
publish any surface that cannot beat a constant baseline on held-out spatially-blocked folds —
without caring *why* a range is long. A surface with a 3,400 km range that predicts well is fine;
one that predicts badly is refused whatever its range.

The nugget itself ships **off by default** as `FitConfig.nugget`, so the experiment stays
reproducible rather than being deleted along with the hypothesis.

Closing. Related: #122, #130, #141.
