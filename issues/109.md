## Body

The surface model has never been scored against held-out data. We can render a map that *looks*
like Piel et al. 2013 Figure 1B, but we have no evidence it predicts HbS frequency at locations
it was not fitted on. Until it does, every downstream burden number rests on an unmeasured model.

## What exists

`genomeos/validation/crossval.py` implements spatially-blocked and random k-fold CV, reporting
per-fold coverage, MAE, RMSE and log score against a constant baseline, with `FoldFailure` so a
single non-converged fold no longer aborts the run (see #108). `scripts/validate_holdout.py`
drives it. Tests pass. **It has simply never been run to completion.**

Two earlier attempts died for reasons now fixed: one fold raised `ConvergenceError` and aborted
an 8.9-hour run, and a fit at M=400 took 80 minutes because inducing spacing was far inside the
correlation range. At a well-conditioned M≈150–200 a fit is ~9 minutes, so 10 folds is ~1.5 hours.

## What this issue wants

1. Run spatially-blocked CV to completion and report the numbers.
2. **Calibration is the headline, not error.** A spatial interpolator with an honest uncertainty
   band is useful; one with a tight band in the wrong place is dangerous. Report interval coverage
   against nominal, per fold and pooled.
3. Report against the constant-frequency baseline. A GP that cannot beat a constant is not adding
   spatial information.
4. **Spatial blocking, not random folds.** Random folds leak: MAP surveys cluster, so a held-out
   point often has a neighbour a few km away in the training set, and the score measures
   interpolation between near-duplicates rather than genuine prediction. Report both, and treat
   the gap between them as the estimate of how much the random score is inflated.
5. Decide what coverage is acceptable before looking at the result, so the threshold is not fitted
   to the answer.

## Relationship to §8

This is *not* golden test 1. §8's HbS parity test scores national estimates against Piel's
published table and is blocked on #94 (population-weighted aggregation to countries). Held-out CV
is the weaker but independent check — it can run now, and it tests the surface directly rather
than an aggregate of it.

Related: #92 (Piel reports an IQR, not a 95% interval, and medians do not sum), #103 (the
inverse-logit link's extrapolation behaviour, which is what held-out points far from data will
expose most sharply).

| | spatial (honest) | random (leaky) |
|---|---|---|
| folds scored | 4/5 | **2/5** |
| MAE | **0.0388** | 0.0261 |
| MAE, constant baseline | **0.0391** | 0.0380 |
| RMSE | 0.0545 | 0.0381 |
| log score | −1027.7 | −392.0 |
| log score, baseline | −1053.6 | −431.0 |
| **skill over baseline** | **+25.9** | **+39.0** |
| coverage 95% (latent) | 0.10 | 0.44 |

## The headline is the MAE column, not the skill column

**Under spatially-blocked validation the model's point predictions are 0.0388 against a constant's 0.0391 — an improvement of 0.8%.** Predicting one global number everywhere is very nearly as accurate, per survey, as the fitted surface.

Log-score skill is positive (+25.9) because `_log_score` weights each observation by `an`: it is a sum of `ac·log(p) + (an−ac)·log(1−p)`, so large surveys dominate. The model evidently helps on well-sampled surveys and not on the rest. Both numbers are correct; they answer different questions, and **MAE is the one that speaks to "can it predict HbS frequency at a held-out location"**, which is what this issue asked.

## Spatial vs random: 34% of apparent skill is autocorrelation

Random folds report +39.0 skill and 0.0261 MAE — comfortably better than baseline. Spatial folds report +25.9 and no MAE advantage. The gap is the leakage this issue anticipated: MAP surveys cluster, so a randomly held-out point often has a near-duplicate a few km away in the training set, and the score measures interpolation between near-duplicates rather than prediction. **Anyone reporting the random number alone would be overstating skill by about a third**, and by much more on MAE.

## Three caveats that all cut the same way

1. **Coverage is not yet interpretable.** 0.10 and 0.44 score the *latent frequency* interval against noisy observations — the defect in #110, whose fix is in progress. Note the two numbers differ hugely between strategies, which is itself consistent with that diagnosis: random folds place held-out points where the latent field is pinned by a neighbour.
2. **4 of 10 folds did not converge** (#111), all on ESS. The surviving folds are plausibly the easier ones, so these scores are, if anything, optimistic.
3. **Spatial folds are the honest test and the weaker result.** That ordering is expected and correct; it is the number to quote.

## Not closing this

The measurement now exists, which is what the issue was opened for, but it says the surface does not yet demonstrably predict held-out frequencies better than a constant under honest validation. Leaving open until #110 gives a real calibration number and #111 gives folds that converge — at which point this should be re-run before any burden layer is published on top of it.

## Against this issue's five requirements

1. **Run spatially-blocked CV to completion and report the numbers** — done, 4 of 5 folds; the
   fifth was excluded under §12 and that is stated in the artifact rather than smoothed over.
2. **Calibration is the headline** — and it is the finding. Under spatial blocking a 95% interval
   covers **74%** of held-out surveys, one fold as low as **0.478**. Under grouped folds it covers
   **96%** against 0.95 nominal. The surface is calibrated where it interpolates and overconfident
   where it extrapolates. Per-fold numbers are in `data/validation/crossval_*.csv`.
3. **Report against the constant baseline** — done. Spatial beats a global constant by **3%** on
   MAE, which is within noise of no spatial skill. Grouped beats it by 29%.
4. **Spatial blocking, and report both** — done, and the inflation estimate is **37%**, measured
   spatial-versus-*grouped* rather than spatial-versus-random. That change matters: random folds
   shatter 94% of multi-site studies here, so the old spatial-versus-random gap confounded spatial
   leakage with the loss of cohort identifiability (#127). #131's grouped folds hold studies intact
   while still leaking neighbours, which isolates the spatial component. This number is
   interpretable for the first time.
5. **Decide acceptable coverage before looking at the result** — **not done, and it cannot be
   retrofitted.** No threshold was pre-registered, so any I state now is fitted to an answer I have
   already seen. Recording the omission rather than inventing a threshold. If a coverage gate is
   wanted it should be set in a separate issue, before the next run.

## Two folds excluded, both on `lengthscale`

Spatial fold 3 (ESS 186) and random fold 4 (ESS 175), against the floor of 200. In both the worst
parameter is `lengthscale`. This is **#111 recurring at draws=1000**, not the 400 it was first
reported at, so a larger sampling budget has not resolved it. Worth separating from #122: that
issue asked whether `lengthscale` is *identifiable* and the answer was yes (posterior contraction
0.40, no Zhang ridge). This is a *mixing* problem in the same parameter, which is a different fault.

## What follows from it

The undercoverage away from data is a measured argument that **#59's data-support mask has to be on
by default**. §7's four support states exist to encode precisely this distinction, and there is now
a number saying the distinction is real: 0.96 coverage near observations against 0.74 away from
them. A map that paints confident colour into unsampled regions is not merely imprecise there, it
is wrong about its own uncertainty.

This does **not** settle golden test 1 (§8 HbS parity), which scores national aggregates against
Piel's published table and remains blocked on #94.
