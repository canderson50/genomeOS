## Body

Two rendering faults found by looking at a map that was numerically correct. Both are fixed in
#143; filed because the *class* of fault has no coverage anywhere in our validation, and it will
recur.

![before and after](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bschilder/genomeOS/21b7708dbf32424be5d2c07072eb4c38361065f1/docs/figures/colour_and_marker_scaling.png)

## Fault 1: a zero-anchored colour scale erases high-frequency variants

The value panel anchored its colour scale at 0. For a variant whose frequency is high everywhere,
that puts the entire surface in a sliver at the top of the bar.

**KIR2DL1 spans 0.877 to 0.956 — 8% of a 0-to-max ramp** — and rendered as a uniform dark red
field (top panel). The model had found real structure: a trough near 0.88 across Amazonia and
Indonesia, peaks near 0.95 in East Asia and the Middle East. All of it invisible.

The question that surfaced it was simply *"even at 97% we would expect SOME variation over space,
right?"* — and the answer was yes, the model found it, the figure hid it.

For contrast, KIR3DS1 spans 0.037-0.439, which is 92% of its ramp, and looked informative all
along. So the fault only bites the variants where it is hardest to notice.

## Fault 2: markers and fill used different windows

Marker area was proportional to `frequency / max(frequency)` while the fill ran `0 -> max(cell)`.
Two unrelated scales on one figure, so **a marker's size and a cell's colour did not mean the same
thing**, and agreement between model and observation was indistinguishable from disagreement.

After the fix, Java shows a tiny marker on a deep blue cell — surface and observation agreeing
that KIR2DL1 is unusually rare there. That agreement was unreadable before.

## Why this class matters

Both faults were **arithmetically faithful and visually false**. A 0.079 span drawn on a
0-to-0.956 ramp is not a wrong number; it is a correct number rendered as a lie. Neither fault
could be caught by:

- the pandera schemas, which bound `ac <= an` and nothing about presentation;
- the convergence gate, which passed at r_hat 1.011;
- the refusal report, which had nothing to refuse;
- any test, since no test renders a figure and inspects it.

A third instance the same afternoon: `afnd_cytokines` reported the alphabetically-second allele,
which is the **major** allele for about half of loci, so TNF-alpha -308 rendered as a solid ~0.9
field worldwide. Same signature — exact arithmetic, useless picture.

**We have no validation for this class at all.** AGENTS.md's "show the map" rule is the only
defence, and it works only because a human looks. That is worth stating explicitly rather than
relying on.

## Fixed in #143

- Each surface stretches to its own range by default; `--shared-scale` restores zero-anchoring
  for side-by-side reading, where absolute magnitude must be comparable.
- Markers share the fill's window and clip at both ends exactly as the colourmap does. Legend
  exemplars sit at the window's ends and midpoint so they read against the colourbar.
- The span is *not* added to the title: the colourbar already carries it.
- Cytokine loci report the minor allele by mean frequency, decided once per locus, with the
  frequency complemented exactly. 0 of 22 loci now report a major allele.

## What would actually catch the next one

Not proposed as work, only recorded: a figure test would have to assert something about the
*rendered* output — for instance that the drawn colour range covers a stated fraction of the
colourmap, or that marker scale and fill scale share a window. Cheap to assert, and it is the only
kind of check that would have caught any of these three.

Related: #133, #143, #59.
