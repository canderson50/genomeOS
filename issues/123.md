## Body

The AFND corpus contains a population with **`an` = 6,912,132** — 3.46 million individuals, the
DKMS German donor registry. The median across the corpus is **300**, so that single population is
**23,040x** the typical weight.

A binomial (or beta-binomial) likelihood weights each observation by `an`. Nothing in the pipeline
caps, down-weights or flags this, so one population can outweigh thousands combined and, through
the GP, pin the surface across a continent.

## Why it is not simply "more data is better"

The weight is only justified if the population is what it claims to be at that precision. A donor
registry of 3.46 million is:

- **spatially aggregated** — "Germany DKMS German donors" is a whole country at one coordinate,
  and its `uncertainty_radius_km` is derived from a settlement class that cannot describe it;
- **ascertained by donor eligibility**, which removes significant disease by policy — the registry
  adapter already flags this as `disease_ascertainment_excluded`;
- **one study**, so its sampling idiosyncrasies are not averaged over as many small studies are.

Treating it as 23,040 typical observations asserts a precision the design of the study does not
support, and §7 places every observation as a disc precisely because a coordinate is not a point.

## Options

- **Cap the effective sample size** per observation, stating the cap. Standard practice in
  meta-analysis and easy to reason about, but arbitrary.
- **Scale weight by the disc area** — a country-scale coordinate should not carry city-scale
  precision, and the pipeline already knows the radius.
- **Refuse above a threshold** and record it, treating a national aggregate as out of scope for a
  point-referenced surface rather than as a very precise point.
- **Do nothing, but flag it in the artifact**, so a consumer can see one population dominating.

`needs-human-decision` on which; all four are defensible and they imply different maps.

Found while building the AFND frequency adapter, before any model was fitted.
