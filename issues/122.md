## Body

G6PD needed `lengthscale_sigma=0.4` where HbS uses the 0.7 default (#116), and that was set by
hand after three pod runs. **AFND supplies 767 modelable alleles.** Hand-tuning a prior per variant
does not scale, and a screening sweep shows why it is not optional:

```
hla:drb1-04-03   rho 2619 km        hla:drb1-01-01   rho 1826 km
hla:drb1-11-01   rho 1584 km        hla:drb1-07-01   rho 1511 km
hla:drb1-14-01   rho 1351 km
```

Against HbS at 680 km and G6PD at 547 km, these are **2-4x larger**, and every one tripped the
inducing spacing-ratio warning (0.14-0.23 < 0.25). A 2,619 km correlation range is the
near-globally-constant regime #116 identified as indistinguishable from the `intercept`.

So these five "successes" may be as informative as the failures: **they converged to a degenerate
answer**, and nothing in the pipeline flagged it. The spacing warning fired, but that reads as a
performance note rather than "this range is not credible".

## Options

- **Derive the prior from the data**, e.g. centre it on the median nearest-neighbour distance
  between that variant's populations. Cheap, per-variant, and no hand-tuning.
- **Reject an implausible range as a convergence-style refusal.** A fitted range above some
  fraction of Earth's radius is not a spatial surface; §12 already refuses a fit that has not
  mixed, and this is the same kind of statement.
- **Empirical-Bayes across variants** — fit many, then shrink each toward the pooled range. Uses
  the corpus breadth rather than fighting it.

Whichever is chosen, the current situation is worse than any of them: the prior is a per-variant
constant maintained by hand, and a degenerate fit passes silently.

Related: #116, #39 (threshold calibration), #40.

### bschilder, 2026-08-27T04:54:43Z (https://github.com/genomeOS/genomeOS/issues/122#issuecomment-5434551154)

Resolved. Both halves of this issue were investigated; one is fixed, and the other turned out to
rest on a false premise.

## The scaling problem: fixed

`derive_lengthscale_prior` (landing with #124) reads two anchors off the observation locations
themselves, so no prior is hand-set per variant:

- **lower** — three times the median nearest-neighbour spacing;
- **upper** — the sampled extent divided by `2 * LENGTHSCALE_REGIONS`.

That closes the original complaint: 767 alleles no longer need 767 hand-tuned `lengthscale_sigma`
values.

## The degeneracy premise: disproven

This issue asserted that the long ranges above were degenerate, "the near-globally-constant regime
#116 identified as indistinguishable from the `intercept`". That was tested directly and it is not
what is happening.

`hla:drb1-04-04` was refit with the convergence gate relaxed so the suspect fit is **retained**
rather than refused, and the posterior inspected:

**Not a Zhang (2004) identifiability ridge.** A ridge makes three predictions, and all three fail:

| Diagnostic | A ridge predicts | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| `corr(log rho, log sigma)` | strongly positive, near 1 | +0.466 |
| `d log sigma / d log rho` | approximately nu = 2.5 | +0.369 |
| sd of `log(sigma^2/rho^2nu)` vs sd of `log rho` | *smaller*, the combination is what is pinned | 1.248 vs 0.278, i.e. 4.5x **larger** |

The third row is decisive. The entire content of Zhang's result is that the microergodic
combination is estimable while the individual parameters are not; here the combination is the
loosest quantity in the posterior and `rho` is among the tightest. That is the signature inverted.

**Not confounded with the intercept either.** The stated mechanism was that a long range makes the
field indistinguishable from a constant. Measured on the same fit:

- GP spatial contrast across space: **0.616 logit**, which is **93% of its own amplitude**. The
  field is using nearly all the variation available to it. It is not flat.
- `corr(log rho, intercept)` = **+0.015**. No trade-off with the intercept at all.

**And `rho` is well identified.** Prior sd 0.694 to posterior sd 0.278, a contraction of **0.40**,
with the posterior at 3,643 km (95% CI 2,111 to 6,464) and **97.9% of its mass above the prior's
own upper anchor of 2,061 km**. The likelihood pushed it there against the prior.

## The prior barely affects predictions

Five-fold cross-validation on three long-range alleles under two priors, the derived anchor and a
weak one centred on domain/5 (~3,000 km, sd 1.0):

| Allele | Folds | Derived | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| DRB1*04:04 | spatial | 0.0279 | 0.0304 |
| DRB1*12:01 | spatial | 0.0335 | 0.0335 |
| DRB1*15:01 | spatial | 0.0446 | 0.0447 |
| DRB1*12:01 | grouped | 0.0184 | 0.0183 |
| DRB1*15:01 | grouped | 0.0328 | 0.0330 |

Five of six agree to the fourth decimal. A 20x change in prior median moves essentially nothing,
which is the same conclusion the ridge test reached, from the prediction side instead of the
posterior side.

## Disposition of the three options

- **Derive the prior from the data** — done, and it is the default.
- **Reject an implausible range as a convergence-style refusal** — **do not implement.** A long
  range here is a well-identified estimate from a spatially structured field, not a failure. A
  refusal rule would discard correct answers, and the threshold it would need (">2000 km") was
  never derived from anything.
- **Empirical Bayes across variants** — filed as #128, with the artifact-immutability conflict it
  raises spelled out. Not scheduled.

## One correction to make in the code

`LENGTHSCALE_REGIONS = 4` has a docstring stating that K=4 "excludes the degenerate ones
(DRB1*12:01 3,143 km, DRB1*04:04 4,111 km)". K was chosen partly *because* it excluded those fits,
and the docstring then cites the exclusions as justification. That reasoning is circular and should
not stay in the file, even though nothing now depends on it. Tracked in #130.

## What replaces this issue

The investigation found a real problem in the place this one was looking: spatial skill varies by
variant, and the pipeline publishes surfaces for all of them identically. Under spatial folds the
model beat a constant baseline for one of three alleles, tied on one, and was *worse* on one.
**#130** proposes gating publication on measured skill. That is the actionable successor.

Closing. Related: #116, #128, #129, #130.
