## Why this candidate

HbC (`HBB` p.Glu6Lys) is the clearest additional disease allele with evidence comparable to the HbS benchmark. The published analysis used georeferenced community allele counts to produce a validated 5 km African frequency surface with spatial uncertainty.

Primary starting point:

- Piel et al., *The distribution of haemoglobin C and its prevalence in newborns in Africa*: https://www.nature.com/articles/srep01671
- The paper's supplementary information and source list; the study reports 174 eligible data sources and the MAP resource previously exposed the assembled survey database.
- Context on the MAP red-cell-disorder resource and its observation counts: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3738938/

## Scientific objective

Determine whether the published HbC survey observations can be legally and reproducibly transformed into a P1 allele-count dataset, preserving measured observations separately from any P2 surface.

## Expected output and acceptance evidence

- A source inventory with stable URLs/DOIs, access route, license, and redistribution terms.
- One row per representative survey location with coordinates, geographic uncertainty, sample size, and recoverable A/C allele counts or AA/AC/CC genotype counts.
- Sampling design, cohort identity, ascertainment, diagnostic method, and survey year where reported.
- Reproduction of at least three published survey frequencies from normalized rows.
- A documented comparison target from the paper's validation and national newborn estimates for a later golden test.
- An explicit ingest/no-ingest recommendation.

## Engineering interface and downstream consumers

If qualified, open a separate adapter issue targeting the existing P1 observation schema. P2 must fit a new versioned surface from normalized observations; it must not import the paper's raster as measured data. P3 can later model HbCC and HbSC outcomes only through an explicit genotype/burden contract.

## Suggested starting steps

1. Download and inspect the article supplements and enumerate every survey-level field that can be recovered.
2. Locate the archived MAP/ROAD-MAP HbC export. If the old portal is unavailable, check the Oxford repository and contact the corresponding authors rather than transcribing points from figures.
3. Confirm that coordinates identify sampling locations and record whether any are polygon centroids.
4. Derive C-allele frequency only from reported allele/genotype counts; retain the original counts.
5. Check whether surveys excluded nonrepresentative, patient, migrant, or targeted ethnic samples using the paper's stated rules.
6. Verify licensing before committing or serving any source data.

## Refusal conditions and pitfalls

- Do not use affected newborn estimates as observations.
- Do not infer counts from rounded map colors or published raster pixels.
- Do not merge HbC with HbS: HbCC and HbSC are clinically different outcomes.
- Do not assign a default uncertainty radius or ascertainment design.
- If only a rendered surface is recoverable, retain it as a literature comparator—not a P1 source.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Raw survey records or an authoritative machine-readable export are located.
- [ ] Terms permit the intended research/serving use, or the restriction is documented.
- [ ] Required P1 fields are present without invented defaults.
- [ ] Published point frequencies are reproduced.
- [ ] Follow-up adapter issue is opened only if the source qualifies.
- [ ] #180 and #3 are updated with the decision.
