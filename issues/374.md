## Body

## TL;DR

The global modeling plan requires five outer folds and three inner folds when feasible. B1G currently creates one inner fold for every block remaining after the outer holdout, so a five-block benchmark produces four inner folds. Enforce an explicit, deterministic three-fold inner protocol before using a nested result as evidence for the plan.

## Trigger

`_select_candidate` passes every outer-training block label to `build_buffered_splits`. That splitter correctly creates one fold per supplied label. With five outer labels, each outer-training set retains four labels and therefore runs four inner folds. The current four-block unit fixture happens to leave three labels after its outer holdout, so it does not expose the five-block behavior.

This conflicts with `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-09-global-af-modeling.md`, which freezes “Five outer / three inner folds when feasible.” It also inflates a nine-candidate outer fold from 27 to 36 inner fits.

## Scientific objective

Make nested model selection match the prespecified three-inner-fold protocol without using allele counts, candidate predictions, convergence results, or challenger-versus-baseline outcomes to choose the partition.

## Prespecified grouping rule

For each outer-training set:

1. require at least three distinct original block labels; otherwise mark selection infeasible;
2. count training records per original block using identities only;
3. sort original blocks by descending record count, breaking ties by literal block ID;
4. assign each block in order to the currently smallest of three inner groups, breaking group-size ties by group index;
5. map every original block to exactly one `inner-0`, `inner-1`, or `inner-2` label, then apply the existing dependency and 300 km buffer exclusions through `build_buffered_splits`.

The rule uses outer-training metadata only, gives every retained training row exactly one inner test opportunity before exclusions, balances survey counts without reading outcomes, and is deterministic under row reordering.

## Measurable output and acceptance evidence

- every feasible five-outer-block plan has exactly three inner folds per outer fold;
- all original outer-training block labels are assigned exactly once and no inner group is empty;
- assignments are invariant to observation, assignment-row, and dependency-edge ordering;
- held-out outer coordinates and counts cannot change the inner grouping;
- inner counts, group membership, algorithm version, and hashes are retained in checkpoint/artifact evidence;
- failed or infeasible inner folds remain explicit and cannot win selection;
- a regression with five outer blocks fails before the fix and proves 27 candidate fits plus one outer fit after the fix;
- existing split leakage, seed, score, convergence, checkpoint, and full-suite tests pass.

## Engineering component and public interface

Add a pure helper at the nested-benchmark boundary that maps outer-training block assignments to exactly three versioned inner groups. Keep `build_buffered_splits` unchanged: its one-fold-per-label behavior is correct and is used by other benchmark tracks. Record the inner grouping algorithm and mapping in immutable B1G checkpoint evidence so a later artifact cannot be confused with the four-fold behavior.

## Assumptions and refusals

- Do not choose three of four blocks and discard the fourth.
- Do not use AC, AN, model scores, warnings, or outcomes to balance groups.
- Do not silently treat four inner folds as equivalent to the frozen three-fold protocol.
- Any artifact produced under the four-fold implementation remains a separate development protocol and is not evidence for the prespecified three-fold gate.

## Expert review

A spatial statistician should confirm that outcome-blind greedy balancing of original geographic blocks is an acceptable way to form three inner folds, and that combining two original blocks in one inner test fold preserves the intended leakage and geographic generalization test.
