## Body

## Question and provisional finding

How many genetic diseases have allele frequencies mapped with geographic precision comparable to the HbS and G6PD resources—rather than only by country, registry cohort, or broad ancestry bucket?

Using the eligibility standard below, the provisional answer is **three disease systems at full or near-full parity, including HbS and G6PD themselves**. **HbC is the only obvious additional disease allele.** A broader definition that accepts regional-only maps, sparse interpolation, or incompletely penetrant risk alleles yields roughly **five to six disease systems**. Even generous inclusion remains under ten.

This is a literature-derived working estimate, not a claim that a formal global census exists. This issue complements the general source assessment in #3 by applying a disease-allele-specific eligibility test.

## Scientific contract

### 1. Scientific objective / product claim

Determine which disease-associated alleles have sufficiently local, representative allele-frequency evidence to support an Atlas observation layer and, separately, a defensible inferred surface.

The resulting claim must distinguish:

- a measured, georeferenced allele frequency;
- a phenotype-derived estimate of an aggregate disease-allele frequency;
- a modeled spatial surface;
- country/administrative summaries and ancestry-group aggregates.

### 2. Measurable output and acceptance evidence

Produce a DOI-level evidence matrix for every candidate below containing:

- disease, gene, named variant or explicitly defined allelic class;
- observation count, participant count, geographic coverage, and coordinate precision;
- whether inputs are direct genotypes, molecularly resolved variants, or phenotypes converted to allele frequency;
- recoverable allele numerator/denominator and sample size;
- sampling-design and ascertainment information;
- existing surface resolution, validation, and uncertainty output;
- raw-data availability, license, redistribution terms, and provenance;
- a final classification: `qualifies`, `regional-only`, `risk/susceptibility locus`, or `does not qualify`.

Acceptance requires independently reproducing at least one published point frequency from each qualifying source and documenting whether the underlying observations—not merely rendered map images—are available for ingestion.

### 3. Engineering component and public interface

This is a P1 source-qualification task. A candidate can advance to an observations adapter only if it can produce the existing versioned observation schema without invented values. Any later P2 surface must consume those immutable observations through the public artifact contract; published rasters must never be presented as measured data.

### 4. Assumptions, refusal conditions, and downstream consumers

Assumptions:

- “Comparable to HbS/G6PD” means subnational georeferencing, representative local sampling, allele-frequency evidence, and a continuous surface with uncertainty where one has been published.
- Regional maps may be useful, but must be labeled as regional rather than global peers.

Refuse ingestion as a measured-observation layer when a source provides only:

- continental or broad genetic-ancestry buckets;
- country-level averages without recoverable local observations;
- frequencies among diagnosed patients rather than a representative population sample;
- affected-birth or disease-prevalence estimates without the underlying allele counts;
- map images or interpolated surfaces with no recoverable observations;
- ambiguous coordinates, ascertainment, or `uncertainty_radius_km`.

Downstream consumers are the P0 population registry, P1 observation store, P2 surface fitter, P3 burden engine, and the P5 dataset selector/provenance UI.

## Eligibility standard

A full peer should have all of the following:

1. A disease-causing allele or clinically coherent allelic class.
2. Representative community/population samples rather than affected-case registries.
3. Local coordinates, or geography precise enough to encode an honest uncertainty radius.
4. Allele counts or sufficient genotype/phenotype counts to derive allele frequency.
5. Continental or otherwise disease-relevant geographic coverage.
6. A validated continuous surface with spatially explicit uncertainty, or observations adequate for genomeOS to fit one without extrapolating beyond support.

## Provisional evidence matrix

| Tier | Allele/system | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Current benchmark | **HbS / sickle-cell disease** | Global georeferenced survey database; continuous allele-frequency surface and posterior uncertainty. [Piel et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1104) |
| Current benchmark, caveat | **G6PD deficiency** | Continuous map from 1,734 local surveys with uncertainty, but the principal surface infers a collective deficiency-allele frequency largely from enzyme phenotypes. The variant-specific follow-up found only 24 suitable unscreened population surveys. [Howes et al. prevalence surface](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1001339); [variant mapping](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3835423/) |
| Full peer / priority candidate | **HbC / haemoglobin C disease** | Georeferenced population allele counts; validated continuous 5 km African surface and uncertainty. This is the clearest next Atlas candidate. [Piel et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep01671) |
| Excellent spatial genetics, not a genetic disease | **Duffy-null / FY\*BES** | 821 geographically unique community surveys; global 5–10 km allele-frequency and uncertainty surfaces. This is a malaria-susceptibility/protection locus, not conventionally a genetic disease. [Howes et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1265) |
| Strong regional peer | **α-thalassaemia** | Direct genetic survey evidence and 1 km surfaces with uncertainty for α⁰, α⁺, and non-deletional classes, but only for Thailand; neighboring Southeast Asian evidence is much sparser. [Than et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6533055/) |
| Near-peer risk locus | **APOL1 G1/G2 kidney-risk variants** | Named-variant frequency surfaces across sub-Saharan Africa, but interpolation rests on 40 populations in 21 countries and the alleles confer incompletely penetrant CKD risk rather than a Mendelian diagnosis. [Cooper et al.](https://elifesciences.org/articles/25461/figures) |
| Regional/coarse | **HFE p.Cys282Tyr haemochromatosis risk** | Strong genotype-based regional analysis, but mapped over 29 British/Irish regions rather than a dense continuous surface. [Atkins et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65511-7) |
| Does not currently qualify | **CFTR/F508del, HbE, β-thalassaemia, founder mutations** | Commonly mapped by country, administrative region, ancestry group, or mutation proportions among patients. β-thalassaemia also comprises hundreds of causal alleles, so a single pooled surface would be biologically lossy without an explicit allelic-class contract. |

## Recommended scope

1. **Qualify HbC first.** Confirm raw observation availability, fields, license, and whether the published validation target can become a golden test.
2. Evaluate **α-thalassaemia** as a clearly regional, allelic-class dataset.
3. Evaluate **Duffy-null** under a separate “infectious-disease susceptibility/protection” category, not as a genetic disease.
4. Retain **APOL1** as a disease-risk candidate with visibly weaker spatial support.
5. Treat **HFE** as a regional descriptive layer unless finer observations can be recovered.

Do not open implementation PRs for any source until access terms and schema completeness are established. In particular, do not substitute country centroids, ancestry labels, or defaults for missing ascertainment and geographic uncertainty.
