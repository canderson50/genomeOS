## Body

The first completed spatially-blocked CV run reports **coverage of 0.10 for a 95% interval** and
**0.03 for a 50% interval** (4/5 folds; 1 did not converge). Before concluding the model is
overconfident, note that the scorer is comparing two different quantities.

## What is being compared

`crossval.cross_validate` does:

```python
observed = ac / an
inside_95 = (observed >= predicted["q025"]) & (observed <= predicted["q975"])
```

and `predicted` comes from `SurfaceFit.predict`, whose quantiles are those of

```python
pm.Deterministic("freq_pred", pm.math.invlogit(f_pred_expr))
```

`f_pred_expr` is the latent field **on the reference design, with no cohort offset** (§7.1a, and
deliberately so — the map should show what a well-ascertained survey would have measured).

So the interval describes the *latent frequency at a location*. It is scored against an
*empirical frequency from a finite survey*, which additionally carries:

1. **binomial / beta-binomial sampling noise** — MAP surveys are frequently tens to a few hundred
   people, so this term is not small,
2. **that survey's own `β_cohort`**, drawn from `Normal(0, cohort_sd)` across 344 cohorts,
3. **its design effect**.

An interval missing three variance components that are present in the data will under-cover, and
the amount is not a rounding error. **The 0.10 is therefore not yet evidence of a miscalibrated
model.** It may be entirely an artifact of the comparison.

## What the correct check is

Score the **posterior predictive for the observed counts**, not the latent frequency: draw
`ac_rep ~ BetaBinomial(an_test, p, concentration)` where `p` includes a cohort offset marginalised
over `Normal(0, cohort_sd)` — for a held-out survey the cohort effect is unknown, so integrating
over it is the honest out-of-sample treatment — and check whether the observed `ac` falls in the
resulting interval.

Both quantities are worth reporting, and they answer different questions:

- **latent-frequency interval** — "how well do we know the underlying frequency here?" This is
  what the *map* claims, and what §7's `posterior_contraction` and the support mask are about.
- **posterior-predictive interval** — "would a new survey here land where we expect?" This is the
  calibration claim, and the one that belongs in a validation report.

## What is not in doubt

Skill over the constant baseline is **+25.9** in log score (-1027.7 vs -1053.6), so the surface is
extracting genuine spatial signal. That number does not depend on the interval question.

## Also worth fixing while here

One fold in five failed to converge and was excluded. That is the `FoldFailure` tolerance working
as designed (#108), but a 20% fold-failure rate is itself a finding — it should be reported in any
validation summary rather than silently averaged over.
