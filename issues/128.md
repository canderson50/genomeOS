## Body

Raised while deciding how to fix the range/variance identifiability problem (#122). **Filed rather
than built**, because it has an architectural consequence that needs deciding before any code.

## The opportunity

AFND gives 767 alleles measured in **the same 942 populations**. A hierarchical model could pool
them to estimate a shared spatial scale, with 767 x ~450 observations informing one range. Given
that Zhang (2004) shows range and variance are *not separately identifiable* from one field under
fixed-domain asymptotics, borrowing strength across fields is one of the few things that genuinely
adds information rather than redistributing it.

## The architectural cost, which is the reason this is not simply a good idea

§5 keys artifacts `(variant_id, model_version, data_version)` and makes them **immutable**: a refit
publishes alongside rather than overwriting, so an older citation keeps resolving.

Under a pooled model, **every variant's estimate depends on every other variant in the fit**.
Adding one allele changes all 767 published surfaces. "Immutable per variant" stops meaning
anything, and a citation to `hla:drb1-04-04` no longer identifies a fixed number — it identifies a
number that was true for one particular set of co-fitted variants.

That is not a reason to refuse it. It is a reason for the decision to be explicit, because it
changes what a published artifact *is*.

## It also assumes the thing in question

Pooling assumes the alleles share a spatial scale. #116 is the standing counterexample: G6PD
deficiency pools ~200 alleles with different geographic origins — A- across Africa, Mediterranean
through the Middle East, Mahidol in Southeast Asia — and that is precisely why it has no single
scale. If HLA alleles are similarly heterogeneous, pooling shrinks genuinely different variants
toward a common value and invents agreement.

## The middle path

**Empirical Bayes.** Fit variants independently, estimate the distribution of range across them,
feed that back as each variant's prior, refit. Two stages rather than one joint model:

- borrows strength, which is the point;
- keeps per-variant models and per-variant artifacts, so §5 survives intact;
- makes the shared-scale assumption *checkable* — if the fitted ranges are bimodal or heavy-tailed,
  that is evidence against pooling rather than something the model quietly absorbs.

Not fully Bayesian, and the second stage's uncertainty is understated. That trade seems worth it
against breaking artifact immutability.

## What would need deciding first

1. Does §5's immutability guarantee permit a model whose outputs are co-dependent at all?
2. If empirical Bayes: pooled across everything, or within gene / within group?
3. How is the shared-scale assumption tested rather than assumed?
