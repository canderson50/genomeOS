## Body

The spec and the code now disagree in two places. Both were deliberate, both are documented in `genomeos/surfaces/fit.py` and in #84 — but the spec is the citable artifact, so it should say what we actually do.

## 1. Inference engine

§7 says:

> **Fit.** INLA with SPDE approximation. Chosen over MCMC (Piel's approach) and over variational GPs because it gives proper marginal posteriors at batch-feasible cost, and because it is the same lineage the model-based geostatistics literature in this exact application already uses — which matters for defensibility.

We use **PyMC with a Hilbert-space GP (HSGP)**. The reasoning is in #34. The paragraph's *requirements* still hold exactly — proper marginal posteriors at batch-feasible cost — so the amendment is to the method, not to the rationale.

The defensibility sentence needs rewording rather than deleting: §8 already makes our standard of proof parity against published estimates, which is a stronger argument than shared lineage. Worth saying so explicitly, since it is the load-bearing claim.

## 2. Kernel smoothness

§7 says `Matérn-3/2`. The implementation uses **Matérn-5/2**, because HSGP's spectral density is better behaved for it.

**This one genuinely wants an expert read.** Smoothness is a modelling assumption about how allele frequency varies over space, not an implementation detail — 5/2 asserts a smoother field than 3/2. If 3/2 is the right assumption for this application, the engine should accommodate the spec rather than the other way round.

## Also worth folding in while §7 is open

- `hsgp_m` (basis functions per dimension) is a new accuracy/cost dial with no counterpart in the SPDE formulation. It is calibrated in #39 and should be named in §6's artifact fields alongside `model_version`, so a published surface records how many basis functions produced it.
- §6 lists `beta_design_applied` as an artifact field. The fit now sets it `False` when only one sampling design is present, since `β_design` is unidentifiable without contrast. §7.1a should state that explicitly — it is a refusal, and refusals belong in the spec (§9).

Related: #34, #35, #39, #83.

## What's changed since this was filed

1. **INLA appears three more times.** §5 l.61 (BigQuery batch, per-variant INLA-SPDE fit), §10 l.225 (per-variant INLA fits as a job array), and the §12 failure-mode row at l.253 ("INLA fit fails to converge"). Whether these become edits or entries in #424's amendments note, they should be covered alongside l.129.
2. **The basis dial now depends on the approximation.** `FitConfig.approximation` is `"hsgp"` (the default, dial `hsgp_m`) or `"inducing"` (dial `n_inducing` plus `inducing_placement`), and `config.py:106` describes `n_inducing` as "the accuracy/cost dial that replaces hsgp_m (#39)". The 2026-09-16 parity run used **150 H3 inducing points**, not HSGP. So the field §6 needs is the approximation together with its dial, not `hsgp_m` alone.
3. **§7's equation is out of date in a second way.** It states a Binomial likelihood, and so does #34's decision record (l.86). The default has been `beta_binomial` since #83, where the binomial arm failed to mix (R-hat 2.61). The published HbS manifests record `beta_binomial`.
4. **The equation has no nugget.** `FitConfig.nugget` (off by default; #137) adds an observation-level logit term. It enters the likelihood but not prediction (`config.py:165–169`). §7 should say so, since that asymmetry affects what a published surface means.
5. **The hierarchical hyperpriors are planned, not running.** §7 describes ρ and σ² under a prior shared across allele-frequency deciles, fitted once and frozen. That is #36, which is open. What runs today is the per-variant `lengthscale_prior="derived"` from #122 (`config.py:146–152`). The rewrite should say which is current and point at #36, so the spec doesn't describe borrowing that no published surface has done.

## The item that is more than docs: a surface can't be traced to its engine

The body proposes naming `hsgp_m` in §6. I think the underlying gap is wider. I fetched the three published HbS manifests (`store/artifacts/chr11-5227002-T-A__{v1,v2,v3}__map-2026-08/manifest.json`, sha256-checked against release `2026-10-18`). Their fields:

`artifact_format, correlation_range_km, data_version, lengthscale_sigma, likelihood, measurement, model_version, n_cells, n_observations, prior_frequency_sd, resolution, support_counts, target_grid_source, target_grid_version, variant_id`

They record `likelihood` (`beta_binomial`), `lengthscale_sigma` (0.7) and `correlation_range_km` (680.3, identical across all three). They do **not** record the approximation, its dial, the kernel, `nugget`, or `lengthscale_prior`. That last omission matters: under the default `"derived"` mode, `lengthscale_sigma` doesn't set the prior (`config.py:150`), so the recorded 0.7 may not describe the fit, and the manifest can't say which mode was used.

`model_version` is a free-text argument defaulting to `"v1"` (`scripts/publish_artifacts.py:121`). Artifacts stay uniquely keyed as long as it is bumped. But a cited surface can't be traced from the artifact itself to the engine and kernel that produced it, which is what §5's immutability is for. My suggestion is to widen the body's `hsgp_m` item into adding `approximation`, its dial, `kernel`, `nugget` and `lengthscale_prior` to `ArtifactManifest`, with a `freeze_contract` diff as the review surface. Happy to do it under this issue or as a separate one, whichever you prefer.

## The kernel question: why it wants its own issue

`fit.py:596–598` gives two justifications for 5/2: HSGP's spectral density is better behaved for it, and the smoothness choice "is calibrated against HbS in #39". Neither currently covers the run v1 is judged by:

- **The parity run didn't use HSGP.** On the inducing-point path the full Matérn covariance is evaluated directly, so the spectral-density argument doesn't apply.
- **#39 doesn't calibrate smoothness.** It covers resolution promotion and contraction thresholds; I found no mention of the kernel or smoothness in it.

Two things bear on what the comparison should be:

- **The nugget question is part of it.** #137's semivariograms put 31–39% of the sill at 0–250 km. A field with no nugget has to absorb that short-range variance through smoothness and range, so 3/2 versus 5/2 should be compared with the nugget both on and off, or the comparison mostly measures the missing nugget.
- **Piel used an exponential kernel** (Matérn-1/2, θ = 0.199 Earth radii; Web Appendix 1 p.11 and Web Table 5). That makes it a natural third arm for parity, and its tail correlation is roughly twice as long as the current fit's.

Because smoothness is a modelling assumption rather than a description, I'd suggest a separate `type:science` issue for it, so the docs reconciliation here isn't blocked on the expert read. I can register that comparison against the held-out score in advance, once the frozen fold files are available (see my note on #45).
