## Body

Design §7 specifies a binomial likelihood:

```
AC_i ~ Binomial(AN_i, expit(f(s_i) + β_design[i] + β_cohort[i]))
```

A malaria-specific comparison study ([Comparison of new computational methods for spatial modelling of malaria](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10664662/)) found that **INLA with a standard binomial response produced "spuriously flat, near-zero predictions on sparse data"**, because the data were overdispersed relative to binomial. **Switching to Beta-binomial resolved it.**

That is the sharpest documented failure mode in exactly our application domain, and it lands on the likelihood rather than on the inference engine — so it survives the engine decision in #34.

Two reasons it matters here specifically:

1. "Spuriously flat, near-zero predictions on sparse data" is precisely the artifact the `prior_dominated` mask (§7.1b) exists to catch. If the likelihood is producing it, the mask is doing remedial work that a better likelihood would make unnecessary — and a mask is a worse fix than a correct model.
2. Golden test 1 (#45) is HbS parity against Piel et al., who worked in this same overdispersed-prevalence setting. If our likelihood is misspecified in the way this paper documents, parity is where it will show up — expensively and late.

## The counter-argument, stated fairly

That study models infection *prevalence*, which is overdispersed partly for epidemiological reasons — infection clusters socially and spatially in ways that genotype does not. Allele counts may be closer to genuinely binomial, since each individual's genotype is fixed and sampling within a population is closer to iid.

So this is **not** a settled "use Beta-binomial". It is a question the data should answer.

## Proposed work

- Implement both likelihoods behind one switch in the surface fit (Beta-binomial nests Binomial, so the extra dispersion parameter is cheap).
- Compare on the MAP HbS survey data, which is the best-ascertained source we have.
- Record the choice per artifact, the way `beta_design_applied` already is (§6) — the likelihood is an assumption and should be visible on the layer info panel rather than buried.
- Decide before #45 (golden test 1) is scored, not after.

## Skills wanted

`wants-expert-review` — a spatial statistician's or genetic epidemiologist's read on whether allele-count data from georeferenced panels shows the overdispersion this paper found in prevalence surveys. That judgement is worth more than the implementation.

Related: #34 (engine decision), #35 (the fit), #45 (golden test 1).

This issue asked whether overdispersion in georeferenced allele-count panels justifies a beta-binomial over a binomial likelihood, and explicitly framed it as *"not a settled 'use Beta-binomial' — it is a question the data should answer."* It has now been answered, on the best-ascertained source we have.

## Evidence

Both likelihoods were implemented behind one switch and fitted to the same 857 MAP HbS surveys, same seed, same everything else:

| | binomial | beta-binomial |
|---|---|---|
| r_hat | **2.61** | **1.02** |
| ESS | **2** | **378** |
| GP amplitude | 11.5 | 1.79 |
| output | allele frequencies **> 0.9** | plausible surface |

This is not a fit-quality margin. Under the binomial likelihood the sampler **does not mix at all** — r_hat 2.61 with ESS 2 means the chains never explored a common distribution, and the fit is rejected outright by the §12 convergence gate. The amplitude of 11.5 shows why: with no dispersion parameter, the only way to absorb survey-to-survey variance is to inflate the spatial field, which drives the latent surface to saturation and produces HbS frequencies above 0.9 — biologically impossible, and the visible symptom that started this investigation.

Beta-binomial nests binomial (large `concentration` recovers it exactly), so the data were free to choose. The fitted concentration is **~36**, far from the binomial limit. The overdispersion this issue anticipated from the prevalence-survey literature is present in allele-count panels too, and it is large.

## Delivered

- `LIKELIHOODS = ("binomial", "beta_binomial")` behind one switch, validated in `FitConfig.__post_init__`
- beta-binomial as the default; `test_the_binomial_likelihood_remains_selectable` guards the switch so this remains re-testable rather than a one-off
- the choice recorded on the fit via `SurfaceFit.config.likelihood`

## What is deliberately *not* claimed

The diagnostics establish that **something** overdispersed is present, not that its source is biological. With 344 cohorts, cohort structure is also absorbing variance here, and a mis-specified cohort term could masquerade as overdispersion. That refinement is worth a spatial statistician's eye — but it would tune the model, not reverse this choice, so it is bonus work rather than a blocker.

Surfacing the likelihood on the artifact (§6, for the layer info panel) still has no artifact writer to surface it in. That belongs to P2/P4 artifact work, not here.
