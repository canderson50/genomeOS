## Body

Golden test 2 (#46) needs G6PD, and G6PD does not fit the data model.

§6 keys observations and artifacts by `variant_id`, and `OBSERVATIONS_SCHEMA` enforced it as
`chr{N}-{pos}-{ref}-{alt}`. **G6PD deficiency is not a variant.** It is an enzyme phenotype caused
by roughly 200 different alleles — G6PD A-, Mediterranean, Mahidol, Viangchan and others — and
MAP's surveys measure *enzyme activity*, not genotype. There is no single allele the 875 usable
surveys could honestly be keyed by, and no allele that dominates globally: A- dominates in Africa,
Mediterranean across the Middle East and India.

Howes et al. 2012 modelled the aggregate deficiency allele frequency as a single quantity, which
is exactly what makes a parity comparison possible. So the science wants a composite even though
the schema wants a locus.

## What was done, minimally

`VARIANT_ID_PATTERN` now also admits `phenotype:[a-z0-9-]+`, and the G6PD adapter writes
`phenotype:g6pd-deficiency`. The prefix is deliberately not parseable as a locus so a composite
cannot be mistaken for a variant in a filename, a join key, or a glance at a table. The contract
diff is committed as the review surface.

**This is a patch on the schema, not a decision about the model.** It unblocks #46 and nothing
more.

## What actually needs deciding

1. **Does §6 admit phenotype layers at all**, or is a composite a second class of artifact with
   its own key space? Every downstream consumer currently assumes an id is a locus.
2. **What does a burden layer mean for a composite?** The HWE expressions in
   `burden/expressions.py` take an allele frequency and an inheritance mode. For G6PD the
   "allele" is an aggregate over alleles of differing severity — A- is class III, Mediterranean
   is class II — so an affected count computed from the aggregate mixes clinical severities that
   a clinician would not pool.
3. **What does the API return** for `phenotype:g6pd-deficiency` when a caller asks for variant
   metadata? There is no dbSNP id, no position, no ref/alt.
4. **Is this the only case?** Thalassaemia and many pharmacogenes have the same shape. If so, the
   composite is a category the design should name rather than a G6PD exception.

Needs a clinical-genetics read on (2) in particular — pooling class II and class III deficiency
into one affected count may be indefensible even where the allele frequency is well estimated.

Related: #46, #42 (penetrance, which has the same severity-pooling problem), #32.

### bschilder, 2026-08-26T02:51:29Z (https://github.com/genomeOS/genomeOS/issues/116#issuecomment-5419904303)

**Empirical support: the composite does not behave as one spatial field, and the sampler says so precisely.**

Fitting G6PD on the real 910-survey corpus fails to converge, and the per-parameter diagnostic identifies exactly which parameter:

```
parameter         max r_hat   min ESS
lengthscale           1.469         8   <-- worst r_hat
f                     1.454         8
p                     1.418         8
beta_cohort           1.386         9
z_u                   1.340         9
cohort_sd             1.340        10
concentration         1.271        11
amplitude             1.102        30
intercept             1.054       111
freq_pred             1.002      1072
```

**`lengthscale` is the root and everything downstream inherits it.** `intercept` (1.054) and `amplitude` (1.102) are nearly fine — the model can agree on the mean level and on how much the field varies. What it cannot agree on is **how far spatial correlation extends**.

That is the one parameter whose failure implicates this issue directly. `lengthscale` assumes a **single stationary spatial scale**, and G6PD deficiency has no such thing: A− across sub-Saharan Africa, Mediterranean through the Middle East and South Asia, Mahidol and Viangchan in Southeast Asia. Each allele has its own geographic footprint, so no single correlation range describes their sum. The chains oscillate between the short range that fits dense point surveys and the long range that fits continental structure, because both are defensible descriptions of a field that is genuinely a mixture.

## It is not a budget problem, which was worth establishing

| draws | r_hat | ESS |
|---|---|---|
| 400 | 1.492 | 8 |
| 1,200 | 1.193 | 15 |

Tripling the budget moved ESS from 8 to 15. Reaching 200 by that trajectory needs roughly **13× more sampling** — tens of thousands of draws. HbS, by contrast, went from failing at ESS 199 to fitting cleanly (ρ = 680 km) on the same 3× increase. **More compute is the right answer for HbS and the wrong answer for G6PD**, and the diagnostic is what separates them.

## What this makes concrete for the questions above

Question 2 in this issue asked whether a burden layer over a composite is defensible. This is evidence that even the **surface** may not be, independent of severity pooling: if the alleles do not share a spatial scale, a single GP is the wrong model regardless of what is done downstream.

Cheap next tests, in order:
- **Refit restricted to one region** (Africa alone, or Southeast Asia alone). If a single-region fit converges, global pooling is the problem and the composite is confirmed as the cause.
- **An informative prior on `lengthscale`.** If it converges under a tight prior, the range is merely weakly identified rather than genuinely multi-scale — a much better outcome.
- **Per-allele surfaces** where the underlying allele is reported, which is the honest model but needs data this source does not carry.

Also noted while diagnosing: 13 of the 910 surveys type a **single male** (`an = 1`), and 52 have `an ≤ 5`, together carrying 157 of 316,448 alleles. Not fatal — the likelihood weights them correctly — but they add cohort levels for almost no information and are worth a look separately.
