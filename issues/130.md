## Body

Measured while testing whether the lengthscale prior in #122 was the cause of long fitted ranges.
It is not, and the experiment found something more useful.

## The lengthscale is not the problem

Two independent negatives:

1. **No identifiability ridge.** Refitting `hla:drb1-04-04` with the convergence gate relaxed so the
   degenerate fit is retained: `corr(log rho, log sigma)` = +0.466 (a ridge needs ~1),
   `d log sigma / d log rho` = +0.369 (a Matern-5/2 ridge predicts nu = 2.5), and the microergodic
   combination `log(sigma^2/rho^2nu)` has sd 1.248 against 0.278 for `log(rho)` alone. Zhang (2004)
   requires the combination to be the *tightest* thing in the posterior; here it is the loosest, by
   4.5x. Posterior contraction on rho is 0.40, and 97.9% of its mass sits above the prior's own
   upper anchor, so the likelihood is driving it, not the prior.
2. **No prior sensitivity.** Five-fold cross-validation on three alleles under two priors, the
   derived `LENGTHSCALE_REGIONS = 4` anchor and a weak one centred on domain/5 (~3000 km, sd 1.0):

   | Allele | Folds | Derived | Weak |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | DRB1*04:04 | spatial | 0.0279 | 0.0304 |
   | DRB1*12:01 | spatial | 0.0335 | 0.0335 |
   | DRB1*15:01 | spatial | 0.0446 | 0.0447 |
   | DRB1*12:01 | grouped | 0.0184 | 0.0183 |
   | DRB1*15:01 | grouped | 0.0328 | 0.0330 |

   Five of six agree to the fourth decimal. A 20x change in prior median moves nothing.

Also worth recording: `LENGTHSCALE_REGIONS = 4` was chosen partly *because* it excluded
DRB1*12:01 and DRB1*04:04, and its docstring then cites those exclusions as evidence the constant
is right. That is circular, and the >2000 km "degenerate" threshold was never derived from
anything. Neither is load-bearing given the above, but the reasoning should not stay in the file
as written.

## What the data actually shows

Spatial folds, derived prior, against the constant-baseline MAE:

| Allele | Baseline MAE | Model MAE | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| DRB1*04:04 | 0.0278 | 0.0279 | no skill |
| DRB1*12:01 | 0.0320 | 0.0335 | worse than a constant |
| DRB1*15:01 | 0.0591 | 0.0446 | 25% better |

Skill tracks how spatially variable the allele is. The model helps where there is structure and
adds noise where there is not. This reproduces the HbS result (0.0388 vs 0.0391 baseline, #83)
rather than contradicting it: HbS is simply on the flat end of the same spectrum.

**Spatial skill is a property of the variant, not of the pipeline.** We currently publish surfaces
for every variant that converges, which means publishing maps that are demonstrably worse than
reporting one global number.

## Proposal

Add a **cross-validated skill gate** to `surfaces.batch`, alongside the existing structural
`MIN_OBSERVATIONS` floor:

- run k-fold spatial CV per variant during the batch build;
- publish the surface only where it beats the constant baseline by a stated margin;
- where it does not, **refuse the surface and publish the pooled frequency instead**, with the
  measured skill recorded in the exclusion list.

This is the §9 refusal principle applied one level up: a map that cannot beat a constant is not a
finding, and emitting no surface is a valid output. It also makes `EXCLUSION_REASONS` carry a
scientific reason rather than only structural ones.

Open questions for whoever takes this:

1. What margin? Requiring any improvement at all will pass noise; requiring too much discards
   genuinely weak but real structure.
2. CV cost. Five folds per variant multiplies batch build time by ~5 across 767 alleles. Cheaper
   options are a reduced fold count for screening, or gating only variants near the threshold.
3. Does the gate belong in `batch` or in `artifacts.publish`? Publishing is where immutability is
   enforced, which is an argument for putting the check there.
