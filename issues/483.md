## Body

## TL;DR

Choosing **Cesium World Terrain** under *Map → Choose basemap and terrain* looks the same as **Smooth globe**. Zooming in shows no mountains. The terrain does load. Three things in how the Atlas draws the globe hide it, and one of them also hides the map data in highlands.

## Evidence (live site, 2026-10-18, about 02:20 UTC)

**The terrain loads:**
- The UI switch replaces the terrain provider with Cesium terrain, with vertex normals and water mask.
- Every Cesium ion request returns 200, and tiles refine to level 11.
- Cesium reports the ground height at Everest as 8,770 m.
- No warning is shown, and the ion token is present in the build.

**Why nothing seems to change:**
1. **Shading follows the clock.** Relief is shaded only while `globe.enableLighting` is on. The Atlas ties that to the day/night setting (`atlas/scene/atlas-scene.ts:416`), and the light is the real-time sun. On the night side, or with day/night off, the ground is lit evenly and looks flat.
2. **No 3D view.** The default camera looks straight down (pitch −90, `atlas/scene/scene-policy.ts`), and scroll-zoom keeps it there: −89.8° at 17 km. Ctrl-drag or middle-drag tilts the view, but nothing in the UI says so.
3. **Terrain buries the data.** The data layers sit at fixed heights above sea level with depth testing on: the surface at 650 m, cell edges at 1,050 m, country borders at 1,800 m (`geometry/surface-buffers.ts:39`, `geometry/edge-buffers.ts:23`, `scene/context-overlay.ts:70`). Wherever the ground is higher, the data is cut away. In the Ethiopian highlands and the Himalaya the surface disappears.

## Expected

- With World Terrain on, relief is clearly visible when zoomed in, whatever the time of day.
- The map data stays readable and follows the ground.

## Plan (decided by the owner)

- **Hillshade lighting while terrain is on:** a fixed light from the north-west, independent of the clock. Day/night still applies to the smooth globe.
- **Drape the data onto terrain:** the data surface, cell edges and borders follow the ground instead of sitting at fixed heights.
- **A vertical-exaggeration control (`scene.verticalExaggeration`),** following Cesium's [3D Tiles vertical exaggeration sandcastle](https://sandcastle.cesium.com/?id=3d-tiles-vertical-exaggeration), kept separate from the existing data-elevation control.
- **Smaller fixes:**
  - surface terrain tile-load errors as a warning;
  - remove the duplicate terrain request at scene creation;
  - investigate a report that the zoom buttons can take the camera underground.
