## Why this candidate

α-thalassaemia has unusually strong regional spatial-genetic evidence. Than et al. assembled a Southeast Asian geodatabase and fitted 1 km Thailand surfaces with uncertainty for three clinically meaningful allelic classes: α⁰, α⁺, and non-deletional α-thalassaemia.

Primary starting point:

- Than et al., *Estimating the burden of α-thalassaemia in Thailand using a comprehensive prevalence database for Southeast Asia*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6533055/
- The article exposes figure source-data spreadsheets for observed α⁰, α⁺, and non-deletional allele frequencies and documents the inclusion/exclusion protocol.
- Figure/source-data landing page: https://elifesciences.org/articles/40580/figures

## Scientific objective

Assess whether the underlying regional observations can support an honest Thailand-first Atlas dataset while preserving exact variants separately from pooled clinical allelic classes.

## Expected output and acceptance evidence

- A normalized inventory of the 88 α⁰, 37 α⁺, and 42 non-deletional model inputs reported by the study, with duplicates and cross-border inputs identified.
- Exact mutation/deletion names and allele counts where available; otherwise an explicit `allelic_class` representation that does not masquerade as a named variant.
- Coordinate precision, sample sizes, diagnostic panels, survey years, sampling design, and ascertainment.
- Reproduction of example observed frequencies and the published Thailand input counts.
- A recommendation covering both mutation-specific observation layers and class-level regional surfaces.

## Engineering interface and downstream consumers

Any adapter must emit distinct immutable P1 records for exact variants and pooled allelic classes. P2 surfaces must be scoped to Thailand and identify neighboring-country observations used to control border effects. The P5 dataset description must say “Thailand” rather than implying global coverage.

## Suggested starting steps

1. Download the three eLife source-data spreadsheets and appendix/supplementary files.
2. Trace each record back to its cited survey to recover numerator/denominator, diagnostic scope, location semantics, and ascertainment.
3. Build a controlled vocabulary for α⁰, α⁺, and non-deletional classes before mapping exact mutations into them.
4. Record which mutations each assay tested; an untested mutation is not an observed absence.
5. Separate point-localized surveys from records available only at national level—the paper marks the latter with black stars and excludes them from continuous Thailand models.
6. Verify the eLife data license and the terms of each underlying source where necessary.

## Refusal conditions and pitfalls

- Do not collapse all α-thalassaemia variants into one allele frequency.
- Do not convert national-only records into country-centroid observations.
- Do not interpret “not reported” or “not tested” as zero.
- Do not extrapolate the Thailand surface across Southeast Asia.
- Do not derive disease burden until genotype combinations, cis/trans configuration, consanguinity, and phenotype definitions have a reviewed P3 contract.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Source spreadsheets and cited surveys are inventoried.
- [ ] Exact variants versus allelic classes are represented explicitly.
- [ ] Required P1 fields and geographic uncertainty are recoverable.
- [ ] Published observation counts and sample frequencies are reproduced.
- [ ] Regional scope and refusal boundaries are documented.
- [ ] A follow-up adapter issue is opened only if the source qualifies.
- [ ] #180 is updated with the decision.
