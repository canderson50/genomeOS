## Why this candidate

HFE p.Cys282Tyr (C282Y; rs1800562) is a common pathogenic/risk allele for hereditary haemochromatosis with a strong northwestern European geographic cline. A recent genotype-based study maps expected homozygote frequencies across 29 British and Irish regions, but that evidence is currently too coarse to claim HbS-like spatial parity.

Primary starting point:

- Atkins et al., *The landscape of hereditary haemochromatosis risk and diagnosis across the British Isles and Ireland*: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65511-7
- The article's supplementary data, UK Biobank allele-frequency inputs, and Irish regional sources.
- Historical European HFE frequency studies should be treated as leads; retain only representative population samples with recoverable counts and defensible locations.

## Scientific objective

Determine whether sufficiently fine, representative C282Y observations can be recovered to support a regional P1 layer, while distinguishing allele frequency, expected homozygote frequency, biochemical penetrance, and diagnosed haemochromatosis.

## Expected output and acceptance evidence

- Region-level C282Y allele counts/frequencies, denominators, sampling design, coordinate/geometry support, and source provenance for the British/Irish study.
- A structured search for finer European population studies with direct genotyping and subnational locations.
- Reproduction of selected regional allele and expected-homozygote frequencies.
- A conclusion choosing among: P1 regional polygon observations, context-only administrative summaries, or refusal pending finer data.
- A clinical note explaining low/incomplete penetrance and why homozygosity is risk rather than diagnosis.

## Engineering interface and downstream consumers

If accepted, the existing administrative regions should remain polygon-supported P1 observations rather than artificial centroid points. A continuous P2 surface should be attempted only if finer local inputs and meaningful holdout validation exist. P5 must label allele frequency and expected homozygote frequency separately; P3 requires an independently reviewed penetrance model.

## Suggested starting steps

1. Download the article supplements and identify which values derive from UK Biobank WGS, Irish cohorts, or other sources.
2. Recover allele counts and denominators—not just squared allele-frequency estimates.
3. Preserve the 29 source regions as polygons and record their geographic vintage/provenance.
4. Search cited primary studies for finer subregional C282Y surveys across Europe, checking whether samples represent residents rather than referred patients.
5. Evaluate p.His63Asp separately; do not merge it with C282Y or compound-heterozygote estimates.
6. Verify UK Biobank and source-specific reuse restrictions before serving derived observations.

## Refusal conditions and pitfalls

- Do not treat expected C282Y homozygotes as diagnosed cases.
- Do not treat low-penetrance risk genotype frequency as disease prevalence.
- Do not assign administrative-region values to precise centroids.
- Do not fit a smooth Europe-wide surface from country or large-region averages.
- Do not combine C282Y, H63D, and compound heterozygosity into a single allele layer.

## Definition of done

- [ ] British/Irish source tables, counts, geometry, and terms are audited.

- [ ] Published regional values are reproduced.
