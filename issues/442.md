## Simulation-based calibration of the surface model (`fit_surface`)

### Trigger

B2, the current spatial GP, is the strongest completed spatial comparator in #189, but its uncertainty is miscalibrated on real held-out HbS. Its 50% and 80% intervals cover **61.6%** and **87.3%** of the 994 matched held-out rows, outside the ±3 percentage-point gate. Only the 95% interval (96.8%) passes.

The project can't yet say *why*. The causes call for different fixes:

- **the inference is wrong**, given the model: the inducing-point/HSGP approximation, sampler settings or mixing (#111). That's a computational fix;
- **the model is wrong**, given the data: priors, the link (#103), cohort or overdispersion structure. That's a modelling decision.

Simulation-based calibration (SBC; Talts et al. 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788) separates the two. Fit data simulated from the model's own prior. If the inference is correct, the rank of each true value among its posterior draws is uniform. If `fit_surface` is calibrated on its own simulations and B2 still over-covers on HbS, the cause is how the model fits reality. If it isn't calibrated even on its own simulations, the cause is the computation.

### Scientific contract

1. **Claim.** Under its own generative model, `fit_surface` with the production `FitConfig` returns calibrated posteriors for its parameters and for the latent surface at held-out locations. Or, if it doesn't, the study identifies which quantities are miscalibrated and in which direction.
2. **Measurable output and acceptance evidence.** Rank statistics and uniformity diagnostics (rank histograms or ECDF-difference bands, with a pre-registered test) for:
   - `lengthscale`, `amplitude`, `intercept`, `cohort_sd` and `concentration`, plus `beta_design` and `nugget_sd` when the configuration enables them;
   - the latent logit `f` at pre-registered held-out query cells;
   - empirical 50/80/95% coverage of held-out frequencies under simulation, for direct comparison with B2's real-data coverage.

   Every non-converged fit is reported, never dropped (§12). Acceptance thresholds are fixed before any results are seen.
3. **Component and public interface.**
   - A pure research module under `genomeos/validation/` with three parts: simulate from the prior on a fixed geometry, fit with the unchanged `fit_surface`, compute ranks.
   - It reuses `sbc_ranks.py` and the `heterogeneity_sbc_*` evidence patterns (#211, PRs #216–#224) where they apply.
   - A thin script under `scripts/` runs a seeded campaign and writes an immutable report.
   - No serving-path change, no change to `fit_surface`, and no new model version.
4. **Assumptions, refusal conditions and consumers.**
   - **Synthetic data only.** No real observations or private data, and nothing derived from indigenous-population panels (#66).
   - **Prior draws must be usable.** The model's priors must be proper and must give simulations in a usable frequency range. If they don't, that is itself a finding to report, not something to fix by quietly changing priors.
   - **Refusals:** a changed `FitConfig`, a fit whose diagnostics can't be retained, or a campaign that can't reach its planned simulation count.
   - **Consumers:** the #189 coverage diagnosis, the `prior_dominated` mask and resolution thresholds (#39), and the inducing-point sizing question in #111.

### Decisions needed before implementation (`wants-expert-review`)

A spatial statistician should settle these before code is written:

- **Simulation geometry.** Purely synthetic sites, or the public spatial structure (locations only) of the HbS survey layout? How many sites, cohorts and denominators?
- **Which quantities to rank.** For `f`: which held-out cells, and how many?
- **Simulation count and pass criteria.** For example, a few hundred to 1,000 simulations, the uniformity test, and the multiplicity handling.
- **Sampler budget.** Use the production `draws`/`tune`/`chains`, or a documented SBC-specific thinning? Ranks need roughly independent draws.
- **Configuration under test.** At least the production defaults (`approximation="hsgp"`, `inducing_placement="h3"`, `n_inducing=200`, `likelihood="beta_binomial"`). Should B2's exact benchmark configuration also be tested?

### Compute

- **Scale:** independent fits. At the ~15–60 s per fit on record (#104), a 200–1,000-simulation campaign is about 1–16 compute-hours, run as parallel processes. It needs no private data.
- **Not batched, by design.** The point is to test the exact production inference path. A separate batched GPU reimplementation would validate the reimplementation instead.
- **Batching follow-up.** If SBC becomes routine, or the curated-set fits need throughput (#36, #40), add batched fitting inside `fit_surface` as a production feature. It should come with its own batched-versus-unbatched agreement tests, and SBC then inherits the speed-up.

### Out of scope

- Real-data validation and the golden tests. HbS parity remains the definition of done.
- Any change to priors, the link or the approximation. SBC findings feed those decisions; they don't make them.
- Promotion of any model.

### What Stage 0 found

**Pilot.** One A40. The published HbS surface config (`approximation="inducing"`, `inducing_placement="h3"`, `n_inducing=150`) ran 4 chains × 1,200 draws after 1,200 tune. The geometry was 672 sites and 293 cohorts.

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Fits converged (R-hat ≤ 1.05, ESS ≥ 200) | **9 / 9** |
| Wall time per fit | 414–997 s, median ≈ 8 min |
| Max R-hat / min bulk ESS | 1.018 (amplitude) / 404 |
| Fits with divergences | 4 / 9 (4, 19, 2, 2 divergences) |
| Slowest-mixing parameter | usually `cohort_sd` / `beta_cohort` |
| Amplitude ranks (of 100) | 100, 44, 15, 34, 97, 8, 71, 0, 44 |
| Total cost | 89 min of pod time, ≈ $0.88 |

So the full study is **feasible** on this hardware. With 9 fits the ranks are only a hint, but amplitude landed at an extreme rank in 4 of 9 fits. That is worth watching in the full run.

**Prior predictive check.** 1,000 draws from the prior on the same geometry:

| Site allele frequency | Prior | Observed MAP |
|---|---|---|
| median | 2.4 % | 3.0 % |
| q95 | 39 % | 14 % |
| q99 | 74 % | 20 % |
| fraction zero | 23.1 % | 22.3 % |

- Near the data, the prior is plausible but has a heavier upper tail than the observations.
- **Far from the data, the prior reverts to the intercept.** 48% of the globe lies more than 2,000 km from any site. There the prior frequency has median 2.9% (q25 1.06%, q95 22.6%). In half of the prior worlds, more than 99.7% of the globe sits above 1%.

This is a statement about the prior, not the posterior. It may still bear on the ~1% non-endemic floor seen in #45 (question 5 below).

### Caveats

- **Geometry:** the pilot used today's public MAP export, keeping the 672 rows with complete HbAA/HbAS/HbSS counts. That is not the curated 994-row input behind #45 and B2 (#189). The file hash is recorded in the `pilot.jsonl` header, and it differs from the parity run's raw input.
- **Ranks:** pilot ranks come from 100 evenly thinned draws, not ESS-based thinning.
- **Sample size:** with n = 9 nothing here is a uniformity test.

### Proposed pre-registered defaults

Comment, edit or veto any of these. Nothing runs until they are agreed.

1. **Configuration under test:** the published HbS config (`inducing` / `h3` / 150 inducing points), with priors and gates exactly as in `fit_surface`.
2. **Geometry:**
   - primary: the public MAP survey locations, with the file hash recorded;
   - secondary: one purely synthetic layout, to separate the model from the site pattern.

   If the curated 994-row geometry can be shared, it replaces the public one as primary.
3. **Quantities ranked:**
   - the five scalars (`lengthscale`, `amplitude`, `intercept`, `cohort_sd`, `concentration`);
   - latent *f* at ~50 seeded held-out H3 cells, stratified near / mid / far from sites (bands fixed before the run);
   - empirical coverage of the 50/80/95% intervals at those cells.
4. **Thinning:** ESS-based, down to ~100 approximately independent draws per fit.
5. **Uniformity test:** ECDF-difference plots with simultaneous 95% bands (Säilynoja et al. 2022), with Holm correction across quantities. Rank histograms are shown but are not the test.
6. **Divergences:** recorded per fit. Ranks are reported both with and without divergent fits, and the choice of which one is primary is fixed now.
7. **Non-converged fits:** reported and counted, never dropped silently. If more than 5% of fits fail the gates, the study is reported as inconclusive.
8. **Seeds:** `SEED = 42`. Simulation *i* uses `default_rng([42, i])`.

### Decisions needed before a full run (@bschilder, plus a statistician if available)

1. **Which configuration?** Choose from:

2. **Pass criteria.** Is "every quantity inside its simultaneous 95% band after Holm" the bar? Or should coverage at held-out cells have its own tolerance, like the ±3 pt gate in #189?

   | Simulations | GPU-hours | Cost |

   Cheaper options are fewer posterior draws, or running in batches across months.
4. **Amplitude.** Should it be a named primary quantity, given the pilot's extreme ranks?
5. **Far-field prior.** Should the study explicitly test whether the far-field reversion to the intercept explains the non-endemic floor in #45? Or should that be a separate issue?
