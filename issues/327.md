## Body

## TL;DR

A Brazilian dataset of 1,171 whole genomes carries an explicit open licence, publishes real
population allele frequencies, and can be downloaded after filling in a name and institution. South
America is one of our thinnest regions, so this is worth doing.

One thing must not be got wrong: the whole-genome and whole-exome sets share 574 of the same people,
and their frequencies must never be added together.

---

## What it is

ABraOM, drawn from the SABE cohort — a census-based study of elderly residents of São Paulo. 1,171
whole genomes plus 609 exomes, on GRCh38, roughly 77 million variants.

## Why it is worth having

This is a genuine observation layer rather than variant context: real frequencies from a defined
population that can be attached to a place. Its ancestry composition is roughly 73% European,
17% African, 7% Native American and 3% East Asian, with a separately identified non-admixed
Japanese-ancestry subset — an admixed South American population we currently cannot represent at
all.

## Terms — surface both halves

From [the terms page](https://abraom.ib.usp.br/terms.php): the data are "available under the ODC
Open Database License (ODbL)" and "All cohort allelic frequency data is released under a Fort
Lauderdale Agreement." A separate "academic use only" string also appears.

These are not in conflict but they are not the same thing, and we should record both rather than
just the open one. The Fort Lauderdale terms gate **burden and phenotype-correlation analyses**
toward collaboration; they do not gate basic access to frequencies. Since burden rendering is
something this project does, that distinction matters later even though it does not block ingestion
now.

Under our commercial-use marking, this needs a declaration with both findings recorded and the
terms URL cited.

## The overlap trap

The site states it explicitly, and it should be encoded in the adapter rather than left to a
reader's care: **574 individuals appear in both the whole-genome and whole-exome sets.** Summing
counts across the two would silently double-count more than half the exome cohort.

Treat them as two sources with an overlap declaration, or ingest the whole-genome set alone. Do not
add them.

## Geography, honestly

A single point: São Paulo. No district or neighbourhood breakdown exists, so this is one
well-founded coordinate rather than a within-city surface. Scored G3. The uncertainty radius should
reflect a metropolitan area of twenty million people, not a city-hall pin.

## What this needs

1. A source adapter producing counts with denominators from the aggregate frequency release.
2. A reviewed coordinate and uncertainty radius for the São Paulo metropolitan area.
3. Ascertainment fields read from the SABE cohort design. It is a census-based elderly cohort, which
   is a real and unusual sampling design — record it, do not default it.
4. An explicit overlap declaration, or a documented decision to use the whole-genome set only.

Assessed in #3, section H.
