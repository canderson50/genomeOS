## Classification

APOL1 G1/G2 are **incompletely penetrant kidney-disease risk alleles with environment-dependent effects**, not deterministic Mendelian disease alleles. The Atlas must not display allele frequency as CKD prevalence or penetrance.

## Why this candidate

Cooper et al. assembled published genotype data for 40 populations (5,287 individuals) in 21 countries and produced spatial frequency maps for APOL1 G1 and G2 across sub-Saharan Africa. It is a valuable named-variant candidate, but its spatial evidence is much thinner than HbS/HbC and the published kriging surface does not establish equivalent uncertainty support.

Primary starting point:

- Cooper et al., *APOL1 renal risk variants have contrasting resistance and susceptibility associations with African trypanosomiasis*: https://elifesciences.org/articles/25461
- Figure and source-data page, including the population-frequency table: https://elifesciences.org/articles/25461/figures
- G1 is a haplotype defined by two coding substitutions; G2 is a six-base-pair deletion. Preserve haplotype/genotype definitions instead of treating every tag SNP as interchangeable.

## Scientific objective

Assess whether the 40 source populations provide schema-complete, sufficiently localized observations for a measured G1/G2 layer, and determine whether any inferred surface should be refused or heavily support-masked.

## Expected output and acceptance evidence

- A normalized population table containing exact G1/G2 definitions, allele/genotype counts, denominators, coordinates, coordinate provenance, and sampling design.
- Identification of frequencies inferred from a proxy SNP rather than the full G1 haplotype.
- Reproduction of the paper's per-population frequencies.
- A spatial-support analysis documenting distances to observations and areas where a P2 model must refuse prediction.
- Clinical language reviewed to distinguish one versus two risk alleles, disease association, incomplete penetrance, and environmental/second-hit dependence.

## Engineering interface and downstream consumers

P1 should store measured G1 and G2 observations separately, plus an explicit derivation flag for proxy-based estimates. P2 must use support masking and publish uncertainty; the paper's interpolated image cannot be ingested as an observation layer. Any P3 kidney-disease interpretation requires a separate penetrance contract and cannot be derived from frequency alone.

## Suggested starting steps

1. Download Figure 2 source data and recover the underlying references for all 40 populations.
2. Reconstruct allele numerators/denominators and verify whether locations are precise sampling sites or approximate population locations.
3. Audit each G1 value for full-haplotype versus rs73885319-only inference, as flagged by the source.
4. Compare the spatial density to the current P2 support/masking rules before attempting a surface.
5. Search for newer African genomic cohorts that can strengthen these exact variants without substituting ancestry-bin frequencies.
6. Review access, governance, and redistribution terms source by source.

## Refusal conditions and pitfalls

- Do not equate APOL1 high-risk genotype frequency with CKD prevalence.
- Do not merge G1 and G2 allele frequencies into “APOL1 risk” without a stated genotype model.
- Do not silently substitute a tag SNP for the complete G1 haplotype.
- Do not extrapolate a continent-wide surface from 40 populations without explicit uncertainty and support masking.
- Do not convert broad cohort ancestry labels into observation coordinates.

## Definition of done

- [ ] All 40 published populations and underlying references are audited.
- [ ] Exact/proxy variant definitions and counts are explicit.
- [ ] Geographic precision and sampling design satisfy P1 or are documented as blockers.
- [ ] Published frequencies are reproduced.
- [ ] A surface-support/refusal recommendation is documented.
- [ ] Clinical interpretation receives expert review.
- [ ] #180 is updated with the decision.
