## Body

§9 writes the two inheritance modes differently:

```
# autosomal recessive
affected_freq  = p² + F·p(1-p)
affected_count = affected_freq × births_in_cell × penetrance

# autosomal dominant
affected_freq  = (1-(1-p)²) × penetrance
```

So `affected_freq` means **genotype frequency** for recessive conditions and a **penetrance-adjusted frequency** for dominant ones. The final counts come out the same either way, so this is invisible until you look at the intermediate.

It stops being invisible in §6, which exposes `affected_freq` as a selectable map metric:

> `metric` (`carrier_count` \| `affected_count` \| `carrier_freq` \| `affected_freq`)

A user toggling to the `affected_freq` layer and comparing a recessive variant against a dominant one would be comparing two different quantities, with nothing on screen to say so. For a project whose premise is that every number is legible and provenanced, that is a real defect rather than a notational quibble.

## What the implementation does

`genomeos/burden/expressions.py` (#41) applies penetrance once, at the affected stage, for every mode:

```
affected_freq  = P(genotype) × penetrance
affected_count = affected_freq × births_in_cell
```

Recessive final counts are unchanged from §9. Only the intermediate is made consistent, and `affected_freq` now means the same thing on every layer.

## What needs deciding

Whether "affected" should include penetrance at all. Two defensible readings:

1. **`affected_freq` = penetrance-adjusted** (what is implemented). "Affected" means manifesting the condition, which is what a health service plans for.
2. **`affected_freq` = genotype frequency**, with penetrance applied only at the count stage. Keeps the layer purely genetic and puts the clinical assumption in one place.

Either is fine; they must not be mixed. §9 should be amended to say which, and §6 should record the choice per artifact the way it already records `beta_design_applied`.

`wants-expert-review` — a clinical geneticist's view on which reading is more useful to the people who would act on the map.
