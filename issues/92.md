## Body

Extracting the golden test 1 target (#90) surfaced two properties of the published numbers that change how §8 must be scored. Both are arithmetic, not modelling — but getting either wrong would make the test meaningless in opposite directions.

## 1. The published intervals are IQRs, so §8's second criterion is currently near-free

§8 says:

> our credible interval overlaps the published interval for **≥95%** of them

The appendix's footnote 5 defines its intervals as *"IQR: interquartile range of the posterior predictive distribution"* — a **50% credible interval**. Our surfaces carry `q025`/`q975`, a **95%** interval.

Overlapping a 95% interval with a 50% interval is close to automatic: our interval would have to miss the middle half of their posterior entirely to fail. Scored that way, the criterion passes almost regardless of model quality — which makes it a decoration rather than a test.

**Fix:** compare like for like. The burden artifacts should carry `q25`/`q75` alongside `q025`/`q975`, and §8's overlap criterion should be scored on the IQR. §6's `burden` schema lists only `mean`, `q025`, `q975`, so this is a schema addition, not just a test change.

## 2. Medians do not sum, so §8's third criterion needs our global posterior

The 191 national medians in Web Table 1 total **5,239,477 AS** and **288,814 SS**. The paper's global figures are **5,476,000 AS** (IQR 5,291,000–5,679,000) and **312,000 SS** (294,000–330,000).

The sums are 4% and 7% lower, and the summed SS falls *below* the published IQR. This is not a transcription error — the extraction was verified complete, 191 of 191 candidate rows parsed. It is what happens when you add right-skewed per-country medians: the global figure comes from the global posterior, not from a sum.

**Fix:** §8's *"global totals agree within the published uncertainty"* must compare **our global posterior** against theirs. If we compute our global total by summing our own national medians, we will reproduce this same ~5% shortfall and read it as a model failure.

`tests/test_piel2013_reference.py` pins the gap deliberately, with a note that a future extraction which made the sums *match* would mean the table had been misread rather than that the transcription improved.

## Why this is `wants-expert-review`

Point 1 is a judgement about how strict the acceptance bar should be. Loosening the comparison to 95%-vs-IQR would let a poor model pass; tightening it to IQR-vs-IQR may be stricter than the spec's authors intended. Someone should decide deliberately rather than inheriting whichever is easier to implement.

Related: #45, #90, #85 (other spec amendments).

**Both halves are now implemented in the scorer (#95), but this issue's actual question — how strict the bar should be — was decided by implementation rather than deliberately. Flagging that for confirmation.**

`genomeos/validation/hbs_parity.py`:

- **IQR-vs-IQR.** `REQUIRED_COLUMNS` demands `iqr_lower`/`iqr_upper`, and the module docstring makes it a documented precondition that the caller passes an IQR, not a 95% interval. This is the **stricter** of the two options — the one that cannot let a poor model pass — but it is not checkable at runtime, so it rests on the docstring.
- **Medians don't sum.** `score_parity` takes `global_estimate` from our *global posterior* and never sums national medians. Omitting it **fails** criterion 3 rather than skipping it, on the grounds that an unmeasured criterion is not a met one.

This issue says *"Someone should decide deliberately rather than inheriting whichever is easier to implement."* I picked the strict reading because it fails safe, but that is exactly the inheritance this issue warns against. **Leaving open for a deliberate call**; if IQR-vs-IQR is confirmed as the intended bar, this can close and the docstring becomes the record.
