## Body

## What

The merged `tests/fixtures/literature/lct-enattah-2007/` slice — **12 evidence rows, 228 field-evidence rows, 37 search-manifest rows** — has no committed builder. The fixture is a hand-maintained artifact with no auditable account of how it was produced.

This came out of reviewing #188, which *does* ship a builder. Deciding that a committed builder is the standard makes this slice the weaker artifact, so it should be brought up rather than left as the precedent.

## The gap, precisely

#156's own commit message ([`01b9345`](https://github.com/bschilder/genomeOS/commit/01b9345cd032490892409fbb008b69102524d22c)) records that the identifiers were machine-generated:

> regenerate all 12 source_record_ids + 228 child field rows via the `make_source_record_id` helper.
> Regenerate searches.tsv search_ids with the deterministic `make_search_id` helper

So a generation process existed and was run twice during review. It was never committed. Nothing in the repo can now reproduce those 228 rows or re-derive those identifiers, and no raw PubMed ESearch payloads for the 3 queries were committed either — so the 37-row manifest cannot be replayed or re-screened.

## Note: the pattern already exists here, one level up

`scripts/audit_lct_pilot.py` is the model, and it is better than what #188 currently does. It takes every path through `argparse`, and `docs/audits/lct-rs4988235-pilot.json` records its exact invocation:

```
"command": ".venv/bin/python scripts/audit_lct_pilot.py --input ... --expected-rows 426
            --extracted-at ... --ingest-version lct-rs4988235@2026-09-05.1 ..."
```

That is the 426-row **pilot**, though, not the 12-record Enattah **corpus slice**. The two are different artifacts and only the pilot has this treatment. (Worth noting the pilot's own input was `/tmp/lct_rs4988235_observations.csv`, which is also not committed — a smaller version of the same problem, trackable separately if you want it.)

## What to build

Follow whatever #188 settles on, so the two corpora share one shape rather than inventing a second:

1. **Commit the raw ESearch payloads** for the 3 queries under the corpus fixture directory, so the manifest can be replayed and the screening decisions re-audited against what PubMed actually returned.
2. **Replay by default, `--refresh` to re-fetch.** No network on a normal run.
3. **Pin `executed_at` and `extracted_at`** from the committed payloads, not the wall clock — `make_search_id` hashes `executed_at`, so a live clock re-mints every search id.
4. **`argparse` for all paths**, matching `audit_lct_pilot.py` and `build_observations.py`.
5. **Drive the 19 field rows off `TRACKED_FIELDS`** so the per-record row set cannot drift from the contract.

## Acceptance evidence

- Running the builder in replay mode reproduces the committed `evidence.tsv`, `field_evidence.tsv` and `searches.tsv` **byte-for-byte**, and a test asserts it.
- Every one of the 12 `source_record_id`s and 3 `search_id`s in the committed fixture is reproduced by the builder, not merely revalidated.
- No published identity changes. This is a provenance backfill, so **any id that moves means the backfill is wrong**, not that the fixture was. Treat a changed id as a failure to investigate rather than a diff to accept.
- The existing 7 corpus tests still pass unchanged, and keep asserting the science independently of the builder.

## Explicitly out of scope

Do not generalise the LCT and HBB builders into one shared module as part of this. Get the second one committed first; AGENTS.md is explicit that abstractions wait for two real consumers, and the shared shape will be obvious once both exist. A follow-up can merge them.

## Why it is worth doing rather than grandfathering

The project's stated standard is that every number traces to the measurement behind it. For this slice the trace currently stops at a TSV nobody can regenerate — the numbers were verified against Enattah 2007 during review, but that verification lives in review comments rather than in anything executable. A replayable builder plus committed payloads is what makes the audit repeatable by someone who was not in that review.

## Blocker found: the LCT search payloads were never saved, so `searches.tsv` cannot be backfilled

Checked before starting. The `lct-enattah-2007` fixture directory holds only the three TSVs — there is no `payloads/` directory, and no ESearch response for that corpus exists anywhere in the repo or its history. `docs/audits/lct-rs4988235-pilot.json` is the **pilot** audit report, a different artifact, and carries no search payload.

That makes one of this issue's acceptance criteria unachievable:

> Running the builder in replay mode reproduces the committed `evidence.tsv`, `field_evidence.tsv` and `searches.tsv` **byte-for-byte**

The first two are fine. `searches.tsv` is not, and cannot be made so:

- **Replay is impossible** — the raw responses that produced its 37 rows are gone.
- **Re-fetching is not a backfill.** PubMed is a live, growing corpus, so a fetch today returns a different candidate set. And `make_search_id` hashes `executed_at`, so any re-run mints new search ids. That would *change* a published manifest rather than reproduce it, which is the opposite of the goal and would breach the immutability the issue exists to protect.

## Revised scope

Backfill a builder for the **two evidence ledgers only**:

- 12 records and 228 field rows, derived from Enattah 2007 Table 3 genotype counts through the allowlisted derivations. These are deterministic given the transcribed counts, so byte-for-byte replay is achievable and should be asserted by a test, exactly as #188 now does.
- Take `--extracted-at` as a required argument so the identifiers are stable, and drive the 19 field rows off `TRACKED_FIELDS`.
- **Any change to one of the 12 `source_record_id`s means the backfill is wrong**, not that the fixture was. That criterion stands and is the main risk to watch.

Leave `searches.tsv` as authored data, and say so in the corpus audit doc rather than leaving a reader to assume it is reproducible. An honest note that the payloads predate the practice is worth more than a builder that pretends to regenerate them — which is precisely how #188's `PROVENANCE.json` handles the same awkwardness.

## Worth recording as the reason the rule exists

This is the concrete cost of not committing payloads, and it is unrecoverable rather than merely inconvenient. #239 now makes committing them part of the discovery step in `docs/literature-evidence-curation.md`. That rule is not hygiene; it is what keeps a manifest auditable by someone who was not present when it ran. This corpus is the counterexample, permanently.

I am leaving the issue open with the reduced scope rather than closing it, since the ledger backfill is still worth doing and is now well specified.

## What this turned up along the way

Committing the payloads exposed a defect in data that had already passed review and merged: **the original Q3 capture was silently truncated.** The committed payload holds 58 candidates; the merged manifest holds 25; those 25 are exactly the first 25 in order — a `retmax=25` prefix. 33 candidates were never screened.

And the inference from that is the useful part: `fetch_pubmed_manifest.py` fetches with `retmax=100000` and refuses a `count` that disagrees with the returned `idlist`, so the merged file **could not have come from the documented tool**. That is the bespoke-fetcher problem #238 and #239 exist to prevent, caught retrospectively by its fingerprint.

Worth recording as the argument for the payload rule: without the raw responses there is nothing to compare a manifest against, and a truncated capture is indistinguishable from a complete one.
