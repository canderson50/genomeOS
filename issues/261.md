## Problem and scientific objective

Before reusing the CLUES2 simulation examples for #189 WP7, reconcile the inspected
script's endpoint selection with the declared mutation-origin/horizon and frequency
ascertainment. The objective is a temporal reference experiment whose generated
histories follow its stated sampling model. This concerns reproducibility of a
candidate method; it does not show that the published CLUES2 estimates are wrong.

At pinned author commit `b20dc5df6b8e6c93a1cfaf2ea6d0f09d04f4c52b`,
[PlotA.sh](https://github.com/avaughn271/CLUES2/blob/b20dc5df6b8e6c93a1cfaf2ea6d0f09d04f4c52b/SimulationStudy/AncientGenotypes/PlotA.sh)
calls
[WrightFisherFreqMax.R](https://github.com/avaughn271/CLUES2/blob/b20dc5df6b8e6c93a1cfaf2ea6d0f09d04f4c52b/SimulationStudy/AncientGenotypes/WrightFisherFreqMax.R#L11-L31)
with minimum present frequency 0.05; the script also requires frequency <0.995.
The loop allocates `timeofmutation` vector slots. If the frequency filter fails at
the original endpoint while the allele remains segregating, the loop continues.
R extends the vector on subsequent assignments, and a later first-passing frequency
can become the returned endpoint.

The [published supplement](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC11321360.1/msae156_supplementary_data.pdf),
§6.1, describes sampling an origin and rejecting trajectories that are lost or
fixed before the present. It does not describe this later first-passing endpoint
rule. A version-to-paper attribution audit is still required; the current source
revision has not been established as the one used for the article's figures.

## Reproduction and observed result

A local deterministic R4.5.3 replay isolated the inspected update/index/acceptance
operations. No author shell campaign, random trajectories or genotype data were
run or generated. For an initial vector length of 3, denominator 100, and the
inspected frequency bounds, supply successive counts 2, 3, 4 and 10:

| Update | Frequency | Vector length | Endpoint test reached | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 0.02 | 3 | no | no |
| 2 | 0.03 | 3 | yes | no |
| 3 | 0.04 | 4 | yes | no |
| 4 | 0.10 | 5 | yes | yes |

An on-time control accepted at length 3. Loss and fixation controls refused.
Under a fixed-horizon rule with that frequency filter, the length-3 endpoint
would be rejected instead of extended. The difference changes the acceptance
mechanism; its effect on selection estimates has not been measured.

Preserved local evidence includes pinned source/blob hashes, the native-R replay,
all four control outputs, and an independent source/diagnostic review. Public
reporting should distinguish this code behavior from historical publication claims.

## Acceptance evidence and engineering contract

- Identify the exact source/caller, time indexing, origin distribution and
  frequency ascertainment intended for the WP7 reproduction.
- Specify whether rejection occurs at a fixed endpoint or uses a stopping time.
  Do not silently change one into the other or describe a changed experiment as
  a literal reproduction.
- The future offline simulator must expose these choices through its reviewed
  experiment contract and retain the original horizon and accepted endpoint as
  auditable outputs. A method-dependent denominator cannot be treated as a
  harmless constant merely because numerical transition checks pass.
- Verify fixed-horizon acceptance, failed endpoint, later threshold crossing,
  loss, fixation and time-index alignment with independent controls before
  using the generated histories to judge temporal-model calibration.
- Assess quantitative impact only in a separately specified experiment, with
  matched ascertainment and versions. No new global-model result is implied here.

This advances #189 WP7 and Atlas design §§7–8,12. It does not alter or restart
the active B0H calibration, admit ancient data, or block the current modern-model
comparison. Expert review should check the ascertainment interpretation and
source-version attribution before a reproduction is accepted.
