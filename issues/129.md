## Body

Filed for the record, not for immediate work.

`surfaces.fit` uses a stationary Matern kernel: **one `lengthscale` describing every part of the
globe**. That is an independent modelling assumption from the identifiability question in #122, and
it is very likely wrong.

The sampling itself makes the case. In the AFND corpus, European populations are densely sampled
and genetically comparatively homogeneous; sub-Saharan African populations are sparsely sampled and
far more diverse. Allele frequencies plausibly decorrelate over a few hundred kilometres in one and
a few thousand in the other. A single range describing both is a compromise between two different
truths, and it will be pulled toward whichever region contributes more observations — which is a
sampling artefact, not geography.

The same applies to the MAP layers: HbS has sharp gradients across West Africa and near-flat
structure across Eurasia, and one range serves both.

## Why not now

Non-stationary Gaussian processes are substantially harder — deformation, process convolution, or a
spatially varying range are all considerably more parameters over data that already cannot identify
one range (Zhang 2004; see #122). Adding flexibility to a model whose existing flexibility is
unidentified is the wrong order of operations.

**#122 first.** If a joint prior on (range, variance) does not stabilise the stationary model, a
non-stationary one will not be more stable.

## What it would change if adopted

- §6 artifacts would need a per-cell range, or at least a per-region one, since "the" correlation
  range would no longer be a single number in the manifest.
- The support mask uses `2 * rho` to decide what counts as "within reach of data" — with a varying
  range, that threshold varies too, and the mask becomes tighter in densely sampled regions and
  looser in sparse ones. That is arguably more honest than the current behaviour and is worth
  noting as a benefit rather than a complication.
