@bschilder a follow-up on question 2 (which comparison next). I read Piel 2013's Web Appendix 1, and it points at a specific candidate.

### What Piel did about the floor (pp. 10–14; Web Table 5)

Piel hit the same symptom. In the appendix's words, the few zero observations at high and low latitudes were not enough "to rule out the possibility that an occasional area of high allele frequency occurs", so predicted values there were "surprisingly high". Their model differs from the canonical run in four ways:

| | Piel 2013 | Canonical run |
|---|---|---|
| Overdispersion | binomial likelihood plus an independent normal term per location on the latent scale (V ≈ 0.45) | beta-binomial (concentration ≈ 46) |
| Background | prior constrained so that P(S(x) > 0.001) ≤ 1e-3 before data | intercept ~ N(−3.5, 1.5), unconstrained |
| Covariance | exponential, range 0.199 Earth radii (~1,270 km) | Matérn-5/2, ~715 km |
| Link | expit of a fitted cubic | expit |

Two observations:
- The cubic link was rejected in #103 for not lowering the floor. In Piel it addressed the long right tail of SS, not the background. The background was handled by the prior constraint.
- #83's binomial arm failed to mix (R-hat 2.61), but it had no per-location term. Piel's structure already exists in the fitter: `FitConfig(likelihood="binomial", nugget=True)`, added for #137 and off by default. As far as the issue record shows, it has never been run on the HbS parity data.

### An exploratory check (decides nothing)

On the 994 parity surveys, without a spatial fit ([script](https://github.com/canderson50/genomeOS/blob/claude/issue-45-overdispersion-preflight/scripts/preflight_hbs_overdispersion.py), [report](https://github.com/canderson50/genomeOS/blob/claude/issue-45-overdispersion-preflight/docs/research/hbs-overdispersion-preflight-2026-10-19.json); the input is refused unless its sha256 matches):

- **Mechanism.** At matched local means, one zero survey of 2,000 alleles favours a 0.05% background over the 0.78% floor by **3.6×** under the canonical beta-binomial, **135×** under Piel's per-location term (V = 0.45; 21× to 1,300× for V from 1.0 to 0.2), and 2.3 million× under a plain binomial. That matches #103's diagnosis of why the China survey (AC 0 / AN 2,000) barely moves the surface.
- **Fit.** Fitted non-spatially, the beta-binomial describes the spread of survey frequencies better: 130 log-likelihood units at equal parameter count, and 0.15 nats per survey on held-out data (cohort-macro). The logit-normal is worse in all 5 folds, for both grouped and spatial folds, zero and positive surveys alike.
- **Limit.** A non-spatial fit pushes all geography into the dispersion term. Under the spatial model only the residual spread is left, and which family describes that better needs the spatial fit.
- **Self-checks** in the report: agreement with `scipy.stats.betabinom`, the zero-variance binomial limit, and brute-force integration, all to about 1e-8.

### Proposed comparison 1 (for your OK)

- **Change, the only one:** `likelihood="binomial", nugget=True` in place of the beta-binomial, i.e. a binomial likelihood plus the existing per-survey logit-scale term (`nugget_sd ~ HalfNormal(1)`). Everything else as in the canonical run: the 994 surveys, cohort and design terms, 150 H3 inducing points, Matérn-5/2, priors, seed 42, posterior budget, 2010-aligned denominators.
- **One difference from Piel, kept deliberately:** genomeOS's nugget enters the likelihood but not the prediction path, so the published surface stays the smooth field. Piel's S(x) = g(f(x) + ε(x)) carries ε into predictions, which raises E[S²] and so the SS burden. The comparison keeps genomeOS's behaviour and reports the difference as a known caveat. If you'd rather match Piel there too, it would be a separate arm.
- **Primary, as #103 specifies:** held-out count log score on the frozen folds the B0/B2 benchmarks use, against the canonical model.
- **Reported, not tuned on:** convergence; the parity table; global and country HbAS/HbSS, including China, Indonesia and Afghanistan; the population-weighted non-endemic background.
- **Advances** only if it converges and does not worsen the cohort-macro held-out score. Otherwise it's recorded as rejected, like the earlier candidates.
- **Code:** none needed. Both settings already exist and are tested; the run only sets them.

At equal correlation: ρ = 0.5 -> Piel 879 km vs genomeOS 745 km; ρ = e⁻¹ -> 1,268 vs 929 km; ρ = 0.05 -> 3,798 vs 1,893 km. Piel's field reaches about twice as far in the tail.

**Already handled in the repo.** The printed cubic is not monotone on ℝ (h′ > 0 only on [−0.032, 0.158]). The p.13 smoothing equation (k+1)/(2n+k+2) conflicts with the uniform-binomial (k+1)/(2n+2). `piel_published_link.py` and the 2026-09-16 flexible-link preflight record both, inverting only the decreasing branch and keeping both equation arms.

## C. Findings that bear on the gate
1. **Criterion 2 cannot be met at current geographic coverage.** The scorer counts all 191 published countries, and refusals count as failures (by design, `hbs_parity.py`). 155 countries are estimated (165 with masked cells propagated), so the most overlap possible is 81.2% (86.4%). ≥95% needs at least 182 countries estimated. Criterion 1 (≥80%) needs 153/191, i.e. 98.7% of the 155 estimated countries. Every likelihood comparison passes or fails criterion 2 on geometry, whatever the model does. The run record lists finer administrative geometry as a "direction". It is a precondition.
2. **The gate requires near-replication of Piel's model.** It compares IQR with IQR, like for like (#92). With two Gaussian posteriors of equal sd s and between-method discrepancy ~N(0,(c·s)²): c=1 -> 50% of points inside, 82% overlap; c=0.5 -> 82% / 99%. Passing 80% / 95% needs c ≲ 0.5. The model has to agree with Piel's to within about half of Piel's own posterior sd in nearly every country. That is a test of emulating Piel (which bears on the policy question about Piel's HbS-specific background prior), not of independent correctness. The run record already calls it a calibration test rather than external validation.
3. **Many targets are near zero.** 67 of 191 countries have a Piel SS point of 0, and 58 have an SS IQR upper bound ≤ 1 neonate. Any non-zero background fails those for HbSS. This explains much of the 29% figure and is the floor problem seen country by country.
4. **The "Piel-comparable" subset is not Piel's input, and the appendix gives no basis for it.** Piel's model used 1,211 datapoints: Web Figure 1, and a validation of 121 held out + 1,090 fitted. It covered 9,032,377 individuals (largest survey 3,212,374). Population estimates were computed from that fit, and the appendix describes no separate population-estimate subset. The run uses 994 surveys and 5,679,565 individuals (82% of datapoints, 63% of individuals; largest 1,285,556), selected by MAP's `population_estimates == YES` flag. The adapter docstring (`map_surveys.py:190`) reads that flag as the rows "used in the 2013 population-estimates", which the appendix does not support. The run record's "repeating the same fit with the exact Piel subset: this run did that" is therefore not established. What the flag means, and why there are 217 fewer points, should be documented before failures are attributed to the model rather than the input.
5. **Comparison 1 is sound in mechanism, but has a scoring hazard.** The benchmark predictive (`predict_new_cohort_parameters` -> `compose_unseen_observations`) draws a new nugget for unseen surveys, so the held-out log score on that path is fair. The older `SurfaceFit.predict_observation` (used by `validation/crossval.py`) has no nugget term and no guard: a nugget fit scored through it gets a binomial predictive that is too narrow and is penalised, with no error raised. Run comparison 1 only through the benchmark path, or make `predict_observation` refuse `nugget=True` (worth filing).
6. **It is unclear whether Piel's burden includes ε, so a "Piel-matched prediction" arm has no definite target.** The comment says Piel's S(x) = g(f(x)+ε(x)) carries ε into predictions, unlike genomeOS, which drops the nugget from the latent surface. Because HbSS ∝ E[p²], ε in prediction would raise SS. The model definition (appendix p.11) does include ε. But the published areal burden procedure (Web Appendix 2, pp.21–22) draws f(zᵢ) from its full conditional and converts with "the inverse‐logit link function". It mentions neither ε nor the cubic h. As written, Piel's national estimates use expit(f). Check Piel's code before treating the prediction-path difference as a known source of the HbSS deficit (India 0.65×, Sudan 0.19×) or adding a matching arm.
7. **The large-polygon radius is an adapter choice, not Piel's.** Piel's area classes are points ≤10 km², wide areas 10–25, small polygons 25–100, and large polygons >100 km² (open-ended). District (admin2) surveys are included as polygons (appendix p.6). The adapter's 500 km² (12.6 km radius) for "large polygon or unknown" applies to 385 of 994 rows (39%) and probably understates spatial support for district-level surveys. The repo already deems area-class radii contract-invalid (`docs/map-spatial-support.md`), but the canonical run used them.
8. **Validation designs are not comparable.** Piel validated on a 10% random hold-out (n = 121; appendix pp.15–16), not spatial or cohort folds. Its calibration results cannot be set beside the genomeOS cohort-macro held-out scores.
9. **Minor.** The sums of Piel's 191 national medians (AS 5,239,477; SS 288,814) are 4.3% / 7.4% below the published global medians. The scorer correctly uses the model's global posterior, not a sum of medians, so this is only a note for anyone sanity-checking by hand.
