## Body

§9 states:

> Births = cell population × national crude birth rate (UN WPP) — **no global gridded birth raster exists**, and this approximation is recorded in `denominator_source`.

The first half is true; the second is narrower than the spec implies. WorldPop's REST catalogue lists a **`births`** product alongside `pop`:

```
births   Births
  bic    Individual countries
```

So gridded births exist **per country** — there is no global *mosaic*, which is what §9's claim is actually about.

## Why the distinction matters

Multiplying a cell's population by a *national* crude birth rate assumes birth rates are spatially uniform within a country. They are not: fertility differs substantially between urban and rural areas, and the gap is largest in exactly the high-fertility, high-HbS countries where burden estimates matter most. Nigeria, the DRC and India all carry large internal fertility gradients.

That approximation therefore biases burden hardest where the numbers are largest — the same shape of problem as #89 and the denominator ambiguity in #91.

## Work

1. Fetch per-country birth rasters for the countries that dominate HbS burden and aggregate them to H3 alongside population, reusing `genomeos/geo/population.py`.
2. Fall back to population × national CBR only where no birth raster exists, and record **which** was used per cell — `denominator_source` already distinguishes them (`...+cbr` marks the approximation).
3. Quantify the difference on a few high-burden countries. If it is small, §9's approximation is vindicated and we can say so with a number instead of an assumption; if it is large, the raster should be preferred wherever available.
4. Amend §9 either way, since it currently states something stronger than the evidence supports.

Note the interaction with #45: Piel et al. used national crude birth rates, so for *parity scoring specifically* matching their method may be more appropriate than improving on it. Improving the denominator could move us away from their numbers for a good reason and look like a failure. Worth settling before #45 is scored.
