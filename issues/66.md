## Body

HGDP, SGDP, AADR and AFND are the data that make the map possible and are precisely what the CARE Principles govern. We need a stated position on whether *fitted surfaces* derived from them are redistributable as a standalone dataset. **Blocks going public.**

Spec §14.1.

## Comments (2 of 2)

### bschilder, 2026-09-07T04:45:52Z (https://github.com/genomeOS/genomeOS/issues/66#issuecomment-5565166083)

Decision recorded by @bschilder:

GenomeOS may publish and redistribute fitted surfaces derived from HGDP, SGDP, AADR, and AFND. The CARE Principles remain part of our provenance and responsible-use framework, but they are not by themselves a publication veto or a substitute for source-specific terms. We will preserve source attribution and biocultural notices, identify model-derived outputs as estimates rather than observations, and honor any explicit redistribution restriction. When no explicit restriction is found, lack of a clearly stated license will not by itself block publication of a valuable derived surface.

CARE means Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics. Authoritative guidance: https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples and https://doi.org/10.5334/dsj-2020-043

This resolves the human decision requested in this issue and unblocks publication.

## A worked example of the governance model this issue is trying to define

While assessing suggested datasets in #3, I read the National Centre for Indigenous Genomics
governance framework in full. It is the most concrete answer I have seen to the question this issue
asks, and it is worth studying whatever we decide.

**What it actually requires of a secondary user:**

- Access is by written Access Agreement, reviewed by a Collection Access Committee, with the
  **final decision taken by an Indigenous-majority Board** rather than by staff or an automatic
  process.
- On publication: *"Publication of material relating to research participants is directed by their
  and their community's wishes."* And: *"Research findings that relate to a community must be
  discussed with the community... The NCIG Board must be satisfied that the community's response
  has been properly addressed before a manuscript can be published."*
- NCIG must appear as a corporate or group author on any resulting output.

**The part most relevant to us:** the framework is **silent on derived products**. It governs access
to material — samples, documents, data — and predates the idea of publishing a derived surface built
from that material. So it grants no standing permission for anything atlas-shaped. A frequency
surface would have to go through the Access Agreement and community-discussion process from scratch,
as a new thing, not inherit permission from data access.

That silence is the interesting finding. It suggests the honest answer to this issue is not a policy
we write, but a question we have to put to each community whose data we would use — which is closer
to what CARE actually asks than any blanket rule we could adopt.

**Three other Oceanian panels were suggested alongside it** — Choin et al. 2021 Pacific, Malaspinas
et al. 2016, and Bergström et al. 2017 for Papua New Guinea. All three route through European
Genome-phenome Archive controlled access, restricted to population-history research. Publishing a
derived frequency surface is not what those terms authorise, independently of anything this issue
decides. Details in #3, section H.

**One is a false lead worth recording** so nobody chases it: two of these cannot support a frequency
estimate at all. The Papuan arm of Malaspinas is 25 genomes, which gives no usable uncertainty, and
Bergström is SNP array data rather than sequence. Their value is population structure and sampling
geography, not frequencies — so for our purposes the governance question about them is close to
moot.

Also relevant here: the **Peruvian Genome Project** recruited from named Native communities —
Matzes, Uros, Chopccas, Q'eros, Nahuas, Matsiguenka among others — with genuine two-tier community
and individual consent, but no explicit CARE language was found in either of its papers. It is a
case where the consent was carefully done and the redistribution question is still open, which is
exactly the gap this issue exists to close.
