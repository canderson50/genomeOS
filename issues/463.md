## Epigenomics and gene expression: scored assessment and integration proposal

**Summary:** genomeOS maps *where* a variant is common. Epigenomic and expression data tell us *what the variant does*: which genes it switches up or down, in which tissues, and through which regulatory elements. This issue scores the candidate data sources the way #3 does, and proposes a narrow v1 slice that respects the existing invariants and the P9/P10 deferral in `docs/overview.md`.

### Why it matters

- **Most disease-associated variants are non-coding.** They act by changing regulatory elements (enhancers, promoters, chromatin accessibility) and, through them, gene expression. A frequency map of such a variant says where it is common, but not why anyone should care.
- **Expression and epigenomic QTLs connect a variant to a gene and a tissue.** An eQTL (expression quantitative trait locus) says "this allele raises gene X in tissue Y". That is the missing link between the Atlas (variants × places) and the deferred Map 2 (the genome in the cell).
- **Regulatory effects can differ between populations**, through linkage disequilibrium, genetic background and environment. A few multi-ancestry resources (MAGE, Geuvadis, AFGR, AIDA) measure this directly at 1000 Genomes sampling sites. These are the only epigenomic data with any geographic anchor.
- **Map 2 (Chromosome and Chromatin modes, P9/P10)** will need these sources as its data backbone. Inventorying them now avoids a cold start later.

### How it would integrate

The same invariants apply as everywhere else (AGENTS.md):

1. **v1 slice: functional context in the variant panel.** This is the same pattern as #193 (AlphaGenome).
   - For each mapped variant (HbS first, then the curated set from #32), show **measured** eQTL/sQTL effects (GTEx, eQTL Catalogue, MAGE) and **overlapping regulatory elements** (ENCODE candidate cis-regulatory elements).
   - Label them as measured functional evidence, kept separate from measured frequency, from clinical significance, and from **predicted** effects (AlphaGenome).
   - Precomputed offline into versioned artifacts, with no inference at serving time.
   - Context only: never used to decide source eligibility, curation or penetrance.
2. **Population-resolved regulatory effects, as observations rather than surfaces.** Where a resource reports eQTL effects or expression by 1000 Genomes population, show them as measured points with their population and sample size. **No fitted geographic surface of expression or methylation.** Both are dominated by tissue, age, cell culture and environment, so a smoothed map of them would map sampling and exposure rather than biology. That is the misuse risk §3 of the overview warns about.
3. **Later (P9/P10): Chromatin mode.** ENCODE, Roadmap, IHEC, 4DN and FANTOM become the reference tracks for the in-cell view. Out of scope for v1. Listed here so ingestion contracts are designed once.

**Refusals and assumptions**
- No tissue is assumed for a variant without evidence.
- Cell-line data (LCLs, lymphoblastoid cell lines) are labelled as cell lines, never as a tissue.
- Ancestry labels are recorded as their sources state them and are never converted into coordinates. Only 1000 Genomes sampling sites, through the P0 registry, can place a population.
- Each source needs a `commercial_use` block. Unchecked terms stay `not_checked`, as `docs/non-commercial-data.md` requires.

---

Scored assessment. **Four axes, 1–5 each.** C, A and G are as in #3. **L** is new because it is how these data connect to the Atlas:

- **C — Comprehensiveness**: samples × tissues or cell types × assays.
- **A — Accessibility**: 5 = open bulk download, no auth; 3 = browser/API only or registration; 1 = proprietary or controlled.
- **G — Geographic precision**: 5 = per-individual coordinates; 4 = population sampling coordinates; 3 = subnational region; 2 = country; **1 = continental ancestry label or none (unmappable)**.
- **L — Variant link**: 5 = per-variant QTL statistics tied to genotypes; 3 = genomic-interval annotation a variant can be overlapped with; 1 = no genotype link (expression or epigenome only).

> **The core data problem here mirrors #3, only sharper:** the deepest functional resources (ENCODE, Roadmap, GEO) have essentially **no geography**, and are dominated by cell lines and European-ancestry donors. The few multi-ancestry expression resources (MAGE, Geuvadis, AFGR, AIDA) are small. Epigenomics will add *meaning* to the Atlas long before it can add *maps*.

*Figures are a first pass from public descriptions, to be verified before ingestion. As in #3, the scores are deliberately arguable; please edit them.*

## Proposed first step (if accepted)

A bounded pilot for **HbS (rs334) and the G6PD variants**:
- pull eQTL/sQTL effects from the eQTL Catalogue and GTEx, and overlapping ENCODE cCREs;
- add any MAGE population-level effects;
- produce one versioned artifact and a review figure.

No UI until the evidence looks useful. No surfaces. No use in eligibility or burden.

## Not proposed

- Geographic surfaces of expression or methylation.
- Any epigenomic input to frequency, penetrance or burden models.
- Chromatin mode itself, which stays deferred under P9/P10.
