## Body

## Product claim

Long Atlas operations should visibly communicate what is happening and, where measurable, how much work remains, without freezing the existing map or fabricating precise percentages.

## Measurable output and acceptance evidence

- Show a compact animated activity panel and progress bar during initial catalog loading, dataset switches, geometry rebuilds, and slow basemap or terrain changes.
- Display immediately on initial load and after a short anti-flicker delay for later operations.
- Report determinate progress only from known byte totals, explicit validation phases, or completed geometry batches; use an indeterminate bar otherwise.
- Yield during batched geometry construction so the activity UI remains responsive.
- Preserve the outgoing map until the incoming rendering is ready.
- Cancel superseded work so rapid changes cannot restore stale data or leave stale progress visible.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion while retaining progress semantics.
- Keep non-conflicting map interactions available; disable only controls that can conflict with the foreground task.
- On failure, replace activity with the existing relevant error/retry path.
- Add unit, integration, accessibility, and end-to-end tests plus a committed reproducible review figure.

## Engineering interface

- Extend the static provider and scene controller with narrow progress callbacks/events.
- Coordinate the current foreground operation in AtlasExplorer and render it through AtlasStatus.
- Instrument fetch, validation, Cesium readiness, swap animation, and chunked surface construction without coupling the provider to React.

## Assumptions and refusal conditions

- Unknown-duration operations remain indeterminate.
- Cached operations may complete before the anti-flicker threshold and show no overlay.
- The loading UI never implies that inferred scientific results are being computed on the serving path.
- Consumers are the Atlas navbar status, activity panel, and automated performance/accessibility tests.
