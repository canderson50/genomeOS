## TL;DR

Egypt has sequenced 1,024 genomes at high depth across 21 of its 27 governorates and written it up,
but has not yet released the data. The accession numbers are listed as pending.

If and when it lands, it would be the best North African resource available to us by a wide margin.
Nothing to do now except check back in a couple of quarters.

---

## What is coming

EGP1K, preprinted 2026-04-06 (doi 10.64898/2026.04.02.715521): 1,024 unrelated individuals, mean
35.9× whole-genome sequencing, 51.3 million variants of which 17.1 million — a third — are absent
from dbSNP. Recruitment spans 21 of 27 governorates.

That would score around C4/G3 and would close a gap nothing else currently touches. What we can get
from Egypt today is a 110-genome reference set, 97 of which are low coverage, pooled to a single
national number with no data licence on the site.

## Why it is not actionable yet

The preprint states that summary statistics and population-level allele frequencies "will be
deposited" at the European Variation Archive and that "accession numbers are pending." I checked the
archive's current release and searched the nucleotide archive for Egyptian human studies: nothing is
deposited. Individual genotypes would route through an Egyptian access committee.

**Check back in roughly two quarters.** If the deposit appears, this moves straight to a
pursue-now assessment.

## One thing to carry into the sovereignty discussion

The six governorates EGP1K does not cover are disproportionately where Egypt's non-Nile-Valley
minority populations live: Aswan, North and South Sinai, the Red Sea governorate and New Valley —
Nubian, Bedouin and Beja communities among them. The gap is not random with respect to ancestry.

Separately, Siwa Oasis **is** covered, but sits in a governorate the analysis buckets into an
"Alexandria region" grouping, which would dissolve any Berber-specific signal into a Delta-coastal
average.

Neither point is a criticism of the study. Both are things that would quietly become our problem if
we ingested the regional breakdown as though it were a neutral partition of Egypt. Relevant to #66
and to how we describe coverage on the map.
