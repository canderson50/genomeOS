## Body

## TL;DR

In a tilted view, **country names from the far side of the globe are drawn on top of the near side**. Over Addis Ababa, tilted 30°, the map is labelled "Sweden", "Norway", "Iceland", "Ukraine" and "Turkey". Over the Himalaya, "Greenland" and "Iceland" sit on the mountains. The labels skip the depth test out to 8,000 km from the camera, and a tilted camera is close enough to the far hemisphere for that.

Found while investigating #483 (World Terrain). It is a separate bug and is **not** fixed there.

## Evidence (live site, 2026-10-18, about 02:30 UTC)

Screenshots were taken during the #483 investigation (Cesium World Terrain, Dark basemap, default layers):

- **Addis Ababa, camera at 17 km, pitch −30°.** The near-side street map carries the European labels "Turkey", "Georgia", "Azerbaijan", "Bulgaria", "Romania", "Serbia", "Hungary", "Slovakia", "Poland", "Belarus", "Ukraine", "Lithuania", "Latvia", "Estonia", "Russian Federation", "Finland", "Sweden", "Norway", "Denmark", "Czech Republic" and "Iceland". It is the same with the fixed hillshade light and without it.
- **Himalaya, camera at 200 km, pitch −30°, 3× vertical exaggeration.** "Greenland" and "Iceland" are drawn on the Nepal–Tibet ranges.

Seen straight down (pitch −90°), the far side is out of range and the bug does not show.

## Why it happens

- `website/src/atlas/scene/context-overlay.ts` (`#addLabels`) creates every country label with `disableDepthTestDistance: countryLabelDepthTestDistance(minLabel)`. No `heightReference` is set, so it is `HeightReference.NONE`, and the labels sit 2.5 km above the ellipsoid.
- `countryLabelDepthTestDistance` (`website/src/atlas/scene/geographic-overlay.ts`) is `min(countryLabelDistanceForScale(minLabel), 8_000_000)`. Cesium skips the depth test for any label closer to the camera than that distance, wherever it is on the globe.
- The 8,000 km cap was meant to hide the far hemisphere ("Overlays visible front-side labels without exposing the far hemisphere", `tests/atlas-scene.test.ts`). That only works from far out: from the home view at 16,500 km every far-side label is more than 8,000 km away. Down at 17–200 km and tilted, far-side countries 4,000–8,000 km away (Europe from Ethiopia) are inside the cap, so they draw through the Earth.
- The labels use `DistanceDisplayCondition` for distance, but nothing checks which hemisphere a label is on.

## Steps to reproduce

1. Open `/app/?lon=38.75&lat=9.03&height=17000&pitch=-30&heading=0` (or any view below a few hundred km, tilted with Ctrl+drag).
2. Keep the Country outlines layer on.
3. Labels of countries on the far side of the Earth appear over the near side.

## Expected

Only labels on the visible hemisphere are drawn. A label is hidden when the Earth is between it and the camera, whatever the camera's height and tilt.

## Possible directions (not decided)

- Hide a label when it is beyond the horizon, using a per-frame horizon test (for example an `EllipsoidalOccluder` check of each label's position against the camera) instead of a fixed distance.
- Or give the depth-test distance a camera-dependent limit: no larger than the distance to the horizon.
- Leave alone whatever keeps labels readable above elevated surface cells: that is why the depth test is skipped near the camera.

Interaction design §5.3 (country overlay); Atlas design §11.
