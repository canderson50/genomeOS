## Body

§7 specifies the standard binomial GLM link:

```
AC_i ~ Binomial(AN_i, expit(f(s_i) + β_design[i] + β_cohort[i]))
```

Fitting that to the real 332 MAP surveys produces a **sickle-cell hotspot across Finland and northern Scandinavia**, where the true allele frequency is approximately zero and there is not one observation within 800 km. Switching the reported summary to the median (#102) roughly halved it — from ~0.25 to ~0.10–0.14 — but did not remove it.

## This is a known failure of this exact model on this exact dataset

Piel et al.'s supplementary appendix describes fitting their 2010 inverse-logit model to this data:

> When we initially attempted to fit Piel et al.'s model to the current dataset and predict, we found that… the best fitting values result in **implausibly long right-hand tails** for the predictive distribution… strikingly high allele frequencies (**greater than 40%**) were predicted with small but practically significant probability (~0.1%). …the long right-hand tail **contained enough mass to skew all of the standard summary statistics**.
>
> To remedy this problem, we attempted several strategies including employing **Stukel's link function** in place of the more standard inverse logit, and modelling as a **skew-Gaussian process** rather than the standard Gaussian. The skew-Gaussian approach indicated that it could solve the problem, but eliminated the crucial conjugate relationship… **Ultimately, we employed an alternative flexible link function**… We were unable to infer its coefficients jointly with the other model parameters in a fully Bayesian manner due to poor MCMC mixing, so we adopted an **empirical fitting approach**.

So the reference implementation we are scored against tried the model §7 specifies, found it inadequate **for this dataset**, and replaced the link. §7 should not specify a link that its own validation target had to abandon.

## Why it matters beyond aesthetics

The mask does not catch it. Those Nordic cells classify as `interpolated`, because the fitted correlation range is 1,410 km and European surveys fall inside 2ρ. **The data-support mask answers "is there data nearby?", never "is this value credible?"** — so a link-function artifact lands inside supported territory and renders as confident signal. §4's central defence against manufactured clines does not cover this failure mode.

## Options

1. **Flexible link**, as Piel used — closest to the reference, and their empirical-Bayes fitting recipe is documented in the appendix.
2. **Stukel's link** — they tried it; the appendix does not say it failed, only that it was among the attempts.
3. **Constrain extrapolation variance instead of the link.** The fitted GP amplitude was 3.32 on the logit scale, which is what lets the field wander far from data. Tightening that prior is cheap but treats the symptom.
4. **Beta-binomial** (#83) addresses overdispersion, which is related but not the same defect.

Also fixed alongside this, and worth noting as a separate contributor: the GP now lives on the **unit sphere** rather than on separately-standardised (lon, lat), which had made the kernel anisotropic in a way driven by survey placement rather than geography.

`wants-expert-review`, `priority:critical` — this gates #45, since golden test 1 cannot pass while the model invents Nordic sickle cell.

Related: #34, #35, #102, #83, #45.

## How it was fixed — not by changing the link

The link is still `expit`. The resolution came through this issue's **option 3, "constrain extrapolation variance instead of the link"**, reached as a consequence of three other changes rather than as a direct attack:

- **Beta-binomial likelihood** (#83) — under the binomial, the only way to absorb survey-to-survey variance was to inflate the spatial field; GP amplitude fell from 11.5 (non-converged) to 1.79. A field that wanders less far from data extrapolates less wildly.
- **The GP moved to the unit sphere**, so the kernel is isotropic in real geography rather than in separately-standardised (lon, lat). This issue already noted this as a contributor.
- **Inducing-point conditioning** (#108) — spacing matched to the correlation range, which is what let ρ be estimated as 604 km rather than absorbed into a degenerate fit.

## What is deliberately not claimed

`expit` remains, so the *mechanism* that produced long right-hand tails still exists in principle. Piel's appendix hit it on a comparable dataset, and a future variant with sparser data could surface it again. Two guards now stand where none did: ρ is small enough that unsupported regions fall outside 2ρ and mask out, and the fit is refused outright if it does not converge (§12).

**If a hotspot ever appears in unsupported territory again, reopen this rather than treating it as new** — the flexible-link and Stukel options above remain the documented next moves.

### bschilder, 2026-09-16T01:43:02Z (https://github.com/genomeOS/genomeOS/issues/103#issuecomment-5690755341)

Primary-method check completed against the open Piel et al. supplementary appendix (PMC3547249, `mmc1.pdf`, Web Appendix 1 pp. 12–13).

The selected link is exactly:

```
h(x) = sum(c_i * x**i, i=0..3)
g(x) = inverse_logit(h(x))
```

Piel estimated `c` outside the spatial MCMC, constrained `h` to be monotone/invertible, and then treated the coefficients as fixed. Their procedure fits a nonspatial normal model to transformed survey frequencies, plots its posterior-predictive CDF against the empirical CDF, and fits the cubic by constrained least squares.

Two reproducibility limits must be explicit before implementation:

1. The fitted coefficient values are not published in the appendix, so we cannot claim to reproduce their link by copying constants.
2. The appendix prints the smoothed survey value as `(k_i + 1) / (2*n_i + k_i + 2)`. For `k_i ~ Binomial(2*n_i, p_i)` with a uniform prior, the standard conjugate posterior mean is `(k_i + 1) / (2*n_i + 2)`. The printed expression and the stated derivation disagree. We must not silently choose one.

Therefore the first reviewable artifact should be a pure coefficient-fitting preflight that labels and compares:

- `piel_printed_2013`: the equation exactly as printed;
- `uniform_binomial_conjugate`: the stated model's standard posterior mean.

It should report monotonicity domain, fitted coefficients, input fingerprints, transformed-frequency distribution, large-zero behavior, and sensitivity. Neither result enters the surface fitter until this ambiguity is reviewed. If the printed formula is confirmed as a typo from another primary source or author code, record that evidence; otherwise retain both as explicit experimental arms.

The appendix uses the HbS survey observations only. It provides no reason to add malaria, EVI, friction, or any other environmental raster to this link experiment.

Primary sources:

- article record/full text: https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC3547249
- supplementary bundle API: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3547249/supplementaryFiles

## Scientific question

Does an explicit absent component improve held-out count prediction after the beta-binomial already represents ordinary sampling zeros and survey overdispersion? An observed zero is never labelled as structural absence: at AC=0 the candidate adds extra-zero mass to the ordinary beta-binomial zero mass.

The same `AN >= 50` subset has 963 observations from 384 cohorts: 200 zero and 763 positive counts. Five folds hold out whole cohorts; split cohorts are a hard error.

## Held-out result

Zero-inflated minus ordinary beta-binomial normalized log score:

- cohort-macro mean: **−0.001328 nats/observation**;
- row-weighted mean: **−0.007840**;
- zero-count rows: **+0.074161**;
- positive-count rows: **−0.029335**;
- folds improved: 4/5, with the remaining fold at **−0.09309** cohort-macro.

Every optimizer start succeeded. The full-data likelihood has a clear optimum near 7.9% extra-zero mass and improves in-sample log likelihood by 6.74, but it raises the conditional mean from 4.36% to 4.75%. Its marginal mean is 4.372%, slightly above the ordinary beta-binomial's 4.356%. Thus it reallocates probability from positive counts to zeros without lowering the aggregate mean.

## Decision

Do not build or run a spatial zero-inflated beta-binomial from this candidate. It fails the predeclared cohort-macro and no-positive-regression gates. This rejects the global probability-mass-transfer mechanism, not every nonstationary model; any later candidate must target low background and localized peaks directly and pass unchanged count gates before burden parity is consulted.
