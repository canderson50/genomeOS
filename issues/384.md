## Body

## TL;DR

The current spatial GP captures broad HbS structure, but leaves too much low-frequency background. Hard local smoothing refuses most surveys, global zero inflation harms positive counts, and the compact positive-basis B1G model nearly doubles zero-count error. Test one narrower mechanism in simulation before another real-data GPU fit: a latent spatial activity probability that is integrated out of the count likelihood and never treats an observed zero as known absence.

## Trigger

The completed B1G comparison in #331 / PR #383 shows the registered compact-positive model is 28% worse than B2 on balanced macro MAE. Its positive-count log score improves slightly, while zero-count log score falls by 0.487 nats per observation and zero-count MAE rises from 0.01177 to 0.02341.

This follows two earlier negative controls in #103: a global extra-zero component reallocates probability toward zeros but harms positive counts, while a hard local smoother refuses nearly all held-out surveys. The remaining hypothesis is spatially varying activity, not a global hurdle and not a post-hoc wider kernel.

## Scientific objective and claim

Test whether a spatially varying latent activity probability can suppress non-endemic background without erasing endemic positive counts.

For survey count `k` of denominator `n`, integrate rather than classify a latent activity state:

```text
P(k = 0) = (1 - q(s)) + q(s) * BetaBinomial(0 | n, p(s), concentration)
P(k > 0) = q(s) * BetaBinomial(k | n, p(s), concentration)
```

`p(s)` is the conditional frequency field and `q(s)` is the probability that the locally active component applies. An observed zero is compatible with either state and is never labelled structural absence. Neither field is interpreted as migration, selection, ancestry, or a measured biological boundary.

The first claim is limited to identifiability and count-predictive behavior under controlled synthetic truth. No real HbS improvement claim is made by the preflight.

## Measurable output and acceptance evidence

Build a pure simulation preflight using the frozen HbS coordinates, denominators, cohort structure, and split geometry but newly generated counts. Include at least:

- a null truth with `q(s)=1`, where the ordinary beta-binomial spatial model is correct;
- a localized-activity truth with a low background and compact positive regions;
- weakly separated and strongly separated activity/frequency cases;
- denominator and cohort-heterogeneity sensitivities;
- deterministic seeds 42, 43, and 44; and
- the same five outer / three inner split protocol when feasible.

The preflight must report marginal count log score, MAE, 50/80/95% coverage, zero/positive strata, recovery of the marginal predictive probability, convergence, and posterior dependence between the gate and conditional-frequency components. It must also measure false support under the `q=1` null.

Stop before a real-data fit unless the candidate:

1. remains competitive with the ordinary model under the null rather than manufacturing a gate;
2. improves held-out marginal count prediction under localized synthetic truth;
3. keeps the registered convergence thresholds with at most one logged doubled-budget retry;
4. does not require interpreting individual zeros as structural absence; and
5. yields stable marginal predictions even if `q(s)` and `p(s)` are not separately interpretable.

If the preflight passes, register the real-data parameterization and priors before inspecting an HbS candidate result. The real comparison must reuse the exact #331 observations, dependencies, geographic folds, 300 km buffer, current B2 baseline, count diagnostics, and zero/positive reporting. B1G outcomes may motivate this mechanism but may not tune its grid or promotion thresholds. Piel burden remains a secondary integration check only.

## Engineering component and public interface

Add pure likelihood and simulation functions under the research validation boundary. They accept explicit arrays/configuration/seeds and return typed predictive draws and diagnostic records. They perform no file, environment, network, HTTP, serving, or UI work.

The likelihood must analytically marginalize the activity state for scoring. Sampling may draw the latent state, but the public predictive contract remains a count distribution. A later offline adapter may serialize immutable simulation and benchmark artifacts using the existing split/report/checkpoint contracts.

## Assumptions, refusals, and downstream consumers

- Do not call `q(s)` prevalence, presence, endemicity, or biological absence without separate evidence.
- Do not initialize or supervise the gate from held-out counts, B1G errors, burden maps, malaria layers, EVI, friction, satellite embeddings, or other downstream/modelled products.
- Do not force zeros into the inactive component or positives into a hand-drawn region.
- Do not add a permissive fallback when the two components are not identifiable; refusal is the result.
- Do not use repeated seeds or posterior draws as independent biological evidence.
- Do not proceed to a real GPU fit merely because in-sample likelihood improves.

This advances #103 and #189. It follows the B1G rejection in #331 and closes none of them. A spatial statistician should review mixture identifiability, the simulation regimes, and whether stable marginal predictions are sufficient when the two latent components remain correlated.

## Identifiability and implementation checkpoint

PR #385 adds the pure marginal count law and simulation boundary before any spatial fit. It does not claim the candidate works.

The implementation now preserves three facts explicitly:

1. At `AC=0`, probability is `(1-q) + q * BetaBinomial(0)`; the zero is never assigned to one latent state.
2. At `AC>0`, probability is `q * BetaBinomial(AC)`.
3. With `AN=1`, swapping `q` and the conditional frequency `p` leaves the complete observed-count law unchanged because both outcomes depend only on `q*p`. The test suite contains this hand-derived exact counterexample. Larger denominators can distinguish some parameter combinations through the positive-count shape, but they do not guarantee useful separation in sparse rare-allele data.

This agrees with the broader warning from non-replicated occupancy/count models: separate latent processes can be formally or practically confounded even when an optimizer or sampler converges. Knape and Korner-Nievergelt show an exact likelihood confounding example and stress that estimability can depend on untestable link/range assumptions: https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12329. A recent spatial zero-inflated beta-binomial model demonstrates that the proposed mixture family can be fitted, but it is an application precedent rather than evidence that the two fields are identifiable for HbS: https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssa/qnaf056.

Consequences for the remaining preflight:

- score and calibrate the marginal count distribution as the primary estimand;
- report posterior `q`/`p` dependence and component recovery under known synthetic truth;
- refuse biological interpretation of either component when synthetic recovery is unstable, even if marginal prediction is stable;
- include `q=1` null simulations so an unnecessary gate cannot be mistaken for discovered geography; and
- keep the real HbS fit blocked until the registered null/localized simulations and spatial-statistics review are complete.

This comment fixes the campaign inputs, computation, and decision rule **before any campaign result is inspected**. The executable implementation is in PR #387 at commit `118226437e584c7f9bdde031baa7c0238b67fcdc`.

### Scientific question

Does a model with a separate spatial probability that the allele is locally active improve held-out allele-frequency prediction enough to justify fitting that extra latent process to real HbS data?

This campaign is simulation evidence only. It is not a real-HbS fit, is not publication-eligible, and cannot itself publish a surface.

### Registered scenarios and tasks

There are 18 scenarios: seeds 42/43/44 across six regimes. The primary regimes are null, localized weak signal, and localized strong signal at the observed denominator scale with no cohort effect. Sensitivity regimes use localized strong signal with 0.25× or 4× denominators, or cohort SD 0.5. Concentration is 40 throughout.

Each scenario runs ordinary and candidate models over five outer folds and five feasible inner plans: 720 content-addressed tasks total (180 outer, 540 inner; 360 per model arm). Every task must finish, converge, and contain the exact registered held-out population IDs. A task may retry a convergence failure once with twice the tuning budget; other failures are terminal.

### Model and sampler settings

- HSGP basis `(6, 6, 6)`, boundary factor 1.5
- log-lengthscale prior `Normal(-2.0, 0.7)` re-derived from each training split
- conditional intercept `Normal(-3.5, 1.5)`, amplitude half-normal scale 1.0
- activity intercept `Normal(2.5, 1.0)`, amplitude half-normal scale 1.0
- concentration half-normal scale 100; cohort SD half-normal scale 0.5
- NumPyro NUTS, 4 chains, 1,000 tuning steps, 500 retained draws, target acceptance 0.9
- convergence requires maximum R-hat ≤ 1.05 and minimum ESS ≥ 200

### Frozen decision rule

The candidate model is eligible to advance to a reviewed real-HbS fit only if **all** checks pass:

- null scenarios: the lower confidence bound for paired log-score improvement is at least -0.01; relative MAE and truth-recovery errors are no more than 5% worse; mean inactive probability is at most 0.10; at most 25% of held-out observations have activity probability below 0.9;
- primary localized weak and strong scenarios: the lower confidence bound for paired log-score improvement is above zero, MAE improves by at least 5%, and marginal truth recovery is nonnegative;
- denominator and cohort-effect sensitivity regimes: mean paired log-score improvement is positive and MAE is no more than 5% worse;
- empirical 50%, 80%, and 95% interval coverage is within 3 percentage points of nominal;
- both true-zero and true-positive held-out strata are represented and pass validation;
- every registered task is present, authenticated, and terminally complete.

The final report also exposes latent-component correlations and both fold-level and observation-level denominators for expert review. Passing this automated gate is necessary but does not replace the requested scientific review of mixture identifiability, priors, regimes, thresholds, and marginal-versus-component stability.

## Final registered preflight result: ineligible for a real HbS fit

The exact 720-task campaign is sealed and reported in PR #397. The spatial activity candidate passes its null safety control but fails the localized mechanism it was designed to solve.

- 720/720 terminal artifacts retained; 704 fits completed successfully.
- 41 fits used the single allowed doubled-budget retry; 16 still failed convergence.
- Primary strong localized truth: MAE **47.3% worse**, marginal-truth recovery **139.9% worse**, log-score change -0.01082 with interval [-0.18033, +0.15870].
- High-denominator sensitivity: MAE **52.2% worse** and recovery **176.9% worse**.
- Cohort-heterogeneity sensitivity: MAE **41.4% worse** and recovery **120.1% worse**.
- In strong conditions, positive-count log score improves by +0.70 to +0.89 nats/observation while zero-count log score worsens by -0.19 to -0.20. The candidate therefore recreates the support tradeoff it was intended to fix.
- Predictive intervals substantially overcover. The low-denominator comparison is unavailable because terminal convergence failures are preserved rather than dropped.

Decision: do not tune or fit this parameterization on real HbS data. B2 remains the strongest completed comparator. A future candidate needs a genuinely different mechanism and must improve zero and positive held-out counts together.

The aggregate report discloses a private finalizer path correction and one accidental task-level display after the manifest, decision rule, aggregate reducer, and renderer were frozen. Neither changed the scientific analysis. All task-owned pods were collected and deleted.
