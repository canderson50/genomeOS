## Body

## Problem

When a surface appears, the hexagons appear all at once. The chunked reveal (fast-load design §B.6.7) already adds chunks nearest-first, but on desktop it finishes in about 0.35 s, so it reads as one pop. Dataset switches are one atomic swap by design.

## Proposal

Reveal the surface as a wave. The hexagons start at the point in the centre of the current view and spread outward in a ring to the horizon, and each hexagon grows from its own centre and fades in as the ring passes.

- **When:** the cold reveal, "Retry globe" and a dataset switch.
  - On a switch, the old surface goes at the commit and the new one grows from the centre. That keeps the rule of never showing two datasets under one legend.
  - Metric, palette and elevation changes stay instant, because they recolour the same data.
- **How, on the GPU only.** The elevated surface already uses the Atlas's own shader (`surface-appearance.ts`).
  - Add uniforms for the wave's origin and its current angular radius, and animate the radius per frame while requesting renders.
  - No geometry rebuild, no extra download and no per-cell buffer writes (see #451 for why those matter).
  - If the vertex buffers don't carry each hexagon's centre, add it and measure the cost.
  - Smooth surface modes get the same wave as a radial fade.
- **Origin:** where the camera's view direction meets the globe, falling back to the point below the camera.
- **Timing:** about 0.8 s to the horizon, as one tunable constant. The far side finishes at once.
  - The wave starts with the first chunk.
  - A hexagon shows once its chunk has arrived and the wave has reached it.
- **Accessibility:** `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` gets today's instant appearance. Hexagons the wave has not reached cannot be hovered or picked.
- **Load milestones keep their meaning.** The wave is cosmetic and never delays data.

## Acceptance

- Captured frames show the wave growing from the view centre to the horizon, on desktop and phone.
- Reduced motion shows no wave.
- E2E tests:
  - early in the wave, a pixel at the view centre is coloured and one near the limb is not;
  - after the wave, both are coloured;
  - existing hover and click tests wait for the wave's end explicitly, with no loosened assertions.
- Unit tests cover the wave timing, the origin, the triggers and reduced motion.
- No new long frames, and the cold-load budgets still pass on the harness.
