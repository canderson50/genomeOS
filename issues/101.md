## Body

§7's four support states — `observed`, `interpolated`, `prior_dominated`, `unknown` — are **entirely about observation proximity**. None of them asks whether the cell contains any people.

The consequence is visible the first time you render a surface: the fitted field is continuous over the plane, so it paints allele frequencies across the Atlantic, the Sahara's empty quarter, and the Antarctic. An allele frequency is a property of a population; there is no population in the ocean.

§9 *does* have the right refusal — "no population denominator exists for the cell" — but it fires at the **burden** stage. A surface layer is published before that and inherits no such check.

## Interim fix

`genomeos/viz/basemap.land_mask()` clips review figures to Natural Earth land polygons, and the surface script marks everything else `unpopulated`. On a 1.5° global grid that is 16,057 of 22,895 cells — **70% of what was previously being coloured in**.

That is a stopgap in the figures layer, not a fix in the model layer.

## What should actually happen

The real test is not "is this land" but "does anyone live here", and #43 now provides exactly that: `genomeos/geo/population.py` aggregates WorldPop to H3 and deliberately distinguishes a cell with valid pixels summing to zero from a cell with no coverage.

So `unpopulated` should become a **fifth support state in `genomeos/surfaces/mask.py`**, derived from the population grid, excluded from aggregation on the same footing as `unknown` and `prior_dominated`, and rendered distinctly — a cell nobody lives in is a different statement from a cell we have no data for, and the map should say which.

That also means §6's `support` enum and §7's four states need amending.

Related: #38, #43, #94.
