## Body

Scored assessment of candidate datasets. **Three axes, 1–5 each** — the third one is the punchline of this whole exercise:

- **C — Comprehensiveness**: variants × individuals × ancestry breadth.
- **A — Accessibility**: 5 = open bulk download, no auth; 3 = browser/API only or registration; 1 = proprietary.
- **G — Geographic precision**: 5 = per-individual coordinates; 4 = population sampling coordinates; 3 = subnational region; 2 = country; **1 = continental ancestry label only (unmappable)**.

> **The core data problem for this project:** the most comprehensive resources have the *worst* geographic precision, and vice versa. gnomAD v4 is C5/A5 and **G1** — 800k people and not one usable coordinate. HGDP is C2 and **G4**. There is no dataset that is both. Every design decision downstream flows from this tension.

**What this national-programme sweep adds:** the same access–geography tradeoff persists within countries. Panels that can be downloaded or queried now generally expose national or broad regional aggregate frequencies. Resources with genuinely fine subnational information usually keep individual-level genotypes or precise locations controlled. Aggregate availability therefore makes a source useful for variant context, model inputs and validation, but does not automatically make it a georeferenced observation layer.

Accessibility and reuse must also be tracked separately. jMorp is the clearest example: its aggregate files can be downloaded without negotiating access, but their terms restrict commercial use and third-party redistribution. Conversely, Turkish Variome provides a CC BY 4.0 bulk aggregate file, Iranome permits free use with citation, and SweGen's access depends on the named release and genome assembly.

**Practical v1 candidates from this group:** Turkish Variome and Iranome can be evaluated immediately for normalized aggregate ingestion. SweGen needs an assembly/release decision. jMorp can inform research now, but serving redistributed or re-edited data requires ToMMo approval.

## D. Direct-to-consumer — the highest geographic precision on Earth, closed

| Dataset | Scale | C | A | G | Notes |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|---|
| **23andMe** | >12M genotyped | 5 | 1 | **5** | Published aggregate work: [Bryc et al. 2015, *AJHG*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25529636/) — 5,269 African American, 8,663 Latino, 148,789 European American, **state-by-state ancestry gradient maps**. That is ancestry proportions, *not* AF-by-location — the thing we need has never been published. **The founder connection is the single highest-value data unlock available to this project.** See "the ask" below. |
| **AncestryDNA** | >20M | 5 | 1 | 5 | [Han et al. 2017, *Nat Commun*](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms14238) — 770k genomes, post-colonial North American structure, IBD clusters mapped geographically. Same shape of asset, same closed status. |
| MyHeritage / Living DNA / Nebula | millions | 4 | 1 | 4 | Living DNA has notably fine-grained British regional reference panels. |

**The 23andMe ask, stated concretely** (so it's a 20-minute conversation, not an open-ended one): *k*-anonymised allele frequencies for a curated variant list (say, ClinVar P/LP + PGx + a few thousand GWAS index SNPs), binned to 3-digit ZIP or county, with counts suppressed below a threshold — **or**, better and easier for them to defend, a *fitted frequency surface* with posterior uncertainty rather than any counts at all. A fitted surface leaks far less, is exactly what our renderer consumes, and sidesteps most re-identification objections. Worth proposing both and letting their privacy team pick.

## H. Suggested additions — assessed 2026-09-16

Thirty datasets [suggested in the thread below](https://github.com/bschilder/genomeOS/issues/3#issuecomment-5697920327), aimed squarely at the coverage gaps this issue admits to at the bottom: East Asia, Latin America, MENA and Oceania. Every one was checked against its primary source rather than its abstract. **The claimed figures were wrong or stale often enough that the checking was the point** — see "What checking changed" below.

**WBBC is the find of this batch.** Its VCF carries `North_AF/AN`, `Central_AF/AN`, `South_AF/AN` and `Lingnan_AF/AN` in the INFO column — regional **denominators**, not just frequencies, which is exactly what an observation layer needs and what almost nothing else here provides. Verified directly:

```
AC=2;AF=0.000223214;AN=8960;NS=4480;North_AF=0.0;North_AN=448;Central_AF=0.0;Central_AN=100;
South_AF=0.000247831;South_AN=8070;Lingnan_AF=0.0;Lingnan_AN=126
```

**But the four regions are wildly unbalanced, and this must not be ingested naively.** South carries AN=8,070 while Central is AN=100 and Lingnan AN=126 — roughly 50 and 63 people. The cohort is overwhelmingly Zhejiang, which is where Westlake is. Ingest all four, then mask or very widely interval Central and Lingnan.

**The GME Variome needs mirroring, not just tracking.** Its host at UC San Diego returns 404, and the Internet Archive captured the download page but never the files behind it. The per-subregion table survives as a single copy last modified in December 2019, reachable with no authentication:

```bash
curl -O http://www.openbioinformatics.org/annovar/download/hg19_gme.txt.gz   # also hg38
```

It is the only open per-subregion frequency table spanning Morocco to Pakistan, and it is one outage from being gone. It also gives something this project rarely gets from an old resource: quotable values for the ascertainment fields the schema refuses to default. The authors state the samples "were not a random sample, they were ascertained within a wide variety of distinct phenotypes" and that they "removed from datasets the allele that brought the family to medical attention."

Two cautions on it. Its regions are **supranational by design** — the authors rejected country borders because "current country borders frequently fail to accurately separate ethnicities" — so it is G2, and drawing those polygons would be our own reviewed-alias decision. And the paper describes six subregions while the released file has seven columns; how `PP` maps onto `CA` and `Israel` is undocumented.

### H4. Indigenous and community-governed panels — governance before scores

Four Oceanian panels were suggested. **[#66](https://github.com/bschilder/genomeOS/issues/66) already forecloses them**, and nothing found changes that: three are EGA-controlled and restricted to population-history research, which is not what publishing a derived frequency surface is.

| Dataset | Scale | C | A | G |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Choin et al. 2021 Pacific | 317 genomes, 20 populations, ~36× | 3 | 2 | 4 |
| **NCIG Indigenous Australian** | 159 genomes, 4 remote communities incl. Tiwi | 3 | 1 | 4 |
| Malaspinas et al. 2016 | 83 Aboriginal Australian + 25 Highland Papuan | 2 | 1 | 4 |
| Bergström et al. 2017 PNG | 381 individuals, 85 language groups — **SNP array, not WGS** | 2 | 2 | 4 |

**NCIG's framework is the strictest and the most instructive.** Read directly: access is by written Access Agreement with the **final decision taken by an Indigenous-majority Board**, not staff. "Publication of material relating to research participants is directed by their and their community's wishes," and "the NCIG Board must be satisfied that the community's response has been properly addressed before a manuscript can be published." NCIG must appear as a corporate author on any output.

Critically, **the framework is silent on derived surfaces** — it predates that use case and governs access to material, not downstream aggregate products. So it grants no standing permission for anything atlas-shaped. That is a template worth studying for #66 rather than a dataset to queue.

Two of these cannot support a frequency estimate regardless of permission. The 25-genome Papuan arm has no usable uncertainty, and Bergström is array data rather than sequence. Their value is population structure and sampling geography.

The **Peruvian Genome Project** belongs in this conversation too. It recruited from named Native communities — Matzes, Uros, Chopccas, Q'eros, Nahuas, Matsiguenka and others — with genuine two-tier community-plus-individual consent, but no explicit CARE language was found in either paper. Its two source papers also report irreconcilable sample counts, 280 sequenced in 2018 against 150 WGS plus 873 array in 2025.

### H5. A time layer, and what it cannot do

**[AmtDB](https://amtdb.org/)** — C2 / **A5** / **G5**, CC BY 4.0, no registration, bulk metadata export. The suggested figure of 1,107 samples is stale: it now holds **3,759** across 52 countries, 14 epochs, 479 cultures and 1,030 sites, each with coordinates and dates.

**It is mitochondrial only, and that is the entire assessment.** Uniparentally inherited, no recombination, so it yields no autosomal allele frequencies and cannot feed the surface pipeline under any circumstance. It is an excellent time-and-place layer beside AADR and nothing else. One caveat before ingestion: its ~479 contributing studies carry their own upstream terms, which would need a per-study pass.

### What checking changed

Worth recording, because the pattern will repeat with the next suggestion list.

- **Announced scale is not released scale.** The Emirati programme's public figures run from 180,000 to 815,000 and conflate samples collected with genomes sequenced. The only number behind a deposited artifact is 43,608.
- **A paper's geography is not the data's geography.** CMDB describes 31 provinces and serves one national number. NyuWa samples 23 of 34 divisions and serves one national number. ChinaMAP's provincial claim could not be confirmed at all because the data sits behind a login.
- **Resources rot.** Four of thirty are dead or dying: the GME Variome's host, FINDbase's query backend, MalariaGEN's summary portal, and Saudi's entire web presence. One survives only as a single unmaintained mirror.
- **"I could not find it" is not "it does not exist."** Two independent checks disagreed on whether the GME mirror and the Emirati deposit existed. Both do; I verified each by fetching bytes. Where reviewers disagree about an artifact, the one who shows the bytes wins.
- **Sample overlap among the Chinese cohorts is an open risk, not a cleared one.** No shared-cohort statement was found across PGG.Han, CMDB, ChinaMAP, WBBC and NyuWa, and they come from visibly distinct institutions — but absence of a statement is not proof of independent recruitment. Double-counting people would corrupt a frequency surface, so this needs a real audit before any of them are merged.
- **Coverage gaps are not random with respect to ancestry.** Egypt's forthcoming 1,024-genome release covers 21 of 27 governorates, and the six it misses are disproportionately where Nubian, Bedouin and Beja communities live. Siwa Oasis is covered but bucketed into an Alexandria-region average that would dissolve any Berber signal.

### Worth watching

**EGP1K** — 1,024 Egyptian genomes at 35.9×, 51.3M variants, across 21 of 27 governorates. Would be C4/G3 and would meaningfully close the North African gap. Accessions at the European Variation Archive are listed as pending and nothing is deposited yet; re-check in about two quarters.


---

## Recommendations

1. **Build on the gnomAD HGDP+1KG harmonized callset** (4,094 genomes / 80 populations / 153M variants, CC0, already on GCP). It is the only open resource that is simultaneously whole-genome and georeferenceable. Everything else layers on.

2. **The binding constraint is coordinates, not variants.** Propose a first-class curated asset: a **population geolocation registry** — every population label from AFND, HGDP, 1KG, SGDP, PGG, GAsP, GenomeIndia and AADR joined to coordinates, with provenance, a sampling-vs-ancestral flag, and an explicit **uncertainty radius**. Nobody has published this. It is the foundation of every map we draw, it is independently publishable, and it is the kind of asset that attracts outside contributors.

3. **Separate observations from surfaces in the data model, permanently.** Layer 1 = georeferenced allele counts with sample sizes (what was measured). Layer 2 = fitted surfaces with posterior uncertainty (what was inferred). The UI must never let a user mistake one for the other — this is the lesson of Novembre & Stephens 2008, and it's cheap to get right at schema-design time and impossible to retrofit.

4. **Use HbS and G6PD as named validation cases, not as preferred sources.** Their measured survey counts and published national estimates provide rare input→output checks with known targets. That specific utility does not give MAP-published datasets general priority: every other MAP asset must pass the same source-neutral utility, leakage, temporal-support, and license screens as any other candidate. If our pipeline cannot reproduce the published HbS neonate estimates, it is not ready to render other allele-frequency surfaces.

5. **Two outreach asks worth making now:** bulk access to PGG.SNV (977 populations, C5 — the biggest single coverage jump available for free), and the 23andMe fitted-surface proposal above.
