## Body

## TL;DR

One of our recorded literature searches says it found 25 papers when the search actually matches 58. Nothing recorded that it had been cut short, so it looked complete. Worth knowing: this did not cost us anything, because none of the papers we use came from that search — they came from searches aimed at authors we already knew. So this is tidying up a misleading record, not fixing broken data. The one genuinely useful piece is an automatic check so a cut-short search cannot look complete again.

---

## What

`tests/fixtures/literature/lct-enattah-2007/searches.tsv` was merged on 2026-09-06 with three
queries. Q1 and Q2 reproduce exactly on re-execution. Q3 does not:

| query | merged candidates | PubMed `count` |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 `Enattah[Author] AND 13910[All Fields]` | 11 | 11 |
| Q2 `Bersaglieri[Author] AND lactase[All Fields]` | 1 | 1 |
| Q3 `rs4988235[All Fields] AND (population[Title/Abstract] OR frequency[Title/Abstract])` | 25 | **58** |

All 25 merged candidates are present in the 58, and they are exactly the set PubMed returns with
`retmax=25`, so the merged capture was truncated at 25 and nothing recorded the cap. That is the
failure mode `docs/literature-evidence-curation.md` names when it explains the `count`-versus-`idlist`
refusal, and `scripts/fetch_pubmed_manifest.py` (retmax=100000, rejects that disagreement) could not
have produced it.

## Why the PR that found it does not fix it

PR #249 backfilled the builder, the committed payloads and a corrected all-pending snapshot. It does
not rewrite the published manifest, because re-deriving a manifest changes `search_id` by
construction: the id hashes `(database, query, executed_at)`. Publishing a manifest whose identifiers
differ from the published ones supersedes a published version, which is a data revision rather than a
backfill.

Consequence of leaving it open: `searches.tsv` (37 rows, `lct-rs4988235@2026-09-06.1`) and
`searches.pending.tsv` (70 rows, `lct-rs4988235@2026-09-12.1`) describe different captures and share
no identifier.

## What needs deciding

1. Supersede `searches.tsv` with a manifest over the corrected snapshot: 70 rows, the two `included`
   decisions carried forward, the 33 newly surfaced candidates `pending` because nothing has screened
   them, and the version and audit record stating plainly that it supersedes
   `lct-rs4988235@2026-09-06.1`.
2. Whether the two `included` decisions carry forward unchanged, or whether the 33 new candidates are
   screened first so the superseding manifest is a complete screening rather than a partial one.
3. Whether something should assert at commit time that a manifest's candidate count agrees with the
   payload it was derived from, so a truncated capture fails there rather than at the next reviewer's
   re-execution.

## Acceptance evidence

- The superseding manifest reconciles per design §8: rows = included + excluded + pending.
- Every payload it derives from satisfies `count == len(idlist)`.
- The audit record states which version it supersedes, and why.

### bschilder, 2026-09-15T21:27:10Z (https://github.com/genomeOS/genomeOS/issues/272#issuecomment-5688325105)

## Rescoped — the screening question is withdrawn

This issue asked three things. Only one survives, and I want to be explicit that I over-weighted the other two.

**Withdrawn: whether to screen the 33 newly surfaced candidates first.** I framed that as a decision about the corpus's coverage claim. It is not, for a reason the corpus itself shows.

Of the three queries, two are `Enattah[Author]` and `Bersaglieri[Author]`. You only write those if you already know whose paper you want — they are retrievals of papers already chosen, not discovery. **Both included papers came from those two.** The one genuine discovery query, the truncated one, included nothing.

So the papers arrived because someone knew them, and the manifest documents a search performed around a conclusion already reached. Losing 33 candidates from that query cost the corpus **zero records**. Screening them is a "find more data" task competing on merit with every other way of finding data, not a correctness fix, and treating it as a blocker was disproportionate.

More fundamentally: these corpora are transcribed measurements, each independently checkable against its own source — 12 records from one paper, 2 from another. A paper nobody found cannot make a record somebody transcribed wrong. That is a coverage gap, not a validity gap. Selection bias in this atlas is controlled by the per-observation ascertainment fields that have no defaults, not by proving a search was exhaustive.

**Withdrawn: whether the two `included` decisions carry forward.** Moot once the above is dropped.

## What remains worth doing

1. **Stop the record being false.** `searches.tsv` states 25 candidates for a query that matched 58, with nothing recording the cap. Correct it, note that it supersedes `lct-rs4988235@2026-09-06.1`, and mark the 33 `pending` — genuinely unscreened, honestly recorded. Not a prerequisite for anything.

2. **Say how each paper actually arrived.** A coverage report reading "44 candidates screened, 1 included" implies systematic discovery. Two of three queries were confirmations. Record "named by a collaborator" or "already known" where that is the truth — it is a better provenance than a query reverse-engineered afterwards, and #291 makes it a first-class route.

3. **Assert at commit time that a manifest's candidate count matches its payload.** The one piece of real machinery here. A silently truncated fetch is a bug, and this catches it at authoring time rather than a week later. Belongs with the mechanical checks in #248 — same class, no model needed, works on fork PRs.

Item 3 is the only one I would prioritise. Items 1 and 2 are tidying that should happen when someone is next in these files, not work to schedule.

Related: #291 removes the "discovery is not optional" language I wrote into the curation doc, `CONTRIBUTING.md` and the PR template — which is what made a search look like a precondition in the first place.
