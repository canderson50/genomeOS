## Classification

This is a **disease-susceptibility/protection locus**, not a classic genetic disease. It must not be grouped or labeled as a Mendelian disorder in the Atlas.

## Why this candidate

The Duffy/ACKR1 study is one of the strongest spatial-genetics resources available: 821 geographically unique community surveys covering 131,187 people, with global 5–10 km allele-frequency surfaces and uncertainty for FY\*A, FY\*B, and FY\*BES. The erythroid-silent FY\*BES promoter allele is strongly relevant to *Plasmodium vivax* susceptibility.

Primary starting point:

- Howes et al., *The global distribution of the Duffy blood group*: https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1265
- The paper's supplementary data and MAP resource description: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3738938/
- The key molecular candidate is the ACKR1 erythroid-silent promoter variant commonly represented as rs2814778, but the source includes mixed genotype, promoter, and serologic data that must remain distinguishable.

## Scientific objective

Determine whether the Duffy survey database can support a provenance-rich P1 susceptibility layer without conflating directly genotyped alleles, inferred blood-group alleles, and the Duffy-negative phenotype.

## Expected output and acceptance evidence

- Recovery and licensing audit of all 821 spatially unique surveys or the authoritative filtered export.
- Preservation of the paper's diagnostic/data-type categories and direct versus inferred allele counts.
- Exact representation of point surveys versus polygon centroids: the paper reports 536 point-geopositioned surveys and 285 polygon centroids and excludes country/province-only surveys.
- Reproduction of selected published allele and phenotype frequencies.
- A reviewed product classification and user-facing description centered on malaria susceptibility/protection, not “Duffy disease.”

## Engineering interface and downstream consumers

P1 should represent FY\*A, FY\*B, and FY\*BES observations independently, with diagnostic method and derivation recorded. A Duffy-negative phenotype layer is derived and must not overwrite allele observations. P2 can fit versioned surfaces; P5 must visibly distinguish allele frequency from predicted phenotype frequency.

## Suggested starting steps

1. Retrieve the supplementary survey database or archived MAP export; do not digitize Figure 3.
2. Study Table 2 and the supplementary methods before designing the adapter because the five diagnostic data types carry different information.
3. Verify how promoter genotypes, serologic phenotypes, and molecular genotypes were converted into the joint allele model.
4. Preserve polygon geometry or an uncertainty radius for centroid-localized observations.
5. Review current terminology with malaria and hematology expertise before writing public copy.
6. Verify source-level reuse terms and whether a Biocultural Notice is required for any included community.

## Refusal conditions and pitfalls

- Do not call Duffy-null a genetic disease.
- Do not treat phenotype-derived and directly genotyped frequencies as equivalent observations.
- Do not conflate P. vivax susceptibility with deterministic infection or clinical outcome.
- Do not replace polygon support with a precise-looking centroid and zero uncertainty.
- Do not ingest indigenous-population-derived artifacts for redistribution until the governance question in #66 is resolved where applicable.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Authoritative survey data and terms are located.
- [ ] Diagnostic modalities and derivations are preserved.
- [ ] Point/polygon geographic support is represented honestly.
- [ ] Published values are reproduced from normalized rows.
- [ ] Clinical/product terminology receives expert review.
- [ ] Follow-up adapter work is scoped only after qualification.
- [ ] #180 is updated with the decision.
