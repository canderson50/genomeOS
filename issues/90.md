## Body

Golden test 1 (#45) has two halves. The **input** half is done — the MAP HbS survey database is downloadable and ingested (332 surveys, 64 countries). The **target** half is not.

§8 scores parity against:

> Piel et al. 2013 national annual AS and SS neonate estimates
>
> - our national point estimate falls inside the published interval for **≥80%** of countries with published estimates, **and**
> - our credible interval overlaps the published interval for **≥95%** of them, **and**
> - global totals agree within the published uncertainty

So we need, per country: the published AS and SS neonate estimates **with their intervals**. Without them there is nothing to score against, and #45 cannot close.

## Where they are

The Lancet supplementary appendix, [`mmc1.pdf`](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/instance/3547249/bin/mmc1.pdf) (1.5 MB), attached to [PMC3547249](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3547249/). The paper states *"Complete subnational estimates for data-rich areas are shown in the appendix pp 39–44"*, with national estimates in the tables.

**PMC serves an anti-bot interstitial for that URL**, so it cannot be fetched by script — it needs a manual download.

## Work

1. Download `mmc1.pdf` by hand and commit it, or note its checksum and fetch path.
2. Extract the national AS/SS neonate table to CSV: `country_iso3`, `as_neonates`, `as_lower`, `as_upper`, `ss_neonates`, `ss_lower`, `ss_upper`, `year`.
3. Check the extraction against the totals quoted in the paper body — a PDF table extraction that silently drops or merges rows is exactly the kind of error that would make parity look worse than it is, and would be blamed on the model.
4. Commit as a small reference dataset with its provenance, since it is the definition of done for v1 and must be citable at a fixed version.

## Note on scope

This is transcription of published numbers, not modelling — but it gates #45, which gates publication of every other variant's burden layer (§8). Marked `priority:critical` for that reason.

Related: #45, #46 (same problem for Howes et al. G6PD).
