## Body

Found while quantifying #123. This is a **placement** fault rather than a weighting one, and it is
the larger of the two.

## 21 distinct populations at a single point

Every NMDP population in the corpus is placed at **(38.8, -76.1)** — the US donor registry's
location in Maryland. They are not geographic populations:

| an | population |
|---|---|
| 2,485,780 | `afnd-usa-nmdp-european-caucasian` |
| 833,162 | `afnd-usa-nmdp-african-american-pop-2` |
| 522,470 | `afnd-usa-nmdp-mexican-or-chicano` |
| 370,782 | `afnd-usa-nmdp-south-asian-indian` |
| 199,344 | `afnd-usa-nmdp-chinese` |
| 155,168 | `afnd-usa-nmdp-korean` |
| 101,228 | `afnd-usa-nmdp-filipino` |
| 87,080 | `afnd-usa-nmdp-vietnamese` |
| ... | 13 more |
| **5,797,500** | **combined, at one point** |

That is **41.2% of the entire corpus's statistical weight, at a single coordinate.**

So the model is told that ~200,000 chromosomes of *Chinese* HLA frequencies were measured in
Maryland, alongside Korean, Vietnamese, Amerindian and Hawaiian frequencies at the identical
location. These are **ancestry strata of one US registry**, not populations sampled where they live.

## What that does to a spatial model

For `HLA-A*02:01`, the 20 NMDP populations reporting at that point span:

```
 0.278  an=   71,582  north-american-amerindian
 0.275  an=2,485,780  european-caucasian
 0.223  an=  522,470  mexican-or-chicano
 ...
 0.049  an=  370,782  south-asian-indian
 0.035  an=   87,080  vietnamese
```

**An 8-fold spread at zero geographic distance**, carrying millions of chromosomes of weight.

No spatial field can reconcile that, because it is not spatial variation. The GP's only recourse is
to flatten and let the likelihood absorb the residual — which is the over-smoothing and inflated
range described in #137. It is very likely that part of what #137 diagnoses as a missing nugget is
**this**: a large apparent nugget is guaranteed when 21 contradictory values sit at distance zero.

It may also explain a result I read as good biology: `A*02:01` peaking in the Americas is real, but
some of that peak is NMDP Amerindian and Mexican/Chicano strata anchored in Maryland, not
Indigenous American sampling.

## A constraint on the available fixes

`radius_km` is **250.0 for every observation in the corpus** — median 250, max 250. It is a
constant, not a per-population value (#21). So #123's "scale weight by disc area" option cannot
currently discriminate between a village and a national donor registry: every disc is the same
size. That option needs #21 first.

## Options

Root question: **what are registry-ancestry populations, for a point-referenced surface?**

1. **Refuse them.** They are not observations of a place. Costs 41% of the corpus's weight, but
   that weight is currently doing harm rather than good.
2. **Keep them as observations of the registry's location.** Defensible if read as "HLA frequencies
   among Maryland donors", with the ancestry label as a stratum — but then the design term, not the
   coordinate, has to carry the ancestry, and 21 strata at one point still fight each other.
3. **Re-place them at the ancestral origin.** Scientifically what you want, but "NMDP Chinese" ->
   China is an *inference*, and §4 forbids an inferred location entering the observations layer as
   though measured.
4. **Keep, but give them a continental `uncertainty_radius_km`** so the disc reflects what the
   label actually covers. Requires #21, and interacts with #37.

`needs-human-decision`. All four imply different maps, and the 20 artifacts published in #140 were
built under option 4-without-the-radius, which is the one option nobody would choose deliberately.

Related: #123, #21, #37, #137, #140, #101.

## Why 91% of the weight moved the surfaces so little

The 35 refused points per allele sit at **exactly two coordinates**, and both are already ringed by
dense clusters of ordinary populations. Removing them from the two best-sampled regions on Earth
leaves the GP leaning on neighbours that were always there.

The difference panels show it directly: **localised bullseyes at Maryland and central Europe**,
near-zero everywhere else. DRB1*01:01 gains +0.024 over the eastern US once the strata are gone
(the registry was pulling it down); A*02:01 loses 0.077 in the northeast, the largest shift in the
figure.

So the fix is **narrower than I claimed when filing this issue**. The eastern US and central Europe
were materially wrong; the rest of the world was already fine. Worth recording, because I filed
this expecting the stratification to be the cause of the inflated ranges and it was not — refitting
five alleles moved ranges 0-20%, all converged at r_hat 1.007-1.016.

It also corrects a suspicion I recorded earlier: **A*02:01's peak in the Americas is unchanged by
the refusal**, so it is genuine Indigenous American signal rather than an NMDP artefact.

## What the figure cannot show

Markers are area-proportional to observed **frequency**, so the registries appear as 35 ordinary
dots. Their dominance lives in `an` — 6,912,132 and 2,485,780 chromosomes — which has no visual
encoding here at all. A weight-scaled map would be a different figure, and would be the one that
explains the 91%.
