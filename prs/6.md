## Body

Implementation plan for the first of three phases, derived from the design spec in #5.

**Plan 1 = P0 + P1.** Nine TDD tasks: scaffold + CI → registry schema → HGDP adapter (establishing the adapter contract) → registry build with alias-collision detection → observations schema with mandatory ascertainment fields → gnomAD HGDP+1KG adapter + registry join → MAP HbS survey adapter → H3 resolution ladder → partitioned store + frozen contract.

**Acceptance signal** is deliberately unglamorous: the pipeline prints `{'healthy_reference': 4, 'population_random': 3}`. Two distinct sampling designs in the corpus is exactly what makes `β_design` identifiable, so it is the gate for Plan 2 starting at all.

Three choices worth reviewing:

1. **The MAP survey adapter is in Plan 1, not Plan 2**, which looks out of place next to the golden tests it feeds. It's here because MAP surveys are the corpus's only `population_random` source, and §7.1's ascertainment correction is identified by *contrast between* designs — without it, Plan 2 cannot start.
2. **One deliberate deviation from spec §6:** `source_labels` is normalised out of a nested `array<struct>` into a `population_aliases` table. One label mapping to two `population_id`s would silently split a population across two map points — the worst silent corruption available in P0 — and a long table makes it a detectable hard error. §6 should be amended to match.
3. **The curated variant set is deliberately excluded**, despite §15 listing it under P1. It needs clinical-genetics review to decide which ClinVar P/LP variants have defensible penetrance, and it gates P2 rather than P1. It sits at the top of Plan 2 alongside the INLA runtime decision.

**Flagged, not decided:** the spec specifies INLA-SPDE and R-INLA is R-only. Containerising R keeps the method and its defensibility to reviewers; reimplementing in PyMC/GPyTorch keeps one language but gives that up. Real cost either way — top of Plan 2.

Also contains the full GitHub Project layout: 56 issues, four release milestones, and three collaborator roles that currently have no owner.

Depends on #5.

## Review requests

Tagging everyone with repo access. These are documents rather than code, so I'm merging them to `main` now to make them citable and linkable — **review continues in comments here**, and anything you want changed lands as a follow-up PR. Nothing is locked in by the merge.

Sections where each of you is the right reader:

**@dwgoblue** — the science, and specifically the part most likely to be wrong:
- **§7.1 (ascertainment correction)** — this is new since the first draft. The claim I'd most like challenged: that recessive carrier-frequency depletion in gnomAD is only *O(p)* and therefore negligible relative to spatial posterior width. If that's wrong, the flagship v1 layer is wrong.
- **§9 (burden expressions)** — HWE with `F = 0` as the shipping default. It will visibly under-predict in specific regions; I'd rather that be your decision than my assumption.
- **Golden tests 2 and 3** (§8) — G6PD exercises X-linked inheritance, and carrier-screening parity is the *only* test that validates §7.1 at all. Are CFTR F508del / SMN1 / HEXA / β-thalassaemia the right four, and do you know better-measured anchors?

**@JirachiWishmaster** — the engineering:
- **§10 (backend)** — DuckDB-on-Cloud-Run for interactive reads, BigQuery for batch. Chosen partly because hg-horizon-web already proves the pattern. Sanity-check the p95 targets (<150ms warm, <500ms cache-cold at res 4).
- **§6 (H3 parquet instead of raster tiles)** — the bet is that lasso aggregation becomes a `WHERE h3_parent IN (...)` predicate pushdown and deck.gl's `H3HexagonLayer` eats the indexes directly, so we carry one geometry stack instead of two. If that's a bad trade, now is the cheap moment to say so.
- **§11 (deck.gl layer stack)** and Plan 1 Tasks 8–9 (resolution ladder, partitioned store) are yours in the issue breakdown.

**@ctbio123** — governance and partnerships, and two of these are on the critical path *before* the repo goes public:
- **§13 (governance)** and **§14.1** — we need a written position on whether fitted surfaces derived from indigenous-population panels (HGDP, SGDP, AADR, AFND) are redistributable as a standalone dataset. This blocks going public, and I'd like your read on how to frame it.
- **§14.3 — PGG.SNV outreach.** 977 populations, 265M SNVs, browser-only access. This is the single largest free coverage gain available to the project; it needs a direct ask to the authors.
- **§14.4 — the 23andMe proposal.** Drafted concretely in #3 so it's a short conversation: *k*-anonymised AF by 3-digit ZIP for a curated variant list, **or** (easier for them to defend) a fitted surface with posteriors and no counts at all.

Also worth your eyes: [Discussion #4](https://github.com/bschilder/genomeOS/discussions/4) (prior art — 20+ tools and papers, and §6 there is the argument for why v1 leads with Mendelian rather than polygenic), and my critiques on [#1](https://github.com/bschilder/genomeOS/discussions/1) / [#2](https://github.com/bschilder/genomeOS/discussions/2).

**Three roles nobody on the roster currently fills**, two of them on the critical path — if any of you know the right person, that's the most valuable thing you can contribute this week:
- **Spatial statistician / geostatistician** (INLA-SPDE) — P2 is the highest-risk sub-project and has no owner. This is the gap most likely to stall the project.
- **Clinical geneticist** — penetrance curation, the curated variant set, and golden test 3 all need someone who can defend a penetrance estimate.
- **Indigenous data governance advisor** — needed before public launch, not after.
