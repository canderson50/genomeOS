## TL;DR

We reviewed four studies that screened newborns for sickle-cell disease. Because of how and where participants were recruited, none can be treated as a representative estimate of how common the variant is in a local population or as an untouched final test set; they remain useful only as development background.

---

The external-validation pool had four title-screened newborn HbS leads but no methods-level qualification. Treating those hospital programs as resident-population allele-frequency observations would invent geography and erase known ascertainment differences.

This change:

- retains the immutable 210-candidate PubMed discovery manifest (all candidates remain `pending`; SHA-256 `0c3d4044d353a869b5f795a7526002b94374b4956b2b72fa2e59af0f95d799f2`);
- qualifies the recruitment, assay, geography, dependency risk, and access terms for Italy, Dar es Salaam, Caluquembe, and Luanda;
- permanently classifies all four as consulted development evidence because outcome summaries became visible during methods retrieval;
- records that none is a point-resident P1 observation or a sealed final confirmation set; and
- makes the covariate rule explicit: MAP, Google Earth Engine, and other environmental products have no privileged status and must earn inclusion through prespecified geography-blocked predictive validation.

The 2023–2024 Luanda program is the strongest aggregate development candidate; Dar es Salaam is next. Caluquembe is a useful rural contrast but small and referral-biased. The Italian data describe two mixed-origin center cohorts, not a national resident frequency.

This advances #189 without closing it. No observation, schema, fitter, surface, burden result, serving path, or environmental covariate changes. No figure is added because this change produces no renderable scientific layer.

Relevant contracts: Atlas design §4 and §5.3; global modeling plan WP0/WP1.
