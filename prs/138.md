## Body

Advances #127 by making its central claim visible rather than a table of counts. Adds
`scripts/plot_fold_strategies.py` and the figure it generates, so it is regenerable and diffable
rather than a screenshot.

![fold strategies](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bschilder/genomeOS/d543baded12b90bd5e77d88784756b6b4d783b80/docs/figures/fold_strategies.png)

Top row: where each fold's surveys are, coloured by fold. The bottom row is the point — how many
folds each multi-site study ends up spanning. **1 is intact; anything above 1 has lost the
within-study replication its cohort term is estimated from.**

| strategy | studies split | intact | shape of the damage |
|---|---|---|---|
| `random` | **135 / 143 (94%)** | 8 | 58 split two ways, 26 three, 27 four, **24 across all five folds** |
| `spatial` | 7 / 143 (5%) | 136 | 6 split two ways, 1 three |
| `grouped` | **0 / 143 (0%)** | 143 | none, by construction |

The **24 studies spread across all five folds** are the sharpest version of the problem: there is
no training split in which those studies retain any replication, so `cohort_sd * cohort_z` and the
beta-binomial `concentration` are describing the same single residual in every fold, in every run.

The maps also show why `grouped` is the right comparison for measuring spatial leakage rather than
`random`: `grouped` looks nearly as scattered as `random` — it leaks neighbours in exactly the same
way — but every study stays one colour. That is what makes the spatial-versus-grouped gap
attributable to autocorrelation alone, which is how #132 arrived at the 37% figure.

Design sections: §7 (surface inference), §12 (convergence gating). Does **not** close #127 — its
third proposal, conditioning rather than marginalising in `predict_observation`, is still
outstanding and now unblocked since #124 landed.
