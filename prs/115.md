## What is here

**`genomeos/geo/countries.py` — country identity and the H3→country join, shared with #61.**
ISO3 is the join key and the country name never is: MAP writes "United Republic of Tanzania",
Piel writes "Tanzania, United Republic of", Natural Earth writes "Tanzania", and a name that
fails to match drops a country out of a total silently, which is indistinguishable from a country
with no burden. `resolve_iso3` raises by name on anything it cannot resolve (§12). Assignment is
point-in-polygon against the already-committed Natural Earth 1:110m geometry with a bounding-box
prefilter, subtracting interior rings so Maseru comes out as LSO rather than ZAF (288k res-4
cells in 0.2 s, so #104's cuSpatial path is not needed yet). `rollup_by_country` aggregates any
per-cell column through `mask.aggregate_cells`, so #61 gets its choropleth rollup from the same
code and masked cells are excluded by the one implementation of that rule.

**`reference/piel2013_national_estimates.csv` gains `iso3`** — all 191 published countries resolve
to 191 distinct codes — and the extractor that produced the file now resolves and checks one per
row. Natural Earth's five `-99` codes are decided explicitly rather than passed through (France
and Norway have real codes; N. Cyprus and Somaliland fold into the state whose published estimate
covers them; Kosovo keeps XKX so it surfaces as unmatched rather than quietly inflating Serbia).

**`genomeos/burden/national.py` — the rollup.**

- **Population-weighted, not a centroid value.** Nigeria, India and the DRC carry most of the
  global burden and have the largest internal variation; a centroid value for India is not a
  number to score parity against.
- **Draw-wise summation.** Cells are summed *within* a draw and quantiles taken across draws.
  Medians do not sum — Piel's own national medians fall 4–7% below their published global
  posterior — so summing our medians would reproduce that shortfall and read as model failure
  (#92). The interval emitted is an IQR for the same like-for-like reason.
- **The global total is our global posterior**, from the same draws, for §8's third criterion —
  never a sum of national medians.
- **`burden.propagate._refusals` is now public as `cell_refusals`**, because the rollup needs
  exactly that policy and a second copy is how a refusal rule stops being enforced in one of the
  two places burden numbers are produced.

## Partial coverage — both methods, compared

- **Coverage is measured by population, not area.** `mapped_population_fraction` is the share of
  a country's denominator in supported cells and is the number that governs; `unmapped_cell_fraction`
  is §10's cell-count share, reported beside it. They disagree hard — a country can be 50%
  unmapped by cells and 91% covered by population — and both directions are pinned by tests.
- **Method A — `national_totals` (the default).** Supported cells only; refusal below
  `MIN_MAPPED_POPULATION_FRACTION = 0.80`, a named constant whose docstring gives the reasoning
  (the emitted total covers the mapped population only, so the threshold bounds the structural
  shortfall against a whole-country published estimate at one fifth) and which is carried on
  every result so a rollup states the bar it was produced under. **No imputation of any kind**:
  masked cells are never filled with a point value, the mapped rate is never assumed to hold over
  the unmapped remainder, and the partial total is never scaled up by the inverse coverage —
  there is a test for the last one specifically, because it is the tempting one.
- **Method B — `national_totals_propagating_masked` (an experiment).** Every populated cell,
  masked ones included, each carrying its full posterior draws through the burden expression with
  the national quantiles taken afterwards. Nothing is imputed and no point value is substituted;
  a masked cell's posterior has barely moved off the prior, so it contributes a wide distribution
  and the interval widens with the unmapped share.
- **The refusal is self-penalising and that is deliberate.** `score_parity` left-joins on every
  published country, so a refused country counts against us; a test asserts a refusal reaches the
  scorer and lowers the score rather than being dropped.

### Method B does not satisfy the §4/§10 invariant as written

Masked cells are inside its aggregation statistic. It is built to be **compared**, not adopted,
and adopting it for published artifacts would need its own decision — **filed as #113** with the
comparison numbers below. In this PR: no default reaches it, the AGENTS.md invariant text is
untouched, the two are separate functions rather than a flag so no call site can get B by
accident (there is a test for that), and the method is recorded on every result and every row
written to disk.

## What I did NOT do, and could not verify

**No real HbS numbers exist in this PR, and there is no way to produce them today.** Two inputs
are missing and only one of them is a download:

1. **WorldPop is not committed** (~870 MB, fetched by `scripts/fetch_worldpop.py`), so no real
   population denominator was used. The synthetic run substitutes each country's *published*
   population spread evenly over its cells — real between countries, uniform within one.
2. **`SurfaceFit` exposes posterior summaries per cell but not the draws**, and a national
   interval cannot be rebuilt from summaries (medians do not sum). So `national_totals` takes
   draws and nothing committed can produce them for a real variant. Filed as **#112**; not fixed
   here because `genomeos/surfaces/fit.py` is being edited concurrently on another branch.

Consequently **golden test 1 has not been run for real**, and the #113 comparison above is
suggestive at best. One property of the stand-in matters for reading it: its per-cell draws are
independent, while a real GP posterior is spatially correlated, so summing averages the
uncertainty away and Method B's characteristic widening is understated rather than flattered.

What *is* covered by tests: every arithmetic property of both methods against hand-computed
draws, the mask and coverage rules, all refusal paths, Method B's widening and its unreachability
from the default path, the ISO3 resolution of all 191 published countries and all 177 Natural
Earth features, and the end-to-end shape through `to_parity_frame` into `score_parity`.

Also not done: area-weighted (rather than centre) cell assignment; a GADM admin-1 rollup (#61 will
want one, and it also fixes French Guiana being inside Natural Earth's France); and the
resolution-promotion interaction, since everything here is at res 4.

## What an expert should check

- **#113 first — whether Method B may exist at all**, and if so whether a wide interval is
  materially different from a refusal to a reader looking at a map.
- **The 0.80 mapped-population threshold.** A starting value chosen to bound the structural
  shortfall at one fifth, not a calibrated one. Should it vary with whether the unmapped part is
  `prior_dominated` rather than `unknown`, and should a refused country carry a bound instead of
  nothing?
- **Comparing a mapped-population total against a whole-country published estimate at all.**
  The threshold bounds the mismatch but does not remove it, and it biases us low in exactly the
  countries with the worst survey coverage — which are not a random sample.
- **The `-99` decisions.** Somaliland → SOM and N. Cyprus → CYP match Piel's national units;
  Kosovo → XKX deliberately does not. French Guiana sits inside Natural Earth's France, so GUF
  gets no estimate although Piel publishes one.
- **The median as the national point estimate** (#102) and the IQR as the interval (#92), both of
  which follow the published table rather than our own convention.
- **Whether the global total should include the cells of countries refused for coverage.** It does
  here, on the argument that the refusal concerns attribution rather than existence.
- **`ANT` for Netherlands Antilles**, dissolved in the reference year itself, and whether the
  published row should be retired rather than kept in the parity denominator.
