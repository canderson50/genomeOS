## Body

## Summary

Part B of the Atlas mobile, fast-load and GCS design: the default HbS view now shows observations
and the surface in seconds, where it used to take 8–33 s or never loaded. The 20 MB surface JSON
leaves the critical path. In its place are content-addressed GOSA binary tiers: one shared grid,
an f32 render tier and an f64 detail tier. A pure Python codec writes them, and a module worker
verifies and decodes them. The worker also builds the chunked mesh, mask, edge and Natural Earth
buffers. The lazily loaded Cesium scene reveals them frame-budgeted on a cold load and swaps them
atomically on every replacement.

**What users get.** The figures are medians from the formal §B.1 harness. "Before" is `5a10b15`
measured with the same harness. "Now" is the range of medians over the quiet-machine invocations
on the code tip (I2, I3, I5–I7 in the table below), with the typical value first.

| Profile | Before: map ready | Now: points visible | Now: surface visible |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop, no throttling | 8.4 s | **~0.4 s** (393–415 ms) | **~1.3 s** (1,333–1,718 ms) |
| Phone, 4× CPU, Fast 4G | 32.1 s | **~3.2 s** (3,174–3,234 ms) | **~5.2 s** (5,125–5,275 ms) |
| Phone, 4× CPU, Slow 4G | never: all 3 runs end in "Atlas request timed out after 15000 ms" | **~11.1 s** (11,108–11,129 ms) | **~16.6 s** (16,582–16,681 ms) |

Before, nothing appeared until the whole map was ready. Now the points come first and the surface
follows chunk by chunk.

| Budget (median of 3) | Old | New | Evidence | Margin |

- **Why the reveal budget moves.**
  - The 11 quiet-machine invocations started and ended below a 1-minute load of 8: I2, I3 and
    I5–I7, plus #2, #4, #9, #10, #13 and #14 in the same-session comparison.
  - In that comparison the tip's pooled median was 932 ms over 18 runs. Over all 33 runs of the 11
    invocations it is 941 ms.
  - The two earlier builds in the same session reached medians of 998 ms (`c3fdef8`) and 946 ms
    (`6187411`), with single runs up to 1,059 and 997 ms.
  - So the 1 s line sat inside run-to-run noise rather than marking a regression.
- **The reveal margin is thin.** 1.1 s is 50 ms above the worst quiet-machine median, and one
  quiet-machine tip run took 1,160 ms. A loaded machine still misses it: I1 measured 1,276 ms.
  The formal run therefore needs a quiet machine.
- **Why the Slow 4G budgets tighten.** §B.1 says they "are tightened in the PR if the measurement
  allows". The rule is the worst quiet-machine median plus at least 0.75 s, rounded up to the next
  0.5 s.
  - The evidence is six quiet-machine Slow 4G invocations on the shipped cold path: I2, I5, I6 and
    I7, plus the two after-side invocations of the R84 A/B (load 4.2–6.3).
  - `observations-visible` medians were 11,059–11,129 ms, and `surface-visible` medians were
    16,417–16,681 ms. Moderate-load I4 measured 11,140 / 16,637 ms.
  - Loaded I1 (11,266 / 17,348 ms) also passes, by 734 and 152 ms. But one of its
    `surface-visible` runs took 18,189 ms, so 17.5 s holds on a quiet machine only.
- **Where it changed.** Commit `65eec38` amends spec §B.1, recording old → new and this evidence.
  It updates the §B.1 Rulings row for Slow 4G and adds a row for the reveal. It changes
  `website/tests/support/cold-load-profiles.ts` and the pin in `atlas-cold-load-analysis.test.ts`,
  and the plan's Global Constraints and spec-coverage rows. The plan's task steps keep the values
  they were written with.
- **The run that judged them.** The harness asserted the old budgets when these runs were made.
  Every verdict below is given against both the original and the amended budgets.

### Spec amendment: one retry flow changes on purpose (§B.7, R30)

§B.7 lists error and retry flows as behaviour that must not change. One of them changes. Before
Part B, a failed data request showed "Retry data", which fetched again and recovered. Part B
decodes every tier in one module worker that the scene and the provider share. If that worker
dies after it starts (its script failed to download, or it threw), it cannot be restarted in the
page, so "Retry data" would fail the same way every time.

- **What the reader sees.** A failure panel: "Map data unavailable", "The map data could not
  load", and "The map data loader stopped, often because the connection dropped. Retrying reloads
  the page; your map, view and layers are kept in the link. No scientific data was changed."
  Its Retry button reloads the page.
- **History.** Final-review fix `c39f5cd` first routed this case to the scene-chunk download
  panel, which says "The globe could not load", although the globe had loaded. The scoped
  re-review flagged the wording. `6160692` gives the case its own panel and amends §B.7 to record
  the change.
- **Unchanged.** A worker that never started, because the browser has no module workers or a CSP
  blocks it, keeps its "Retry data" error.
- **Tests.** A unit test renders every failure panel. The Playwright test aborts the first
  worker-script request, expects the map-data panel and no globe panel, and recovers through the
  reload, in both projects.

**Three overlay regressions found and fixed by these captures** (each has its own commit and unit
test):

1. **Country labels were off their anchor** (`fix: center Natural Earth country labels on their
   anchor`). Cesium's `Label` defaults to `LEFT`/`BASELINE`, whereas the legacy entity labels
   (`LabelVisualizer`) were `CENTER`/`CENTER`. As a result, "Egypt", "Nigeria" and "Kenya" were
   drawn about 15–20 px right of and 4 px above their anchors.
2. **Borders were too thin** (`fix: draw buffer borders at the legacy entity polylines' weight`).
   - Cesium's entity polyline and `PolylineCollection` shaders draw every line 0.5 px wider than
     its width, and `BufferPolylineMaterialVS` does not. The legacy 1.65 px borders were therefore
     2.15 px on screen, and the §B.6.9 buffer borders were 1.65 px, with about 77% of the legacy ink.
   - The buffer width is now 2.15 px, and the projected 2D borders keep 1.65 px.
   - The every-layer delta fell from 1.09–1.32% to 0.06–0.09%, and the border ink ratio is now
     0.996–0.997 (honmoon 1.03).

3. **Country labels could stay undrawn** (`fix: keep rendering until the country label glyphs are
   drawn`). In some cold loads (4 of about 80 every-layer after-captures in Task 85's first runs),
   some or all country labels did not appear until something else requested a frame:
   - Cesium creates label glyphs lazily inside `LabelCollection.update`, and
     `TextureAtlas.addImage` queues them only after an `await`.
   - The atlas update in that frame's `afterRender` therefore finds nothing queued, returns
     `false`, and requests no further render; in request-render mode nothing draws the glyphs.
   - `ContextOverlay` now requests frames from `scene.postRender` until `LabelCollection.ready`
     (at most 120 frames, none while the countries layer is hidden), then unsubscribes.
   - An interleaved screenshot probe (HbS triangles, every layer, same procedure as the captures)
     found a short label set in 4 of 120 pre-fix loads and 0 of 80 post-fix loads. All 4 came
     in one batch, so the probe alone is not decisive (one-sided Fisher p ≈ 0.13); the unit tests
     pin the behaviour.
