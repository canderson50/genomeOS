## Body

## TL;DR

The model was letting its baseline level and the average spatial effect cancel each other, which made sampling unstable when the simulated data contained no spatial activity signal. The baseline parameter now means the average fitted value across the training locations, while the model keeps the same prior and produces the same fitted values at training and prediction locations. The three frozen simulation checks now complete on their first attempt with zero divergent sampler steps.

## Problem and change

PyMC's HSGP basis generally has a nonzero mean over the training coordinates. Adding a separate constant intercept therefore gave the sampler two nearly interchangeable ways to set the field's global level.

`_spatial_field` now:

- constructs the same PyMC Laplace basis and Matérn spectral weights explicitly;
- subtracts the realized training-set mean from the spatial deviation at both training and prediction coordinates;
- samples the named intercept as the exact training-field mean; and
- shifts that intercept's conditional prior mean by the same realized basis mean.

If the old field was `a + h(s)` and `m` is the training mean of `h`, the new coordinate is `g = a + m`, with field `g + h(s) - m` and `g | h ~ Normal(mu + m, sigma)`. This is a one-to-one, unit-Jacobian change of coordinates. It preserves the original prior, every likelihood-facing field value, and every prediction while removing the redundant likelihood direction.

Both the conditional-frequency field and activity-probability field use the same transform. The marginalized count likelihood, cohort effects, sampler settings, retry rule, and scientific gates are unchanged. This implements design §§7–8.
