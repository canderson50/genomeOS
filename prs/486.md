## Body

## TL;DR

With **Cesium World Terrain** chosen under *Map → Basemap & terrain*, mountains now show when you zoom in, at any time of day.

- **Shading:** the ground is shaded by a fixed light from the north-west, like a shaded-relief map, not by the real sun. This light fades out from continental scale up, so the far view and the whole-world 2D map have no fake night side. Day/night still applies to the smooth globe.
- **Relief control:** a new **Physical relief ×** slider (1× to 10×) raises the mountains. It is separate from the data's *Statistical elevation*.
- **Hidden-data warning:** while World Terrain is drawn, a note says it can hide map data on high ground.
- **Zoom:** the zoom-in button and the + key stop 1 km above the ground. Before, they could put the camera underground on a grey screen.
- **Help:** the **Keys** help now explains how to tilt the view: Ctrl+drag, middle-button drag, or two-finger drag.

This PR **advances #483**. It does not close it. A second PR will drape the data onto the terrain (see the last section).

## What this changes, and why

Issue #483 reports that World Terrain loaded but looked identical to the smooth globe. There were three reasons:

- **Clock lighting.** Shading followed the real-time sun, and only when day/night was on. Anywhere on the night side, or with day/night off, the ground was evenly lit and looked flat.
- **No tilt.** The camera looks straight down, and nothing in the UI said how to tilt it.
- **Buried data.** The data layers sit at fixed heights, so high ground cuts through them.

This PR fixes the first two and the smaller problems listed in the issue. The owner's decisions:

1. **Hillshade lighting whenever World Terrain is on.** A fixed light from the north-west (azimuth 315°) is aimed at the view target. The day/night toggle applies to the smooth globe only. It stays visible while World Terrain is on, disabled, with the reason.
2. **A vertical-exaggeration control,** following Cesium's vertical-exaggeration Sandcastle (`scene.verticalExaggeration`). It is kept clearly separate from the data-elevation control.
3. **Draping the data onto the terrain comes in a second PR,** not this one.

**Changes, by commit:**

- **Hillshade** (`scene/terrain-shading.ts`, `scene-policy.ts`):
  - The light is 35° above the horizon at the view target. From about 660 km out, it rises to stay 10° above the edge of the visible disc.
  - Cesium's vertex lighting has no distance fade, so the shading fades out between 1,000 and 3,000 km (`vertexShadowDarkness` 0.3 → 1). Beyond that, World Terrain is drawn unlit, as the smooth globe is with day/night off.
  - The light is aimed at the ground drawn on the view axis (`viewCentre`, beside the desktop controls panel), not at the canvas centre.
  - Switching back to the smooth globe restores the sun, day/night, the atmosphere setting and the shadow darkness.
- **Physical relief ×** (`ReliefControl.tsx`, `relief` URL parameter): 1 to 10 in steps of 0.5, default 1.
  - It sets `scene.verticalExaggeration` from sea level, with `verticalExaggerationRelativeHeight` 0.
  - It applies only to World Terrain outside the 2D map.
  - The scene applies it at most once per animation frame.
  - The data slider now reads "× data height" and says it scales data values, not the ground.
- **Hidden-data warning:** "World Terrain can hide map data on high ground, and raised relief hides more."
  - It shows whenever World Terrain's height is drawn, even at 1×, because #483 point 3 happens at true scale.
  - It sits in a `role="status"` region and is part of the slider's description, so screen readers announce it.
- **Controls follow the terrain actually on the globe.** The scene reports the terrain it applied through `onTerrainApplied`. So while World Terrain is loading, or after it fails, day/night stays usable and the relief slider says World Terrain has not loaded.
- **Zoom floor** (`scene/camera.ts`): the zoom-in button and the + key stop 1,000 m above the ground under the camera.
  - Before, a 50 km step from 29.6 km took the camera to −20.4 km.
  - A tilted camera that lands over higher ground backs off along its view line.
  - A zoom-in during a view morph does nothing, because Cesium has no camera pitch then.
  - Wheel and pinch zoom are unchanged.
- **Ion requests:**
  - Terrain is requested once at scene creation. A repeat request for the same terrain reuses the one in flight.
  - Tile-load failures raise the terrain warning once and are each logged to the console. Cesium stops logging them once anything listens.
  - The error listener is removed when the scene is destroyed.
- **Tilt help:** Q/E are relabelled "look up or down", which is what they do, and the help gives Cesium 1.145's tilt gestures. The Keys popover is anchored so the longer text stays inside the panel.
- **Spec:** interaction design §5.2 is amended with these decisions.
- **E2E:** CI has no ion token, so the e2e build stands in a synthetic cone for World Terrain. It is test-only and kept out of the production `dist/`, which a unit test checks.

## What I verified, and what I did not

**Verified on the live site, with real Cesium World Terrain.** CI and local builds have no ion token, and the production token is restricted to the production URLs. So I loaded https://genome-os.org/app/ in a browser, applied this PR's lighting rules to the live scene from the console, and compared the result with the rules as they stood before the review:

- **Desktop home view** (camera at 18,000 km over 20° E, 12° N):
  - Before the review, the light was aimed through the canvas centre, which lands at 16.1° W: 36° west of the view. The east limb came out visibly darker.
  - With this fix, the globe is evenly lit, exactly as the smooth globe with day/night off.
- **Whole-world 2D map** (Cesium reports a 19,000 km camera height): the darkened eastern edge before the fix is gone.
- **Himalaya at 200 km:** the hillshade shows the relief clearly, with shadows on the south-east faces. This answers the first PR's open question about real terrain.
- **About 1,500 km:** the shading is about half faded. With World Terrain at 1×, the data surface stops at the Himalayan foothills and is missing over the Tibetan plateau. That is #483 point 3, and it is why the warning now shows at 1×.

**Verified by tests:**
- The policy functions and the shading controller (fade, aim, restore, 2D zoom).
- The applied-terrain plumbing and the zoom guard.
- Controller teardown.
- The e2e suite drives World Terrain through the UI with the synthetic cone. It checks:
  - hillshade on and the sun off;
  - relief from sea level;
  - the warning at 1× and at 3×;
  - the zoom floor for both the button and the key;
  - World Terrain drawn unlit at the default global view;
  - the sun and day/night back on the smooth globe.

**Not verified:**
- **Pixels in CI.** The synthetic cone has no vertex normals, so the e2e checks the lighting state, not the image.
- **The lighting code itself on the live site.** The browser check applied the same rules through the console. It did not run this branch's build, which can only be deployed by merging.

**Observed and left alone: ocean glints.** World Terrain carries a water mask, and Cesium's water effect lights the ocean with the scene's light.
- **On main:** the ocean under World Terrain turns bright cyan where the real sun is up and shows a sun glint. This ignores the Ocean colour control.
- **Under the fixed light:** the ocean is always lit, and from far out a soft glint sits near the view centre when that centre is ocean.

This is not a night side, and main already shows the same effect by day. Whether World Terrain should keep Cesium's water effect (`globe.showWaterEffect`) at all is the owner's call.

## Next: the draping PR

The rest of #483 follows the draping design and its implementation plan in #485 (approved by the owner on 2026-10-18). It ships as four stacked PRs on top of this one, starting with phase 1 (`feat/atlas-drape-1`). Together they will:

- **Drape the data onto the ground.** The inferred surface, support masks, cell outlines, country borders and sampling footprints will follow the terrain instead of sitting at fixed heights (650 m, 1,050 m, 1,800 m and up).
  - The data worker will draw them into image tiles, which Cesium lays on World Terrain.
  - They will then follow the ground at every zoom, view and relief, and take the hillshade from this PR.
  - Each stays its own layer, so observations never share an image with the inferred surface.
  - The smooth globe does not change.
- **Keep symbols and labels above ground.** Observation symbols, labels and the selection highlight will be lifted by the ground height under them, so high ground never buries them.
- **Fix picking on slopes.** A pick will hit the drawn terrain, so the cell under the cursor is right on slopes and in angled views.
- **Retire the hidden-data warning** added here, once the data follows the ground.
- **Turn off Cesium's water effect** while the data surface is shown. That also settles the ocean-glint note above.
- **Reuse Physical relief ×** (`relief`, added here) as its terrain-exaggeration control.

The owner settled the design's three questions on 2026-10-18:

1. *Statistical elevation* is off under World Terrain (the URL keeps it; the control says why).
2. A 1 s fade replaces the radial reveal wave there; the smooth globe keeps the wave.
3. World Terrain may be desktop-only if phones still miss the memory budget after the fallbacks.
