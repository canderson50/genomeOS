## What this changes, and why

Two small fidelity defects found while auditing the identity question in #228. **Neither changes a published number.** Both concern whether what we record matches what we computed.

## Issues

Closes #231, closes #234

## #231 — the docstring described an assumption, not the corpus

`afnd_frequencies.py` claimed "The frequency is printed to four decimal places." Measured against the 2026-08 harvest, **10,040 of 123,502 rows print to seven**, all `hla`, for rare alleles like `0.0000980`.

The science is unaffected: four decimals is the worst case across the corpus, so the stated `0.00005 * 2n` reconstruction bound stays conservative for the higher-precision rows rather than being violated. But the factual claim was wrong in a load-bearing way — **that false premise is what made a fixed-decimal identity format look safe in #228.** The docstring now states the range and carries the warning, because `f"{af:.4f}"` maps `0.0000980` and `0.0000900` onto one string and `0.0000050` onto `"0.0000"`, collapsing distinct measurements and publishing an observed rare allele as absent.

## #234 — the manifest rounded values a consumer needs

`publish_artifacts.py` rounded `correlation_range_km` to 1 dp and `prior_frequency_sd` to 5 dp on the way into the artifact manifest.

**Nothing published is wrong.** `cell_table` computes the contraction from the live fit at full precision, so every `support` verdict is correct — I verified that before changing anything. But the manifest is the record of what a consumer can redo, and `cell_table`'s own docstring invites applying a stricter threshold without refitting, which divides by `prior_frequency_sd` — available only at the rounded precision. Both are now stored unrounded. The `print` on the following line already formats them for reading, so nothing became less legible.

Magnitude, stated honestly: rounding ~0.125 to 5 dp is a relative error of about 4e-5, so only a cell already sitting on the threshold could flip. This is a fidelity fix, not a correctness one.

## The test guards the serialisation, not the assignment

Storing an unrounded float is worth nothing if `ArtifactManifest.to_json` truncates it, so the new test round-trips a 17-digit value through `to_json` *and* through a published `manifest.json` on disk. `float(x) == x` would have been a vacuous test; this one has a real failure mode.

Confirmed it is a genuine tripwire rather than a tautology: adding a float formatter to `to_json` makes it fail, and removing it makes it pass again.

**No test for the docstring claim.** It is about a 6.3 MB raw corpus that is not a checked-in fixture, and asserting it would mean committing that file or fabricating a stand-in. The measurement is recorded in the docstring and in #231 instead. Flagging this rather than leaving you to notice the asymmetry.
