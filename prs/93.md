## Two findings that change how §8 must be scored — #92

Both are arithmetic rather than modelling, and getting either wrong breaks the test in opposite directions.

**1. The published intervals are IQRs — 50% credible intervals**, per the appendix's footnote 5: *"IQR: interquartile range of the posterior predictive distribution"*. Our artifacts carry `q025`/`q975`, a 95% interval.

§8 requires *"our credible interval overlaps the published interval for ≥95% of them"*. Overlapping a 95% interval with a 50% one is close to automatic — ours would have to miss the middle half of their posterior entirely to fail. **Scored that way the criterion passes almost regardless of model quality**, which makes it a decoration rather than a test. Fixing it means carrying `q25`/`q75` in the burden artifacts, so it's a schema change (§6 lists only `mean`, `q025`, `q975`), not just a test change.

**2. The point estimates are medians, which do not sum.** The 191 national medians total **5,239,477 AS** and **288,814 SS**, against the paper's global posterior of **5,476,000** (IQR 5,291,000–5,679,000) and **312,000** (294,000–330,000) — 4% and 7% lower, with the summed SS falling *below* the published IQR.

That gap is arithmetic, not error: adding right-skewed per-country medians understates the global figure, which comes from the global posterior. So §8's *"global totals agree within the published uncertainty"* must compare **our global posterior** against theirs. Sum our own national medians instead and we will reproduce this same ~5% shortfall and read it as a model failure.

`tests/test_piel2013_reference.py` pins the gap deliberately, with a note that a future extraction which made the sums *match* would mean the table had been misread — not that the transcription improved.
