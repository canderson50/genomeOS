## 1. Presence markers scaled by observed frequency, now the default

A presence survey was drawn at a fixed size, so a population carrying an allele at 0.02% and one
carrying it at 18% were the same dot. The figure threw away the quantity it exists to show.

Marker **area** is now proportional to observed frequency, which is the cartographic convention for
a proportional-symbol map. The bounds are the design rather than decoration: measured on
DRB1*01:01, A*02:01 and HbS alike, **about half of all presences sit below a quarter of the maximum
frequency**, so without a floor most of the evidence shrinks below visibility, and without a ceiling
the dense clusters merge into one opaque mass and hide the surface underneath.

- Area runs 4 to 32 by default, adjustable with `--marker-area-min` / `--marker-area-max`.
- Defaults are tuned against the **densest** layer, not the sparsest: HbS carries 840 presences to
  DRB1*01:01's 392, so a ceiling that reads well on an HLA allele overplots HbS. Sparser alleles can
  be pushed back up.
- Largest drawn first, so a small marker inside a dense cluster lands on top and stays findable.
- The legend carries three real frequencies drawn at the size the map uses, with `markerscale`
  pinned to 1.0 — a scaled legend would make the exemplars lie about what they encode.

![HbS surface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bschilder/genomeOS/18440da4188b13dc17a826dcc59cd38b2a89600d/docs/figures/hbs_surface_geodesic.png)

What the sizing exposes: the surface peaks near 0.14–0.16 in the Congo basin while the largest
observed surveys reach **21.9%**. The fitted peak sits well below the largest observations, visible
now as a systematic gap rather than a one-off, and consistent with #109's finding that the model
flattens what it extrapolates.

## 2. `--layer afnd`

The plotter knew two hardcoded layers, so **none of the 767 modelable AFND alleles could be
rendered at all**. It now takes `--populations` and `--variant`, reconstructs the display name from
the variant_id (`hla:drb1-15-01` -> `HLA-DRB1*15:01`) since 767 alleles rule out a title table, and
credits AFND rather than MAP in the caption so the figure's own provenance is not wrong (§4).

## 3. Two figures that show the support mask failing

Same locus, same populations, similar observation counts, 5x apart in fitted range:

| allele | observations | fitted range | cells masked |
|---|---|---|---|
| C*07:02 | 243 | 823 km | 2,078 / 11,668 (17.8%) |
| C*03:03 | 240 | **4,189 km** | **0 / 11,668** |

![C*07:02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bschilder/genomeOS/18440da4188b13dc17a826dcc59cd38b2a89600d/docs/figures/afnd_hla_c_07_02_surface.png)
![C*03:03](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bschilder/genomeOS/18440da4188b13dc17a826dcc59cd38b2a89600d/docs/figures/afnd_hla_c_03_03_surface.png)

**C\*03:03 claims an allele frequency for every inhabited cell on Earth from 240 observations.** The
mask's distance rule scales with the fitted range, so a wide-range allele masks nothing. This is the
mechanism behind the 0.74 spatial-fold interval coverage measured in #109, now visible instead of
inferred, and it is direct evidence for #59 (mask on by default) needing a threshold that does not
scale with the range.

It is also the first committed evidence that **one gene carries very different spatial scales** —
an argument for #129 (non-stationarity) and against pooling toward a shared scale in #128.

## What an expert should check

- Whether area-proportional is the right encoding, or whether a perceptual (Flannery) correction is
  worth it. Area-proportional is the convention and I did not deviate from it.
- The default ceiling of 32. It is set for HbS's 840 presences; anyone rendering a sparse allele
  should raise it, and the flag exists so that is a one-word change rather than an edit.
