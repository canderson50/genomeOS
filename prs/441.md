## What this changes, and why

- **A plain HTML form** posts straight to Mailchimp in a new tab. The site loads no Mailchimp script or embed and sets no cookie.
- **Consent** is an unticked, required box. It names the sender ("genomeOS Foundation, being registered in New York State"), says Mailchimp records opens and link clicks, and points to the unsubscribe link and the privacy section.
- **Mailchimp's bot trap** field is included, named from the account and audience IDs.
- **The privacy page** gains a "Launch-updates newsletter" section:
  - who holds the list;
  - that the email address is the only field;
  - consent as the only basis, confirmed by double opt-in;
  - Mailchimp (Intuit) storing it in the United States, and recording sign-up and confirmation time and IP as the consent record;
  - open and click tracking, with blocking images given as the way to stop open tracking;
  - retention until unsubscribe, with Mailchimp keeping unsubscribed addresses as suppressed, and full deletion on request;
  - rights of access, correction and deletion via `info@genome-os.org`, and complaint to a data protection authority;
  - a note that it's not meant for under-16s.

  The page's description, intro and "What is not" list now mention the newsletter.
- **Build wiring** follows the GA measurement ID pattern:
  - `pages.yml` passes `vars.MAILCHIMP_FORM_ACTION`;
  - the fallback build and the unit-test build clear it;
  - `test:e2e` uses a placeholder action, which it never submits to.

## What I verified, and what I did not

- **Verified:**
  - the form is absent from every page of a build without the variable, and the string `list-manage.com` appears in no HTML there;
  - with the variable set, the form posts to the exact action with `EMAIL` and the trap field, and the trap sits offscreen;
  - the consent box starts unticked and blocks submission;
  - no request goes to Mailchimp on page load;
  - the privacy notice is present in every build.
- **Not verified, and needed before launch:**
  1. **Legal review.** This follows common GDPR, UK GDPR, CASL, Quebec Law 25, CAN-SPAM and CCPA practice, but compliance with every privacy law can't be established by code. A lawyer should review the notice and consent wording.
  2. **The controller is not yet a legal entity.** The page names the Foundation as "being registered in New York State", as the owner chose. Counsel should confirm that's acceptable until filing, and the text should be updated once it's filed.
  3. **A postal address.** CASL wants the sender's mailing address in the consent request, and CAN-SPAM and Mailchimp want a physical address in every email. The Foundation has none yet, so none is given.
  4. **Mailchimp settings that must match the page:**
     - double opt-in on;
     - the audience's address and the "from" identity set to the Foundation;
     - open and click tracking on (the page discloses them).
  5. **Transfer basis.** The page says Mailchimp stores data in the US. Counsel should confirm the EU/UK transfer mechanism (Mailchimp's data processing terms) and whether the page should name it.
  6. **The Intuit privacy-statement link** (`https://www.intuit.com/privacy/statement/`) is external and wasn't checked by the link checker.
  7. **Opt-in-region tracking.** In the EU and UK, open and click tracking arguably needs consent of its own. Here it is folded into the single sign-up consent, which discloses it. Separating it would mean turning tracking off, or using Mailchimp's GDPR marketing-permission fields.
