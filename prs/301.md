## TL;DR

The sickle-cell benchmark accidentally compared disease estimates from 2010 with population totals from 2020. This change aligns both to 2010; the benchmark still fails, showing that the remaining error comes from the prediction model rather than the year mismatch.

---

Piel et al.'s HbS benchmark reports 2010 neonatal burden, but the offline parity runner was using 2020 WorldPop cell counts as national denominators. Across represented benchmark countries that compared 7.552B people with Piel's 6.882B, turning population growth into apparent model error.

This change keeps the declared gridded population source as within-country spatial weights, rescales each represented country to Piel's explicit 2010 population before applying its birth rate, and records the weight source, target source/year, and country scale factor. Missing targets, bad provenance, nonfinite/negative weights, and zero-weight countries are hard errors.

The corrected canonical run uses 994 Piel-comparable surveys and 500 coherent posterior draws. It still fails golden test #45, but now for an interpretable model reason:

| Metric | Countries | Points inside published IQR | Interval overlap | Model global median (IQR) | Piel global median (IQR) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HbSS | 155/191 | 29.3% | 46.6% | 253,080 (231,469–275,293) | 312,000 (294,000–330,000) |
| HbAS | 155/191 | 28.8% | 40.3% | 6,471,133 (6,229,413–6,707,077) | 5,476,000 (5,291,000–5,679,000) |

The geography is broadly learned (country-rank Spearman 0.927 for HbSS and 0.826 for HbAS), but a diffuse non-endemic frequency floor inflates HbAS while flattened endemic peaks depress HbSS. This rules out a single global calibration factor and motivates one bounded comparison of the stationary GP against a low-background/local-departure model. MAP malaria, EVI, friction, and other source-adjacent rasters remain unscheduled unless a named mechanism and controlled comparison show unique value.
