## What changed, and why

**The figure was drawn on the wrong geometry.** It used `pcolormesh` over a lat/lon meshgrid, so
every "cell" was a rectangle whose ground area varies by a factor of two between the equator and
60°N. That reintroduced at the rendering step exactly the distortion the spherical kernel and the
geodesic inducing grid exist to remove, and it disagreed with §6, which specifies H3 for the
product. The surface is now evaluated and drawn on H3 cells directly.

Only land cells are evaluated at all, which removes the separate ocean-masking pass: §7's support
states are about observation proximity and never ask whether a cell is inhabited. This is a
partial answer to #101 — land is still a coarse stand-in for "anyone lives here", which WorldPop
(#43) is the real test for. **It does not close #101.**

**Inducing spacing must match the correlation range.** A fit at M=400 on a fully-utilised A40 took
**80 minutes**; M=200 on a laptop CPU took **8.8 minutes** and converged. The cause was not
hardware. Inducing points 96 km apart against a ~600 km correlation range correlate at ~0.99, so
they add parameters without adding information:

| M | spacing | spacing/ρ | cond(K_uu) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 259 km | 0.65 | 1.5e4 |
| 200 | 127 km | 0.32 | 1.5e5 |
| 400 | 96 km | 0.24 | **5.8e5** |

`K_uu` goes near-singular and NUTS grinds at maximum tree depth. Default `n_inducing` is now 200,
`JITTER` 1e-6 → 1e-4, and `SurfaceFit` reports `inducing_spacing_ratio`, warning below 0.25.
Worth knowing for #57: **H3 placement is ~5× worse conditioned than k-means at equal M** (5.99e5
vs 1.22e5), because near-uniform spacing maximises redundancy. That is a real cost of the geodesic
grid, to weigh against its determinism and its alignment with §6.

**Cross-validation tolerates a non-converged fold.** A single `ConvergenceError` previously aborted
the whole run — one fold cost 8.9 pod-hours and returned nothing. Folds now record a `FoldFailure`,
are excluded from the means, and are reported as "N did not converge" rather than as `NaN`.

## Colour choices, decided by measurement

`coolwarm` was tried first and rejected. Being diverging it passes through neutral grey at its
midpoint and lands **0.070 from the grey land base — under the ~0.10 just-noticeable threshold**,
so cells near 8% HbS, a clinically serious frequency, were indistinguishable from *"no data"*. On
a figure whose entire purpose is separating what we know from what we do not, that is a defect
rather than a taste question. `turbo` clears grey by 0.70 and white by 0.80 with 60% better
adjacent-value contrast.

Per the review-figure rules in AGENTS.md: observations and the fitted surface are separate layers,
absence surveys are drawn as distinct shapes so contrary evidence stays visible, cells with no
claim are shown as bare grey land rather than left blank, and the ramp reads low-to-high without
the legend.

## Caching — a palette change cost 9 minutes before this

- `save_fit`/`load_fit` persist the trained model. `predict` needs the live PyMC graph alongside
  the posterior, so cloudpickle carries both. **These are caches, not artifacts**: coupled to the
  installed PyMC, and pickle executes on load. §6 artifacts remain the parquet outputs.
- `--cache` additionally stores per-cell predictions, valid only for the same resolution.

A palette change now takes **1.4 s**.
