## Why

`cohort_id` is the contributing *study*, and a study effect is identified by within-study replication. A random split scatters a study's sites across folds, so in training it becomes a singleton — one observation, one level — at which point `cohort_sd * cohort_z` and the beta-binomial `concentration` describe the same single residual and are not jointly identifiable.

On the MAP HbS corpus that is not a corner case (numbers from #127):

| Fold strategy | Multi-site studies split | Folds failing to converge |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial | 7 / 143 (5%) | 1 of 5 |
| Random | 135 / 143 (**94%**) | 3 of 5 |

So the spatial-versus-random comparison in #109 confounds spatial leakage with a loss of identifiability, and `predict_observation`'s premise — that a held-out survey belongs to a cohort the model never saw — is false 94% of the time under random folds.

## Changes

- **`grouped` fold strategy.** Whole studies are assigned to folds, largest first, each to the currently emptiest fold. That is `GroupKFold`'s algorithm written out rather than taking scikit-learn as a dependency for one greedy loop — it is not in `pyproject.toml` and has no entry in `requirements.lock`. Balanced by **observation** count rather than study count: studies are uneven enough (419 studies over 1,071 surveys) that round-robin over cohorts would leave folds of incomparable size. Deterministic and seedless, since ordering is by cohort size.
- **`studies_split_across_folds()`**, computed and reported for **every** strategy rather than only `grouped`, so the confound is visible wherever it exists instead of inferred. Single-site studies are excluded — they cannot be split and carry no within-study contrast either way.
- **`None` means not computed**, and never prints as "none split". A zero here is a measurement and has to be earned.
- **`validate_holdout.py`** runs all three strategies and carries the split counts into the summary artifact, so the confound is auditable from the artifact and not only from stdout.

## One judgment call, called out rather than slipped in

The **"autocorrelation share of apparent skill"** statistic now compares spatial against `grouped` rather than `random`.

Both `grouped` and `random` leak neighbouring surveys across the split; only `random` also shatters studies. So the old spatial-vs-random gap mixed spatial autocorrelation with a loss of identifiability and could not be attributed to either — whereas spatial-vs-grouped isolates spatial leakage cleanly, because both keep the cohort term identifiable.

This changes a documented number, so it is flagged here rather than buried. Happy to revert to the old contrast and merely annotate it if you'd rather.

## Not done here

**#127's third proposal** — conditioning rather than marginalising in `predict_observation` when the cohort was seen in training.

It rewrites the same predictive path as the cohort-identifiability fix in open PR [#124](https://github.com/bschilder/genomeOS/pull/124), which already changes when the cohort term is fitted at all (`beta_cohort_applied`) and "integrates over no cohort offset when none was fitted". Doing it now would collide with that work and partly duplicate it. It should follow once #124 lands.

`genomeos/surfaces/fit.py` is untouched by this PR for exactly that reason — the diff is confined to `crossval.py`, its tests, and the holdout script.
