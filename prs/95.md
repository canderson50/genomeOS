## The three criteria, implemented

| § | Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 8.1 | national point estimate inside the published interval | ≥80% of countries |
| 8.2 | our credible interval overlaps the published one | ≥95% of countries |
| 8.3 | global totals agree within the published uncertainty | — |

## Written to be pessimistic where it could flatter us

A scorer that flatters the pipeline is worse than no scorer, so the tests check for that as much as for arithmetic:

- **The denominator is every country with a published estimate**, not every country we produced a number for. Otherwise the score *improves as coverage shrinks* — refusing to answer would look like accuracy. A country we cannot estimate counts against us.
- **A missing global estimate fails criterion 3 rather than skipping it.** An unmeasured criterion is not a met one.
- **Intervals must be like for like.** The published intervals are IQRs, so the caller must pass an IQR. That can't be checked at runtime, so it's a documented precondition — and the reason #92 matters: scoring a 95% interval against a 50% one makes criterion 2 nearly free.
- **`worst_countries()` ranks failures by published burden**, because a bare pass/fail isn't actionable. When parity fails, the question is immediately *which countries, and are they the ones that matter*.

## What's left for #45 — #94

The middle: turning a fitted surface into one AS/SS estimate per country.

The tempting shortcut is a country-centroid lookup. It would be worst exactly where it matters — Nigeria, India and the DRC carry most of the global burden and are precisely the countries with large internal HbS variation. **A centroid value for India is not a number anyone should score parity against.** §9 already specifies the right approach (population-weighted over H3 cells), so #94 does it properly, sharing the country-rollup implementation with #61 rather than growing a second one.

#94 also flags a cheap-now/expensive-later detail: the three data sources spell country names differently ("United Republic of Tanzania" vs Piel's conventions vs GADM's), so the join should be on ISO3 codes before three call sites depend on names.
