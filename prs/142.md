## Body

Closes #141. Design sections: §4 (observations vs inference), §9 (refusal is a valid output), §12.

## The fault

37 of 942 populations carried **91% of the corpus's statistical weight**, all sitting at two
coordinates:

| registry | populations | coordinate | weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| DKMS | 15 | 48.52, 9.05 (Germany) | 6,996,066 — 49.8% |
| NMDP | 22 | 38.75, −76.10 (Maryland) | 5,797,650 — 41.2% |
| **both** | **37** | | **91.0%** |
| everything else | 905 | genuinely geographic | 9.0% |

For `HLA-A*02:01` the NMDP strata span **0.035 to 0.278 at zero geographic separation**.

## Why refused rather than relocated

NMDP builds these strata deliberately, because HLA matching requires a donor of the same ancestral
background. The label is meaningful and the data is the best-powered in the corpus. It is simply
not point-referenced, and there is nowhere to put it:

- **"NMDP European Caucasian"** is a blend of European source populations mixed by US immigration
  history. That blend exists at no location in Europe.
- **"NMDP African American"** is ~73% African and ~24% European (Bryc et al. 2015), matching no
  single source population. America is where those people are; Africa would assert something false
  about a quarter of the genome.
- **A country is not an answer either.** The US contains European-, African-, Asian- and
  Indigenous-descended populations living interleaved in the same cities. That is stratification,
  which a smooth spatial field cannot represent whatever coordinate it is given.

`DKMS German donors` is refused with the rest despite being a national sample rather than a
stratum: it shares the registry coordinate with 14 strata, is donor-ascertained, and at 6,912,132
chromosomes is 49.8% of the corpus alone. Strongest candidate for re-admission once #21
(per-population radii) and #123 (weight capping) exist.

## Effect

```
69180/123502 rows retained (56%) — 723 alleles across 875 populations
  refused  19172  below_min_populations
  refused  17220  donor_registry_ancestry_stratum
  ...
```

Total weight **14,056,204 → 1,256,020**. Largest single population **6,912,132 → 123,310**, a 56×
reduction. The refusal runs ahead of the parsing checks so a registry row that also lacks a
frequency is reported as a stratum rather than as missing data, and the arithmetic still balances
exactly.

## It does NOT fix #137

Refitting five alleles with the strata removed — **all converged**, r_hat 1.007–1.016:

| allele | before | after | |
|---|---|---|---|
| C*03:03 | 4,228 km | 3,386 km | 0.80× |
| B*40:01 | 2,875 km | 2,597 km | 0.90× |
| A*02:01 | 1,377 km | 1,289 km | 0.94× |
| DRB1*01:01 | 1,730 km | 1,700 km | 0.98× |
| C*07:02 | 841 km | 931 km | 1.11× |

Removing 91% of the weight moved ranges 0–20%. **The stratification was a real fault worth fixing
on its own merits; it was not the cause of the long ranges.** I expected it to be, and said so in
#141 — recording that it wasn't.

## Superseded artifacts

The 20 AFND artifacts published in #140 were built on the old corpus. Per §5 they stay as `v1` and
a rebuild publishes `v2`; nothing citing `v1` stops resolving. They should be rebuilt, but that is
better done once #137 is settled than twice.

## Verification

350 tests pass, including 2 new ones covering the refusal and its ordering ahead of the
missing-data checks. `ruff`, `freeze_contract --check`, `check_module_size`,
`check_private_files` and `smoke` all pass.

## What an expert should check

- Whether `DKMS German donors` should have been kept as a national sample. I refused it for
  weight and co-location reasons, but it is genuinely a sample of Germany and reasonable people
  could keep it.
- Whether matching on the registry name is robust. AFND may add registries under other names, and
  the current rule would not catch them; the safety net is that any new population with an `an`
  far above the corpus median deserves a look.
