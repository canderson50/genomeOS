## Body

## What users get

These changes apply when Cesium World Terrain is switched on. The default smooth globe looks and behaves exactly as before.

- **The relief reads as a hillshade.** A fixed light from the north-west, 45° up, shades the ground. As you zoom out it turns toward your line of sight, so the globe's edge never goes dark. Flat ground keeps its legend colour and only slopes that face away from the light get darker. In 2D and Columbus view the far-out light comes from straight overhead.
- **The data colours are no longer tinted.** While the inferred surface or evidence support layer is shown, the globe's ground atmosphere and water effect stay off. They come back when you hide both layers or return to the smooth globe. If the globe had them off to begin with, they stay off.
- **Clicks and hovers pick the cell on the ground you see**, including on raised relief and in 2D and Columbus view. Before, a click on high ground that hid the floating surface selected nothing. A point on an observation still wins. Mid-morph, nothing is picked.

Known and expected: the data still floats at fixed heights, so high ground still hides it. Phase 2 drapes it onto the terrain, and the #483 repro below starts passing then.

## Changes per task

- **Task 1: lighting** (`scene/scene-policy.ts`, `scene/terrain-shading.ts`, interaction design §5.2 amendment).
  - The hillshade elevation goes from 35° to 45°. PR 1's horizon rule is kept.
  - Between 500 km and 5,000 km the light swings to a headlight: `camera.directionWC` in 3D, overhead at the target in 2D and Columbus view.
  - `vertexShadowDarkness` is 0.45 and `lambertDiffuseMultiplier` is 0.9, so `sin 45° × 0.9 + 0.45 ≥ 1` and flat ground keeps the legend colour.
  - `terrainLighting` now says whether the atmosphere and water effect may show. `TerrainShading` ANDs each with the globe's own value and restores all three terms on the smooth globe.
  - A frame mid-morph keeps the last finite light direction.
- **Task 2: draped pick** (`scene/surface-pick.ts`, `scene/scientific-layers.ts`).
  - `resolveDrapedPick` runs `globe.pick(camera.getPickRay(p), scene)`, then `latLngToCell`, then `rowForH3`, then `drawn(row)`. It skips the 650 m clearance, the ellipsoid and the translucent depth pick.
  - `PickTarget` gains `drawn(row)` (supported rows need the surface layer, masked rows need the support layer, and the group must be shown) and `draped`, which is false for chunk groups.
  - Chunk groups still get the reveal wave's final say.
- **Task 3: fixture and probe** (`e2e-hooks.ts`, `scene/synthetic-terrain.ts`).
  - `SYNTHETIC_PEAK` is a 6 km Gaussian peak, σ 40 km, under the Madrid HbS survey `map-surveys:1135`. The e2e grid supports every res-4 cell within 2σ of it; it has none of the Kenya survey's cells.
  - The probe reports the four globe terms and `apexWindow`, the summit's position on screen.
  - The production build still carries no synthetic terrain.
- **Task 4: scene wiring** (`scene/atlas-scene.ts`).
  - `SurfacePickContext.draped()` is `hasPhysicalRelief(applied terrain) && elevationFactor === 0`. Picks stay on the smooth path until World Terrain is actually on the globe, and data lifted by elevation keeps the depth pick.
  - `setLayerVisibility` tells the shading whether a surface layer is shown.
- **Task 5: e2e** (`tests/atlas-drape.spec.ts`, `tests/atlas-terrain-helpers.ts`, `playwright.config.ts`).
  - The #483 repro runs at nadir and oblique, relief 1 and 3, and is held under `test.fail` until Task 17.
  - An oblique click at the summit must select the summit's own cell.
  - A probe test checks the hillshade, atmosphere and water terms.
  - The probe, camera and chooser helpers move into a file shared with `atlas-terrain.spec.ts`.
- **Review follow-ups.**
  - A smooth-globe control runs the repro's summit check outside `test.fail` and needs it to return true. The repro's expected failure therefore means "the summit buries the surface", not "the check cannot see".
  - The World Terrain round trip in `atlas-terrain.spec.ts` now asserts that the smooth globe gets its diffuse, shadow, ground atmosphere and water terms back.
  - The "no ellipsoid pick" unit test passes a chunk hit, so a draped branch that fell through to the smooth path would now fail it (checked by mutation).
  - The oblique pick test's title says what it proves.
  - Docstrings cite the spec by heading.
- **CI fix: find the summit once its own ground has loaded.** CI renders with software WebGL. There, the oblique drape camera's tiles out to the horizon did not finish loading within `apexOnPage`'s 30 s, so the oblique pick test failed on all three attempts. The oblique repros also failed for that reason, hidden by `test.fail`, instead of failing at their check.
  - The probe now reports `apexGround`, the rendered ground at the apex.
  - `apexOnPage` takes the summit once that ground is within 0.5% (or 10 m) of the peak at the current relief, or once every tile has loaded. It polls for up to 60 s.
  - Measured under SwiftShader: going from 99.4% to 99.99% of the summit's height moves the summit 0.43 px on the page.
