## TL;DR

Our research plan had already decided that malaria maps were a high-priority thing to test against
sickle-cell. Nothing had shown they actually help. A well-known data provider and a plausible story
were enough to book the work in advance.

This change removes that head start. Pathogen data now has to earn its place the way any other data
source does: somebody has to name the variant, say what the biological mechanism is, show the
timing makes sense, and beat a simpler comparison. Until that happens it is not scheduled.

Two paragraphs of an existing research memo change. No code, no data, no schema.

---

## What changed, and why

The September memo listed malaria layers from one publisher as a **high-priority mechanistic pilot**
for the sickle-cell locus. That is backwards. The famous dataset and the familiar story about
malaria and sickle-cell were doing the work that evidence of predictive value should have been
doing. Being associated with malaria is not a qualification.

Two edits:

**The covariate table.** The row is now about pathogen exposure *hypotheses* rather than one
provider's product, and it is unscheduled by default. A named asset has to bring a
variant-specific mechanism, defensible temporal support, an independent evaluation target, and a
simpler comparator before it enters a benchmark. It also records the trap directly: a contemporary
derived map does not establish historical selection.

**The order of the first experiments.** Simple qualified terrain, water and climate summaries come
first, one family at a time. A pathogen feature enters only under a named variant-specific
hypothesis that passes the same screen. The memo now says explicitly that a malaria-associated locus
or a particular publisher's label does not qualify one.

## What this does not change

The measured sickle-cell and G6PD survey inputs from the same publisher are untouched. Those are
observations feeding golden tests, a completely different role from a covariate raster competing for
a place in a benchmark. Nothing about them is affected.

## Scope

Implements the covariate admission rule in design §7 and advances #290. It does **not** close the
broader qualification work, which is about getting these datasets assessed rather than about
removing one preselection.
