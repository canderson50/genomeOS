## Body

Population per H3 cell, for both jobs §9 needs it for: the per-capita denominator, and the multiplier that turns an allele frequency into expected people. Unblocks #94, which needs population-weighted country rollups for parity scoring.

## Two decisions that carry the correctness

**Aggregation is a sum over pixels, not a sample at cell centres.** Sampling would be far faster and badly wrong: population is concentrated in a tiny fraction of pixels, so a centre sample either lands on a city and overstates a 1,700 km² cell by orders of magnitude, or misses it and reports empty land where millions live.

**Nodata is not zero, and that distinction is load-bearing.** §9 refuses to emit a burden number where no denominator exists — which is only possible if *"nobody lives here"* is distinguishable from *"we have no coverage here"*. So a cell with valid pixels summing to zero is emitted as `0.0`, while a cell with no valid pixels is absent entirely and the burden engine refuses it downstream.

I got this wrong on the first pass — the implementation skipped zero-valued pixels, which collapsed both cases into "absent" while the docstring claimed otherwise. Establishing coverage exactly would mean an H3 lookup for every land pixel globally (hundreds of millions), so populated pixels are mapped exactly and empty-but-valid pixels are sampled every `coverage_stride`-th pixel — ample for a yes/no question about a cell spanning ~1,700 pixels at res 4. The stride is recorded on the result rather than hidden.

## Fetching

`scripts/fetch_worldpop.py` resolves the download through WorldPop's REST catalogue rather than hardcoding a path. I first guessed the URL from their directory convention and got a 404 — the layout has changed before, and a hardcoded path fails silently in the sense that it fails for the wrong reason. The global 1 km mosaic is ~870 MB, fetched and never committed.

## A spec claim that doesn't hold — #96

§9 states *"no global gridded birth raster exists"*. WorldPop's catalogue lists a `births` product with per-country rasters; what doesn't exist is a global **mosaic**.

The distinction matters because multiplying cell population by a *national* crude birth rate assumes fertility is spatially uniform within a country. It isn't — urban/rural fertility gaps are largest in exactly the high-fertility, high-HbS countries where burden estimates matter most. Same shape of problem as #89.

#96 also flags an interaction with #45 worth settling first: Piel et al. used national CBRs, so for *parity scoring specifically*, matching their method may beat improving on it. A better denominator could move us away from their numbers for a good reason and read as failure.
