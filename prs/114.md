## The defect

Cross-validation compared two different quantities and called the result calibration:

```python
observed = ac / an
inside_95 = (observed >= predicted["q025"]) & (observed <= predicted["q975"])
```

`predicted` comes from `SurfaceFit.predict`, whose quantiles describe `freq_pred` — the **latent frequency on the reference design**, deliberately carrying no cohort offset (§7.1a: the map shows what a well-ascertained survey *would* have measured). `observed` is an empirical frequency from a finite survey, which additionally carries:

1. binomial / beta-binomial **sampling noise** — the median MAP survey is 173 people, so this is not small,
2. that survey's own **cohort offset**, from `Normal(0, cohort_sd)` across 344 cohorts,
3. its **design effect**.

An interval missing three variance components that are present in the data under-covers **however good the model is**. The first completed validation run (#109) reported 0.10 coverage for a 95% interval, and that number was largely an artifact of this comparison rather than evidence of an overconfident model.

## The fix

`SurfaceFit.predict_observation(lat, lon, an)` returns the posterior predictive for a **new survey of that size**, composing the three layers the latent interval omits:

- latent field →
- a cohort offset **drawn fresh** from `Normal(0, cohort_sd)` →
- overdispersed sampling of `an` alleles.

Two details that matter:

- **The cohort offset is drawn, not looked up.** A held-out survey belongs to a cohort the model never saw, so integrating over the cohort distribution is the honest out-of-sample treatment. Reusing a fitted `cohort_z` would leak information across the split — the same leakage that makes random CV folds optimistic, so importing it into the calibration statistic would be self-defeating.
- **A beta-binomial draw is exactly `p ~ Beta(α, β)` then `Binomial(n, p)`**, so the whole predictive composes in numpy from posterior draws without rebuilding or re-sampling the PyMC model. Draw alignment between the posterior and the posterior predictive is checked explicitly and raises rather than broadcasting silently.

Cross-validation now reports **both** quantities, because they answer different questions:

| statistic | question | status |
|---|---|---|
| `coverage_*_predictive` | would a new survey here land where we expect? | **the calibration claim** |
| `coverage_*_latent` | how well do we know the underlying frequency? | what the map claims; not a calibration statistic |

The gap between them is a direct measure of what the map's interval leaves out.

## Verified

On synthetic fixtures, 3 spatial folds:

```
predictive coverage 95% : 0.842   (target 0.95)
latent     coverage 95% : 0.544
predictive coverage 50% : 0.414   (target 0.50)
latent     coverage 50% : 0.251
```

- `pytest` — **193 passed**
- `ruff check .`, `check_module_size.py`, `check_private_files.py`, `freeze_contract.py --check`, `smoke.py` — all clean
- New test `test_predictive_coverage_exceeds_latent_coverage_on_the_same_data` asserts predictive ≥ latent as an **invariant**, so the predictive path cannot silently stop adding the variance it exists to add.

## What this does NOT establish

**This PR does not re-run the real validation.** The numbers above are from small synthetic fixtures. The MAP HbS figures in #109 are still scored the old way and remain uninterpretable as calibration until a re-run happens on real data — which also wants #111 fixed first, since 4 of 10 folds did not converge and the surviving folds are plausibly the easier ones.

Note also that 0.842 is still short of 0.95 on synthetic data. That may be residual under-dispersion, or an artifact of the deliberately-starved fast test config (`n_inducing=25`, 300 draws, and a spacing-ratio warning fires). **It is not yet evidence either way**, and should not be read as the model now being well calibrated.

## Design sections

§7 / §7.1a (the reference-design prediction path and why it carries no cohort offset), §12 (a non-converged fit is refused, not published — unchanged here).

## For an expert reviewer

Whether integrating the cohort effect over its population distribution is the right out-of-sample treatment for a held-out survey, or whether surveys from a cohort *seen in training* should instead condition on that cohort's fitted offset. The current code always marginalises, which is correct for genuinely new cohorts and mildly conservative for repeat ones.
