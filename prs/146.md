## Body

Closes #130. Design sections: §9 (refusal is a valid output), §12 (exclusions are published).

Spatial skill is a property of the **variant**, not of the pipeline. On spatially-blocked folds:

| variant | held-out MAE | constant baseline | skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| DRB1*15:01 | 0.0446 | 0.0591 | **+24.5%** |
| HbS | 0.0384 | 0.0394 | +2.5% |
| DRB1*04:04 | 0.0279 | 0.0278 | −0.4% |
| DRB1*12:01 | 0.0335 | 0.0320 | **−4.7%** |

The pipeline published all four identically. A map that loses to one global number is not a
finding; §9 already treats a refusal as a valid output, and this applies that one level up.

## The three questions #130 left open

**1. What margin?** None — `MIN_SKILL_MARGIN = 0.0`. That spread offers no natural cut point, and
any line between 2.5% and 24.5% would be a preference dressed as a criterion.

The noise guard is **consistency** instead: `MIN_SKILL_FOLD_SHARE = 0.6` asks the surface to beat
the baseline in *most* folds, however small the margin. A variant that wins on average because one
fold went well has not shown spatial skill; one that wins in four folds of five has, at +2.5%.

**2. CV cost?** Off by default. `skill_folds=0` runs the batch exactly as before. A k-fold refit
per variant is five times the batch across 767 alleles, so switching it on is a decision the caller
makes with the compute in front of them.

**3. Batch or publish?** Batch — it owns exclusions, and `artifacts.publish` should not be doing
science. `Exclusion` gains a `skill` field, so the published list records **how far short** a
variant fell rather than only that it did. That is what keeps the threshold recalibratable from
the record instead of re-argued.

## Folds are spatially blocked

Because that is the question: does the surface predict where there is no nearby survey? Grouped or
random folds leave a neighbour in training and measure interpolation, which #109 measured as
inflating apparent skill roughly fourfold (37% of it attributable to autocorrelation alone).

## A footgun found by running it on real data

`measure_spatial_skill` shrinks `n_inducing` to suit the **training fold**, not the full
observation set. A config sized for the whole variant breaches `fit_surface`'s M≪N guard once a
fold is taken, and the entire gate raises rather than scoring. That killed a real batch run
outright before it was fixed; the caller supplies one config per variant and only this function
knows the fold size.

## Also in this PR

`save_fit`, `load_fit` and `FIT_FORMAT` move to `genomeos/surfaces/persistence.py`. `fit.py`
reached the 800-line agent-readable budget, and AGENTS.md calls for a split rather than a trim when
adding a feature at that threshold. The seam is real — fitting is science, serialisation is
plumbing carrying caveats (environment coupling, arbitrary code execution on load) that have
nothing to do with the model. `fit.py` re-exports all three, so the nine existing call sites are
untouched.

`FitConfig.nugget` ships **off by default**. It is the instrument #137 used to reject the
missing-nugget hypothesis, kept so that experiment stays reproducible rather than deleted along
with the hypothesis.

## Verification

**365 tests pass**, including five new ones: gate off unless asked for, refusal on negative skill,
refusal on high-mean-but-inconsistent skill, passing on small-but-consistent skill, and refusal
when skill is unmeasurable (absence of evidence must not publish a surface). `ruff`,
`freeze_contract --check`, `check_module_size` (51 modules), `check_private_files` and `smoke` all
pass.

A run applying the gate to eight published variants is in flight; I will post the results here.

## What an expert should check

- Whether 0.6 is the right fold share at k=5. It means 3 of 5 folds, which is a bare majority plus
  one; at k=10 it would mean 6 of 10. Neither is a significance test, and it is not claimed to be.
- Whether the gate should also refuse on **absolute** error rather than only relative skill. A
  variant whose baseline MAE is already tiny can beat it consistently while adding nothing useful.

## `hla:b-40-01` is the case the consistency rule exists for

**+15.3% mean skill, but improving in only 40% of folds.** Any magnitude threshold between 2.5% and
24.5% would have published it; the fold-share rule catches it. Its average is carried by folds that
went well, which is precisely "won on average because one region happened to fit" rather than
spatial skill.

Conversely `drb1-15-01` passes at **+2.6%**, a margin most magnitude thresholds would reject, but it
improves in 60% of folds. The gate is asking the right question.

## `hla:c-03-03` is the strongest performer

The same allele whose 3,310 km range drove all of #137, now closed as refuted. It predicts
held-out regions better than any other variant tested. Direct vindication of both decisions: the
long range is not a defect, and the gate is right not to care *why* a range is long.

## `kir:3ds1` at −144% is worth a look

Carrier frequency, not allele frequency, and it fails badly. Either KIR carrier surfaces genuinely
do not extrapolate, or the binomial-over-individuals framing needs its own baseline — a constant
carrier frequency may be a much harder baseline to beat than a constant allele frequency. Not
resolved here; the gate correctly refuses it either way.

## Two footguns this run caught, both invisible to the unit tests

1. `n_inducing` sized for the whole variant breaches `fit_surface`'s M≪N guard once a fold is
   taken, and the gate raises rather than scoring.
2. **Spatial folds are k-means clusters and markedly uneven.** A variant with 208 observations
   produced a **130-row** training fold where `(k−1)/k` implies 166, so the first fix was still
   wrong. The gate now takes the minimum training size directly from `make_folds`.

The unit tests mock `measure_spatial_skill` to exercise the decision logic and never touch real
fold geometry, so neither was reachable from them. Fixed in `216f484`; CI green on that commit.
