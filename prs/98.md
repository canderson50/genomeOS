Adds `scripts/plot_observations.py`, the first committed review figure, and an `AGENTS.md` convention: **once a change affects something renderable, show the map in the PR rather than describing it.**

![MAP HbS observations](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bschilder/genomeOS/feat/observation-figures/docs/figures/hbs_observations.png)

## The figure is also a test of #91

This is the first look at the ingested data, and it validates it: the high-frequency surveys fall across equatorial Africa, the Mediterranean, the Middle East and India, with the diaspora signal in the Americas. **That is the known HbS distribution.** A bug in the allele-count derivation — the wrong denominator, say — would show up here as a pattern that doesn't match the malaria belt, which is a check no unit test in #91 could make.

## Two deliberate choices

**AC=0 surveys get their own hollow marker**, not the bottom of the colour ramp. 79 of 332 surveys measured *absence*, which is real evidence (§7.1b); rendering it as the palest shade of "present" makes the strongest negative evidence in the corpus invisible.

**Observations only** — no fitted surface, no interpolation between points. §4's first invariant is that measured and inferred are never conflated, and that applies to a review figure as much as to the product. A figure that quietly interpolates is exactly the artifact Novembre & Stephens warn about.

## Two legibility bugs I fixed while iterating

Both worth naming, because they're the kind of thing a map lies with:

- **Unequal axes** distorted every distance the eye reads off the plot.
- **A reversed colour ramp** (`inferno_r`) rendered high frequencies dark and low ones bright — inverting the meaning for anyone who doesn't consult the legend. Now `YlOrRd`: pale to saturated reads as low-to-high without the legend, and matches the convention of the prevalence-mapping literature.

The `AGENTS.md` addition encodes both as standing requirements, alongside "always show where there is no data".
