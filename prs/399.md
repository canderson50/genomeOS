## Body


## What this changes, and why

`h3_inducing_points` kept `np.argsort(distance)[:n_inducing]`. When more candidate cells are collocated with observations than the budget allows, their distances are 0 to ~1e-12 km. At that scale:

- the ranking was decided by libm noise and by the unstable default sort;
- so the same `(config, data_version, seed)` could select a different inducing basis, and therefore a different fitted surface, on different platforms (#280);
- that breaks the determinism invariant.

The new rule, all in `genomeos/surfaces/fit.py`:

1. **Distances are compared at 1 mm** (`DISTANCE_TIE_DECIMALS = 6`). That is far below any spacing on §6's ladder, and it is the same rounding #267's review figure uses.
2. **Candidates clearly nearer than the budget's cut are kept outright.** Nearest-first coverage is unchanged wherever it is well determined.
3. **The tier that ties at the cut is settled by greedy maximin spacing** (`_maximin_fill`): each pick is as far as possible from everything already kept.
   - With nothing kept yet, the first pick is the tied candidate nearest the observations' mean direction on the sphere.
   - Only a score still tied at 1 mm after maximin falls to candidate order. That is a total order among places that are geographically indistinguishable, never the selection rule.
   - **There is no H3-index truncation.** On the #280 geometry, index order would keep a block with half the maximin spacing.
4. **The stable sort also fixes the order of the returned points**, which indexes the inducing field. Before, the order among kept ties was itself platform-dependent.

Kept out of scope: the density-promotion sort (`sorted(density, key=-density)`) breaks ties by observation row order. That is deterministic given the inputs and not platform-dependent, so I left it alone.

## TL;DR

Right fix for #280. Nearest-first is untouched wherever it's well determined, and real coordinates come out byte-identical. Two small holes reintroduce platform noise on global, symmetric geometry. Neither is structural, and neither touches a published artifact.

## Finding 1 — antipodal NaN in the maximin distances

`km_to_nearest` (`fit.py:499-505`) calls `genomeos.geo.h3util._haversine_km`, which doesn't clip `a`. The old code only measured candidate-to-observation distances (≤ reach), so it never hit this; maximin is the first place candidate-to-candidate distances are computed. The res-4 grid is antipodally symmetric, so exact antipodes exist: `84090cbffffffff` ↔ `84eaf35ffffffff` gives `nan`. After that, `np.minimum` keeps the NaN and `argmax` picks it — in a direct call, `_maximin_fill` chose a cell scoring 40.5 km over one scoring 8,771 km. NaN-or-not is last-ulp, i.e. platform.

**Suggested:** `np.clip(a, 0, 1)`, as `_haversine_matrix` (`fit.py:146`) and #267's figure already do, or the arctan2 form the anchor uses.

## Finding 2 — the anchor is undefined when the mean direction is ~0

For observations on the 122 res-0 centres, |mean| = 5.7e-17. ±1 ulp of noise in `to_unit_sphere` changed 18–20 of 60 chosen cells (n_inducing=60, reach 500 km), end-to-end through `h3_inducing_points`. The docstring at `:494` says this case lands on index order; that's only true for an exact zero. The noise test patches `_haversine_km` only, so it can't see this.

**Suggested:** fall back to candidate 0 when ‖mean‖ < 1e-9, or just anchor on candidate 0 (farthest-point's 2-approximation holds from any start). Add this geometry with ulp noise on `to_unit_sphere` as a regression test.

## Nits

- Guard `n_inducing < 1`. Today 0 returns 36,933 points; `FitConfig` blocks it, but the function is public.
- The `:461-463` comment: the tied tier returns in maximin order, not cell-id order.

## On your review questions

1 mm and maximin both look right to me. Greedy farthest-point is a 2-approximation to k-centre coverage, and density is already spent on promotion. No model-version bump and no `FIT_FORMAT` change: real-coordinate fits are unchanged. A warning when the tie rule fires would help whoever refits next.
