## What this changes, and why

`h3_inducing_points` kept `np.argsort(distance)[:n_inducing]`. When more candidate cells are collocated with observations than the budget allows, their distances are 0 to ~1e-12 km. At that scale:

- the ranking was decided by libm noise and by the unstable default sort;
- so the same `(config, data_version, seed)` could select a different inducing basis, and therefore a different fitted surface, on different platforms (#280);
- that breaks the determinism invariant.

The new rule, all in `genomeos/surfaces/fit.py`:

1. **Distances are compared at 1 mm** (`DISTANCE_TIE_DECIMALS = 6`). That is far below any spacing on §6's ladder, and it is the same rounding #267's review figure uses.
2. **Candidates clearly nearer than the budget's cut are kept outright.** Nearest-first coverage is unchanged wherever it is well determined.
3. **The tier that ties at the cut is settled by greedy maximin spacing** (`_maximin_fill`): each pick is as far as possible from everything already kept.
   - With nothing kept yet, the first pick is the tied candidate nearest the observations' mean direction on the sphere.
   - Only a score still tied at 1 mm after maximin falls to candidate order. That is a total order among places that are geographically indistinguishable, never the selection rule.
   - **There is no H3-index truncation.** On the #280 geometry, index order would keep a block with half the maximin spacing.
4. **The stable sort also fixes the order of the returned points**, which indexes the inducing field. Before, the order among kept ties was itself platform-dependent.

Kept out of scope: the density-promotion sort (`sorted(density, key=-density)`) breaks ties by observation row order. That is deterministic given the inputs and not platform-dependent, so I left it alone.

## Quantification

The old rule is `main` at `5a10b15`; the new rule is this branch. Both place points only; nothing is fitted.

- `cond(K_uu)` uses Matérn-5/2 on the unit sphere with `JITTER`, at the prior-median lengthscale from `derive_lengthscale_prior`.
- Coverage is the distance from each observation to its nearest inducing point.

**(a) #280 geometry:** 61 resolution-1 centres, `n_inducing=16`, reach 1,500 km.

|  | min NN spacing | median NN spacing | cond(K_uu) | median obs→inducing | max obs→inducing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| old | 638.7 km | 886.7 km | 1.1 | 836.1 km | 1,638.9 km |
| new | 1,561.9 km | 1,696.8 km | 1.0 | 836.1 km | 1,540.9 km |

**(b) Real global geography:** the 1,176 distinct AFND population coordinates in `data/raw/afnd_populations.tsv`, at `FitConfig`'s default `n_inducing=200`.

- **The output is byte-identical: same cells, same order** (spacing 36.4 / 243.6 km, cond 4.3e4 either way).
- Real coordinates do not sit on H3 centres, so nothing ties at the cut and the new rule changes nothing.

**(c) The same sites snapped to resolution-4 centres:** 996 sites, `n_inducing=200`. This is the collocated regime #280 describes, at global scale.

|  | min NN spacing | median NN spacing | cond(K_uu) | median obs→inducing | max obs→inducing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| old | 38.6 km | 135.3 km | 3.03e5 | 289.6 km | 5,519.4 km |
| new | 583.5 km | 716.5 km | 34.3 | 241.1 km | 583.2 km |

Under the old rule, which 200 of the 996 tied cells were kept was float noise. It clustered them, left observations up to 5,500 km from any inducing point, and conditioned `K_uu` ~9,000× worse.

## Model version

- The basis changes only where candidates tie at the budget's cut; (b) shows real global coordinates are unaffected.
- Where it does change, the fit is a different model. Any surface refitted under this code whose geometry hit such a tie must be published under a new `--model-version` (the existing artifacts are at `v3`).
- `artifacts.publish` already refuses to overwrite an existing `(model_version, data_version)` directory, so no cited map can be mutated.
- I did **not** add a placement-rule field to `SurfaceFit`. That would change the persisted fit format (`FIT_FORMAT`), which seemed larger than this issue warrants. Say if you want it.

## wants-expert-review: what to check

1. **Tie tolerance: 1 mm.** It needs to be far above float noise (~1e-12 km) and far below any meaningful distance. Rounding narrows the window for a platform flip to values straddling a 1 mm boundary; it cannot close it.
2. **The maximin coverage rule for the tied tier.** Is maximum spread the right secondary criterion once every candidate is equally near the data? An alternative is weighting by local observation density.
3. **The first-pick anchor,** the tied candidate nearest the observations' mean direction on the sphere.
4. **Model-version handling,** above.

`scripts/plot_prior_normalization.py:_spatially_balanced_sites` implements the same maximin shape for #267's figure. The two could share `_maximin_fill` later. I left the script alone to keep this PR's scope narrow.
