## Body

## Summary

When a surface appears, its hexagons no longer pop in at once. They spread out from the point at the centre of the view in a ring to the horizon, and each cell grows from its own centre and fades in as the ring passes.

Closes #453.

## Behaviour

- **When it runs:** on the cold reveal, "Retry globe" and a dataset switch.
  - On a switch, the old surface goes at the commit and the new one grows from the centre. Two datasets never show under one legend.
  - Metric, palette and elevation changes stay instant, because they recolour the same data.
- **Origin:** where the camera's view ray meets the globe.
- **Timing:** about 1 s to the horizon (`REVEAL_WAVE_MS`, one constant). The ring's on-screen radius grows at an even speed, easing out only over the last fifth, so the spread stays visible the whole time. The far side finishes at once.
- **Reduced motion:** `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` shows no wave, as before.
- **Picking:** cells the ring hasn't reached can't be hovered or picked, because the pick pass shares the vertex shader.
- **Datasets with no cells:** a cold stream that ends with no chunks writes `data-atlas-wave="done"`, so nothing waits forever.
- **Overlays:** per-cell overlays never show ahead of the ring. The cell outlines are built after the wave and appear once it ends.

## How

- **GPU only.** Two uniforms on the surface's own shader carry the wave's origin and its radius and band, animated per frame while renders are requested. The driver stops requesting renders when the wave ends.
- **Nothing else happens per frame:** no geometry rebuilds, no downloads and no per-cell buffer writes. #451 explains why those are dangerous.
- **The timeline is a pure module** (`reveal-wave.ts`) that uses the globe's real perspective. A thin Cesium driver (`reveal-wave-driver.ts`) runs it.
- **Diagnostics:** an `atlas:reveal-wave` User Timing measure and a `data-atlas-wave` attribute. The existing marks keep their meaning.
