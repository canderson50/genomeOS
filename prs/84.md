## The decision, in one line

**PyMC with a Hilbert-space GP (HSGP), not R-INLA-SPDE.** No R toolchain.

§7 names INLA-SPDE, but what it actually *requires* is (a) a binomial-type likelihood over a spatial random field, (b) **proper marginal posteriors**, and (c) a per-variant cost cheap enough to batch. (b) is non-negotiable and load-bearing: `posterior_contraction` in §7.1b is defined as *posterior sd ÷ prior sd*, so an engine that does not produce real posteriors cannot support the data-support mask at all.

HSGP meets all three — its basis functions do not depend on the covariance hyperparameters, so they are precomputed once and inference is **linear rather than cubic** in the number of observations.

**pyINLA was the near-miss.** [pyinla.org](https://pyinla.org/) is a native Python interface to the same C/C++ INLA core — not an `rpy2` wrapper — and on paper is exactly what §7 asks for. But PyPI declares it `License: Proprietary` **with bundled binaries**, at v0.1.8, in an "internal testing phase". A closed binary in the inference step is incompatible with regenerating a cited map at a fixed version (§5, §13), and may collide with whatever licence the repo adopts (#65). Worth revisiting if that clears — full reasoning in #34.

On defensibility: the plan worried this abandons the "same lineage as the geostatistics literature" argument. **§8 already makes our standard of proof empirical rather than genealogical** — reproducing Piel et al.'s intervals is a stronger claim than sharing an implementation.

## Model behaviour worth reviewing

| Behaviour | Why |
|---|---|
| `β_design` is a **contrast against `population_random`** | The well-ascertained anchor. Makes the correction an estimated, auditable parameter rather than a hidden adjustment (§7.1a) |
| **One design present → the term is omitted entirely**, `beta_design_applied=False` | With no contrast it is unidentifiable. Refusing to report a number we cannot estimate, rather than returning one (§9). That flag is the artifact field §6 already specifies |
| `β_cohort` is **hierarchical** | Absorbs residual cohort effects including founder over-sampling (§7.1d) without being free to absorb the spatial signal |
| `predict()` returns the **reference design, no cohort offset** | What a well-ascertained survey would have measured there — not what a depleted panel would have |
| **Beta-binomial selectable** alongside binomial | #83 — the documented failure mode in this application is in the likelihood, not the engine |

## Two deviations from §7 that want a spatial statistician's eye

1. **Matérn-5/2 rather than the 3/2 §7 names** — HSGP's spectral density is better behaved for 5/2. This is a smoothness assumption, not a free choice, so it should be a deliberate amendment to §7 rather than a silent substitution.
2. **`hsgp_m=(8,8)` basis functions** — a placeholder. This is the accuracy/cost dial and it belongs in the HbS calibration (#39), alongside the resolution-promotion thresholds.

Both are called out in the module docstring. Flagging for `wants-expert-review`: **the HSGP-for-SPDE substitution itself is the part where an expert read would most lower risk.**
