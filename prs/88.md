## Body

Closes #41, closes #44, closes #48. Implements design §9, §12.

These three arrive together because they are one thing: #44 has nothing to propagate through without #41's expressions, and #48 is the test suite for the refusals both produce.

## Expressions (#41)

Pure numpy functions, so they broadcast over posterior draws unchanged and unit-test against hand-computed Hardy–Weinberg values with no model in the loop. Recessive, dominant, and X-linked recessive — the last computed by sex from cell-level ratios, since hemizygous males manifest at *p* while females need *p²*.

**Dominant conditions return zero carrier frequency**, not the heterozygote frequency. A heterozygote is a case, not a carrier; emitting a number there invites reading it as a carrier layer.

## Propagation (#44)

By draws, not analytically. §9 is explicit that the transformations are nonlinear and the posterior is not Gaussian on the frequency scale, so a mean and sd pushed through the expressions would understate the interval and misplace the centre.

There is a test asserting the draw-based mean **differs** from plugging the mean frequency into the expression — so the requirement can't silently regress into analytic propagation during a future refactor.

## Refusals (#48)

Each one a test, not a convention:

| Condition | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `unknown` or `prior_dominated` support | refused — and a supported cell in the same frame is still computed |
| penetrance missing | affected metrics refused; **carrier layers still ship** |
| denominator missing | count metrics refused; **frequencies still ship** |

**A refused cell is returned with null values and a stated reason rather than dropped**, so a caller can tell *"we will not say"* from *"this cell does not exist"*. HWE travels per row as `hwe_assumed`, since §9 requires the assumption surfaced rather than buried.

## One spec defect found — #87

§9 writes `affected_freq` as **genotype frequency** for recessive conditions but **penetrance-adjusted** for dominant ones. Final counts are identical either way, so it's invisible until you look at the intermediate — and §6 exposes `affected_freq` as a *selectable map metric*. A user comparing a recessive variant against a dominant one on that layer would be comparing two different quantities with nothing on screen saying so.

Implemented consistently — penetrance applied once, at the affected stage, for every mode. Recessive counts are unchanged from §9; only the intermediate is made uniform. **Which reading is correct is a clinical-genetics question, not mine**, so #87 puts it up for decision rather than settling it here.
